Types of Welding

1. 原文

Gas Welding

Gas welding is accomplished by heating the ends or edges of metal parts to a molten state with a high temperature flame. The oxy-acetylene flame, with a temperature of approximately 6,300 °Fahrenheit (F), is produced with a torch burning acetylene and mixing it with pure oxygen. Hydrogen may be used in place of acetylene for aluminum welding, but the heat output is reduced to about 4,800 °F. Gas welding was the method most commonly used in production on aircraft materials under 3 ⁄ 16-inch in thickness until the mid 1950s, when it was replaced by electric welding for economic (not engineering) reasons. Gas welding continues to be a very popular and proven method for repair operations.

Nearly all gas welding in aircraft fabrication is performed with oxy-acetylene welding equipment consisting of: • Two cylinders, acetylene and oxygen. • Acetylene and oxygen pressure regulators and cylinder pressure gauges. • Two lengths of colored hose (red for acetylene and green for oxygen) with adapter connections for the regulators and torch. • A welding torch with an internal mixing head, various size tips, and hose connections. • Welding goggles fitted with appropriate colored lenses. • A flint or spark lighter. • Special wrench for acetylene tank valve if needed. • An appropriately-rated fire extinguisher.

The equipment may be permanently installed in a shop, but most welding outfits are of the portable type.

Electric Arc Welding

Electric arc welding is used extensively by the aircraft industry in both the manufacture and repair of aircraft. It can be used satisfactorily to join all weldable metals, provided that the proper processes and materials are used. The four types of electric arc welding are addressed in the following paragraphs.

Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW)

Shielded metal arc welding (SMAW) is the most common type of welding and is usually referred to as “stick” welding. The equipment consists of a metal wire rod coated with a welding flux that is clamped in an electrode holder that is connected by a heavy electrical cable to a low voltage and high current in either alternating current (AC) or direct current (DC), depending on the type of welding being done. An arc is struck between the rod and the work and produces heat in excess of 10,000 °F, which melts both the material and the rod. The welding circuit consists of a welding machine, two leads, an electrode holder, an electrode, and the work to be welded.

When the electrode is touched to the metal to be welded, the circuit is complete and the current flows. The electrode is then withdrawn from the metal approximately 1 ⁄ 4-inch to form an air gap between the metal and the electrode. If the correct gap is maintained, the current bridges the gap to form a sustained electric spark called the arc. This action melts the electrode and the coating of flux.

As the flux melts, it releases an inert gas that shields the molten puddle from oxygen in the air to prevent oxidation. The molten flux covers the weld and hardens to an airtight slag that protects the weld bead as it cools. Some aircraft manufacturers, such as Stinson, used this process for the welding of 4130 steel fuselage structures. This was followed by heat treatment in an oven to stress relieve and normalize the structure. Shown in Figure 5-3 is a typical arc welding machine with cables, ground clamp, and electrode holder.

Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW)

Gas metal arc welding (GMAW) was formerly called metal inert gas (MIG) welding. It is an improvement over stick welding because an uncoated wire electrode is fed into and through the torch and an inert gas, such as argon, helium, or carbon dioxide, flows out around the wire to protect the puddle from oxygen. The power supply is connected to the torch and the work, and the arc produces the intense heat needed to melt the work and the electrode.

Low-voltage, high-current DC is typically used with GMAW welding. Figure 5-5 shows the equipment required for a typical MIG welding setup.

This method of welding can be used for large volume manufacturing and production work; it is not well suited to repair work because weld quality cannot be easily determined without destructive testing. Figure 5-6 depicts a typical power source used for MIG welding.

Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW)

Gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW) is a method of electric arc welding that fills most of the needs in aircraft maintenance and repair when proper procedures and materials are used. It is the preferred method to use on stainless steel, magnesium, and most forms of thick aluminum. It is more commonly known as Tungsten Inert Gas (TIG) welding and by the trade names of Heliarc or Heliweld. These names were derived from the inert helium gas that was originally used.

The first two methods of electric arc welding that were addressed used a consumable electrode that produced the filler for the weld. In TIG welding, the electrode is a tungsten rod that forms the path for the high amperage arc between it and the work to melt the metal at over 5,400 °F. The electrode is not consumed and used as filler so a filler rod is manually fed into the molten puddle in almost the same manner as when using an oxy-acetylene torch. A stream of inert gas, such as argon or helium, flows out around the electrode and envelopes the arc thereby preventing the formation of oxides in the molten puddle.

The versatility of a TIG welder is increased by the choice of the power supply being used. DC of either polarity or AC may be used.

• Either select the welder setting to DC straight polarity (the work being the positive and the torch being negative) when welding mild steel, stainless steel, and titanium; or • Select AC for welding aluminum and magnesium.

Electric Resistance Welding

Electric resistance welding, either spot welding or seam welding, is typically used to join thin sheet metal components during the manufacturing process.

Spot Welding

Two copper electrodes are held in the jaws of the spot welding machine, and the material to be welded is clamped between them. Pressure is applied to hold the electrodes tightly together and electrical current flows through the electrodes and the material. The resistance of the material being welded is so much higher than that of the copper electrodes that enough heat is generated to melt the metal. The pressure on the electrodes forces the molten spots in the two pieces of metal to unite, and this pressure is held after the current stops flowing long enough for the metal to solidify. The amount of current, pressure, and dwell time are all carefully controlled and matched to the type of material and the thickness to produce the correct spot welds.

Seam Welding

Rather than having to release the electrodes and move the material to form a series of spot welds, a seam-welding machine is used to manufacture fuel tanks and other components where a continuous weld is needed. Two copper wheels replace the bar-shaped electrodes. The metal to be welded is moved between them, and electric pulses create spots of molten metal that overlap to form the continuous seam.

Plasma Arc Welding (PAW)

Plasma arc welding (PAW) was developed in 1964 as a method of bringing better control to the arc welding process. PAW provides an advanced level of control and accuracy using automated equipment to produce high quality welds in miniature and precision applications. Furthermore, PAW is equally suited to manual operation and can be performed by a person using skills similar to those for GTAW.

In the plasma welding torch, a nonconsumable tungsten electrode is located within a fine-bore copper nozzle. A pilot arc is initiated between the torch electrode and nozzle tip. This arc is then transferred to the metal being welded.

By forcing the plasma gas and arc through a constricted orifice, the torch delivers a high concentration of heat to a small area. The plasma process produces exceptionally high quality welds.

Plasma gas is normally argon. The torch also uses a secondary gas, such as argon/helium or argon/nitrogen, that assists in shielding the molten weld puddle and minimizing oxidation of the weld.

Like GTAW, the PAW process can be used to weld most commercial metals, and it can be used for a wide variety of metal thicknesses. On thin material, from foil to ⁄ 8 -inch, the process is desirable because of the low heat input. The process provides relatively constant heat input because arc length variations are not very critical. On material thicknesses greater than ⁄ 8 -inch and using automated equipment, a keyhole technique is often used to produce full penetration single-path welds. In the keyhole technique, the plasma completely penetrates the work piece. The molten weld metal flows to the rear of the keyhole and solidifies as the torch moves on. The high quality welds produced are characterized by deep, narrow penetration and a small weld face.

When PAW is performed manually, the process requires a high degree of welding skills similar to that required for GTAW. However, the equipment is more complex and requires a high degree of knowledge to set up and use. The equipment required for PAW includes a welding machine, a special plasma arc control system, the plasma welding torch (water-cooled), the source of plasma and shielding gas, and filler material, when required. Because of the cost associated with this equipment, this process is very limited outside of manufacturing facilities.

Plasma Arc Cutting

When a plasma cutting torch is used, the gas is usually compressed air. The plasma cutting machine works by constricting an electrical arc in a nozzle and forcing the ionized gas through it. This heats the gas that melts the metal which is blown away by the air pressure. By increasing air pressure and intensifying the arc with higher voltages, the cutter is capable of blasting through thicker metals and blowing away the dross with minimal cleanup.

Plasma arc systems can cut all electrically conductive metals, including aluminum and stainless steel. These two metals cannot be cut by oxy-fuel cutting systems because they have an oxide layer that prevents oxidation from occurring. Plasma cutting works well on thin metals and can successfully cut brass and copper in excess of two inches thick.

Plasma cutting machines can rapidly and precisely cut through, gouge, or pierce any electrically conductive metal without preheating. The plasma cutter produces a precise kerf (cut) width and a small heat-affected zone (HAZ) that prevents warping and damage.

2. 中文翻译

气焊

气焊是用高温火焰把金属零件的端部或边缘加热到熔化状态来完成的。氧乙炔焰的温度约为6,300 °F（华氏度），由焊炬燃烧乙炔并与纯氧混合而产生。焊接铝时可用氢气代替乙炔，但热输出降到约4,800 °F。20世纪50年代中期以前，气焊是生产中最常用的方法，用于厚度小于3/16英寸的航空材料；此后出于经济原因（而非工程原因）被电焊取代。气焊至今仍是修理作业中非常普及且经过验证的方法。

飞机制造中几乎所有的气焊都用氧乙炔焊接设备完成，该设备包括：• 两个气瓶，乙炔瓶和氧气瓶。• 乙炔和氧气减压器及气瓶压力表。• 两段有色软管（乙炔用红色，氧气用绿色），带连接减压器和焊炬的接头。• 一把带内部混合头、多种规格割嘴和软管接头的焊炬。• 装有适当颜色镜片的焊接护目镜。• 一个燧石打火器或火花点火器。• 必要时用于乙炔瓶阀的专用扳手。• 一个额定规格合适的灭火器。

设备可以固定安装在车间里，但多数焊接装置是可移动式的。

电弧焊

电弧焊在飞机的制造和修理中都为航空工业广泛使用。只要采用正确的工艺和材料，它就能令人满意地连接所有可焊金属。以下各段介绍四种电弧焊。

焊条电弧焊（SMAW）

焊条电弧焊（SMAW）是最常见的一种焊接，通常称为“焊条”焊接。其设备包括一根涂有焊剂的金属焊条，夹在焊钳中；焊钳通过粗电缆接到低压大电流电源上，电源为交流（AC）或直流（DC），视所进行的焊接类型而定。在焊条与工件之间引燃电弧，产生超过10,000 °F的高温，把母材和焊条同时熔化。焊接回路包括焊机、两根电缆、焊钳、焊条和待焊工件。

当焊条接触待焊金属时，回路接通，电流通过。随后把焊条从金属上提起约1/4英寸，在金属与焊条之间形成空气间隙。若间隙保持得当，电流就会跨越间隙，形成持续的电火花，称为电弧。这一作用使焊条和药皮熔化。

焊剂熔化时释放出一种惰性气体，把熔池与空气中的氧隔绝，防止氧化。熔化的焊剂覆盖焊缝，硬化成致密的渣壳，在焊道冷却时起保护作用。一些飞机制造商（如斯廷森公司）曾用该工艺焊接4130钢机身结构，随后在炉中进行热处理，以消除应力并使结构正火。图5-3所示为典型的弧焊机，带电缆、接地夹和焊钳。

熔化极气体保护焊（GMAW）

熔化极气体保护焊（GMAW）以前称为金属惰性气体（MIG）焊。它是对焊条焊接的改进：无药皮的焊丝被送进并穿过焊炬，氩气、氦气或二氧化碳等惰性气体从焊丝周围流出，保护熔池不受氧的侵害。电源接在焊炬和工件上，电弧产生熔化工件和焊丝所需的高温。

GMAW焊接通常采用低压大电流直流电。图5-5所示为典型MIG焊接装置的设备。

这种方法可用于大批量制造和生产作业；它不太适合修理作业，因为不做破坏性试验就难以判定焊缝质量。图5-6所示为MIG焊接所用的典型电源。

钨极气体保护焊（GTAW）

钨极气体保护焊（GTAW）是一种电弧焊方法，在采用正确工艺和材料时，能满足飞机维护与修理的大部分需要。它是焊接不锈钢、镁以及大多数厚铝件的首选方法。它更常被称为钨极惰性气体（TIG）焊，商品名有Heliarc或Heliweld。这些名称源于最初使用的惰性气体氦。

前面介绍的两种电弧焊方法都使用可熔化的电极，由电极本身产生焊缝填充金属。在TIG焊中，电极是一根钨棒，为它与工件之间的高电流电弧提供通路，把金属加热到5,400 °F以上。电极不熔化，也不作填充金属，因此要用手把填充焊丝送入熔池，方式与使用氧乙炔焊炬时几乎相同。氩气或氦气等惰性气体从电极周围流出并包住电弧，从而防止熔池中生成氧化物。

TIG焊机的通用性因所选电源不同而提高。可使用任一极性的直流电或交流电。

• 焊接低碳钢、不锈钢和钛时，把焊机设置选为直流正接（工件接正极、焊炬接负极）；或 • 焊接铝和镁时选择交流。

图5-9所示为TIG焊的典型电源，以及焊炬、脚踏式电流控制器、惰性气体减压器和各种电源电缆。

电阻焊

电阻焊（点焊或缝焊）通常用于在制造过程中连接薄板金属件。

点焊

两个铜电极装在点焊机的钳口中，待焊材料夹在其间。施加压力使电极夹紧，电流通过电极和材料。被焊材料的电阻远高于铜电极，因而产生足够的热量使金属熔化。电极上的压力使两片金属中的熔核结合，电流停止后压力仍保持足够长的时间，让金属凝固。电流、压力和保持时间都要仔细控制，并与材料种类和厚度相匹配，才能焊出正确的点焊点。

缝焊

缝焊机不必像点焊那样松开电极、移动材料来形成一串焊点，而是用于制造油箱及其他需要连续焊缝的部件。它用两个铜滚轮取代棒状电极。待焊金属从两轮之间通过，电脉冲产生的熔核相互重叠，形成连续的焊缝。

等离子弧焊（PAW）

等离子弧焊（PAW）于1964年开发出来，目的是更好地控制电弧焊过程。PAW利用自动化设备提供高水平的控制与精度，可在微型和精密应用中焊出高质量焊缝。此外，PAW同样适合手工操作，可由具备类似GTAW技能的人员施焊。

在等离子焊炬中，不熔化的钨电极位于细孔铜喷嘴内。先在焊炬电极与喷嘴端部之间引燃引导电弧，再把这电弧转移到被焊金属上。

由于迫使等离子气和电弧通过一个收缩的孔口，焊炬把高度集中的热量送到很小的区域。等离子工艺能焊出质量极高的焊缝。

等离子气通常为氩气。焊炬还使用一种辅助气体，如氩/氦或氩/氮，帮助保护熔池并尽量减少焊缝氧化。

与GTAW一样，PAW工艺可焊接大多数商用金属，并适用于很宽的厚度范围。对于薄材料（从箔材到1/8英寸），该工艺因热输入低而理想。由于弧长变化不太关键，该工艺的热输入相对恒定。对于厚度大于1/8英寸的材料，使用自动化设备时常采用锁孔技术，以获得全熔透的单道焊缝。在锁孔技术中，等离子体完全穿透工件。随着焊炬前进，熔融焊缝金属流向锁孔后部并凝固。所焊出的高质量焊缝具有深而窄的熔透和较小的焊缝表面。

手工进行PAW时，该工艺要求很高的焊接技能，与GTAW相当。不过设备更复杂，安装和使用都需要很高水平的知识。PAW所需设备包括焊机、专用等离子弧控制系统、等离子焊炬（水冷式）、等离子气与保护气气源，以及需要时的填充材料。由于设备成本高，该工艺在制造厂之外应用非常有限。

等离子弧切割

使用等离子切割炬时，气体通常为压缩空气。等离子切割机的工作原理是在喷嘴内收缩电弧，并迫使电离气体通过喷嘴。这加热了气体，气体熔化金属，再由空气压力把金属吹走。通过提高空气压力并用更高电压增强电弧，切割机能切穿更厚的金属，并把熔渣吹掉，清理工作量很小。

等离子弧系统能切割所有导电金属，包括铝和不锈钢。这两种金属无法用氧燃气切割系统切割，因为它们表面有氧化层，会阻止氧化反应发生。等离子切割对薄金属效果很好，也能成功切割厚度超过两英寸的黄铜和铜。

等离子切割机无需预热即可快速、精确地切穿、开槽或穿孔任何导电金属。等离子切割机产生的切口（kerf）宽度精确，热影响区（HAZ）小，可防止变形和损伤。

4. 语法解析

① 被动语态 + by 动名词 + with 方式状语

原句：Gas welding is accomplished by heating the ends or edges of metal parts to a molten state with a high temperature flame. 拆解：主句为被动 Gas welding is accomplished；by heating... 为方式状语，heating 的宾语是 the ends or edges of metal parts，to a molten state 为结果状语（heat sth. to ...），with a high temperature flame 为工具状语。 要点：by + 动名词表示“通过做某事”，与 with + 名词表示“用某种工具”分工明确，不能互换。heat ... to a molten state 中 to 表结果，不是方向。被动语态在技术英语里常用来突出工艺本身而非施动者。

② 定语从句 + until + when 非限制性定语从句

原句：Gas welding was the method most commonly used in production on aircraft materials under 3 ⁄ 16-inch in thickness until the mid 1950s, when it was replaced by electric welding for economic (not engineering) reasons. 拆解：主句主语 Gas welding，系动词 was，表语 the method，后接过去分词短语 most commonly used... 作后置定语（相当于 that was most commonly used）；under 3 ⁄ 16-inch in thickness 修饰 materials；until the mid 1950s 为时间状语；逗号后 when 引导非限制性定语从句，先行词是 the mid 1950s。 要点：most commonly used 前省略了关系词和 be 动词，是定语从句的紧缩形式。when 在此不是时间状语从句而是定语从句，先行词是时间名词，可用 when 替换 in which。括号里的 (not engineering) 是对 economic 的当场更正，翻译要保留这种对比语气。

③ 从句套从句的长定语 + depending on 分词短语

原句：The equipment consists of a metal wire rod coated with a welding flux that is clamped in an electrode holder that is connected by a heavy electrical cable to a low voltage and high current in either alternating current (AC) or direct current (DC), depending on the type of welding being done. 拆解：主句 The equipment consists of a metal wire rod；coated with a welding flux 为过去分词短语作后置定语；that is clamped in an electrode holder 是第一层定语从句，其中 electrode holder 又带第二层定语从句 that is connected ... to a low voltage and high current；depending on... 为现在分词短语作伴随状语，逻辑主语是整台设备的使用者。 要点：两层 that 从句层层嵌套，第二个 that 修饰 holder 而非 flux，靠“紧邻先行词”判断。connect A to B 在此为被动 is connected to。depending on 的逻辑主语与主句主语不一致（悬垂分词），技术文献中常见，翻译时补出“视……而定”即可。

④ 时间状语从句 + 定语从句 + 目的不定式

原句：As the flux melts, it releases an inert gas that shields the molten puddle from oxygen in the air to prevent oxidation. 拆解：As 引导时间状语从句（“当……时”）；主句 it releases an inert gas，it 指 the flux；that 引导定语从句修饰 gas，从句谓语 shields，固定搭配 shield A from B；to prevent oxidation 为目的不定式，逻辑主语是 gas。 要点：shield ... from ... 意为“把……与……隔开、保护……免受……”，介词固定用 from。目的不定式 to prevent 修饰的是整个从句动作，不是紧邻的 oxygen。as 在此表时间，与表原因的 since/as 需靠语境区分。

⑤ 分号并列 + because 原因状语从句 + 被动

原句：This method of welding can be used for large volume manufacturing and production work; it is not well suited to repair work because weld quality cannot be easily determined without destructive testing. 拆解：分号前为第一分句，情态动词被动 can be used for；分号后第二分句 it is not well suited to repair work，because 引导原因状语从句，从句为被动 cannot be determined，without destructive testing 为条件状语。 要点：be suited to 中 to 是介词，后接名词或动名词。分号在此表示对比（“可用于……；但不适合……”），比 and 更能体现转折。cannot be easily determined 中的 easily 插在助动词与过去分词之间，是英语副词位置的典型表现。

⑥ by + 动名词作方式状语

原句：By forcing the plasma gas and arc through a constricted orifice, the torch delivers a high concentration of heat to a small area. 拆解：句首 By forcing... through a constricted orifice 为介词短语作方式状语，forcing 的宾语是并列的 the plasma gas and arc，through... 为路径状语；主句主语 the torch，谓语 delivers，宾语 a high concentration of heat，to a small area 为方向状语。 要点：force A through B 意为“迫使 A 通过 B”，介词用 through 而非 across，因为孔口是三维通道。by + 动名词置于句首时，其逻辑主语必须与主句主语一致——这里 forcing 的施动者正是 the torch。
