Advisory Circulars (AC)

AC refers to a type of publication offered by the FAA to provide guidance for compliance with airworthiness regulations. They provide guidance such as methods, procedures, and practices acceptable to the Administrator for complying with regulations. ACs may also contain explanations of regulations, other guidance material, best practices, or information useful to the aviation community. They do not create or change a regulatory requirement. The AC system became effective in 1962. It provides a single, uniform, agency-wide system that the FAA uses to deliver advisory material to FAA customers, industry, the aviation community, and the public.

Unless incorporated into a regulation by reference, the content of ACs are not binding on the public. ACs are issued in a numbered-subject system corresponding to the subject areas of the FARs (14 CFR, Chapter 1, Federal Aviation Administration) and Chapter 3, Commercial Space Transportation, Federal Aviation Administration, Department of Transportation, Parts 400–450. An AC is issued to provide guidance and information in a designated subject area or to show a method acceptable to the Administrator for complying with a related federal aviation regulation.

The AC Numbering System

There are three parts to an AC number, as in 25-42-C.

• The first part of the number identifies the subject matter area of the AC. This corresponds to the part of the FAA’s regulations. In the above example, this would be part 25.

• The second part of the number, beginning with the dash, is a sequential number within each subject area. In the above example, this would be the 42nd AC relating to part 25.

• The third part of the number is a letter assigned by the originating office showing the revision sequence if an AC is revised. The first version of an AC does not have a revision letter. In the above example, this is third revision, as designated by the “C.”

Airworthiness Directives (AD)

In accordance with 14 CFR part 39, the FAA issues ADs in response to deficiencies and/or unsafe conditions found in aircraft, engines, propellers, or other aircraft parts. ADs require that the relevant problem must be corrected on all aircraft or aircraft parts using the same design. ADs are initiated as either proposed, corrective, or final (telegraphic) via the Federal Register. The Federal Register is the official daily publication of the United States Government. It is the printed method of informing the public of laws that are enacted or will be enacted. Electronic versions of ADs are available from the Federal Register and from the Regulatory and Guidance Library. You can search by manufacturer, model, or AD number. All ADs are “incorporated by reference” into part 39 and are considered final. ADs must be followed to remain in compliance with the FAA. Once an AD has been issued, a person/company is authorized to use the affected aircraft or part only if it has been corrected in accordance with the AD.

Types of Airworthiness Directives (AD)

Three types of ADs are issued:

• Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM), followed by a Final Rule

• Final Rule; Request for Comments

• Emergency ADs The standard AD process is to issue an NPRM followed by a Final Rule. After an unsafe condition is discovered, a proposed solution is published as an NPRM and solicits public comment on the proposed action. After the comment period closes, the final rule is prepared, taking into account all substantive comments received with the rule perhaps being changed as warranted by the comments. The preamble to the final rule AD provides response to the substantive comments or states there were no comments received. In certain cases, the critical nature of an unsafe condition may warrant the immediate adoption of a rule without prior notice and solicitation of comments. This is an exception to the standard process. If time for the terminating action to be accomplished is too short to allow for public comment (that is, less than 60 days), then a finding of impracticability is justified for the terminating action, and it can be issued as an immediately adopted rule. The immediately adopted rule is published in the Federal Register with a request for comments. The Final Rule AD may be changed later if substantive comments are received.

An Emergency AD is issued when an unsafe condition exists that requires immediate action by an owner/operator. The intent of an Emergency AD is to rapidly correct an urgent safety deficiency. An Emergency AD may be distributed by fax, letter, or other methods. It is issued and effective to only the people who actually receive it. This is known as “actual notice.” All known owners and operators of affected U.S.-registered aircraft, or those aircraft that are known to have an affected product installed, are sent a copy of an Emergency AD. To make the AD effective to all persons, a follow up publication of the Final Rule AD in the Federal Register is critical. This Final Rule AD must be identical to the Emergency AD and is normally published in the Federal Register within 30 days of the Emergency AD issue.

Generally, ADs include:

• A description of the unsafe condition

• The product that the AD applies to

• The required corrective action or operating limitations or both

• The AD effective date

• Where to go for more information

• Information on alternative methods of compliance with the requirements of the AD

ADs have a three-part number designator. The first part is the calendar year of issuance. The second part is the biweekly period of the year when the number is assigned. The third part is issued sequentially within each biweekly period.

Applicability and Compliance

The AD subject line specifically identifies the TC holder of the aircraft or products affected by the AD. The specific models affected and any special considerations, such as specific installed part numbers or modifications, are listed in the AD applicability section. In order to find all applicable ADs for a specific product, you must search for ADs on the product, aircraft, engine(s), propeller, or any installed appliance. If there are multiple series under the aircraft or engine model, you must also search for ADs applicable to the model, as well as the specific series of that model. The final determination of ADs applicable to a particular product can only be made by a thorough examination of the ADs and the product logbooks. No person may operate a product that an AD applies to, except in accordance with the requirements of the AD. Furthermore, the owner or operator of an aircraft is required by 14 CFR part 91, section 91.403 to maintain the aircraft in compliance with all ADs. The AD specifies a compliance time that relates to the effective date of the AD. That compliance time determines when the actions are required.

Alternative Method of Compliance

Different approaches or techniques that are not specified in an AD can, after FAA approval, be used to correct an unsafe condition on an aircraft or aircraft product. Although the alternative was not known at the time the AD was issued, an alternative method may be acceptable to accomplish the intent of the AD. A compliance time that differs from the requirements of the AD can also be approved if the revised time period and approved alternative method provides an acceptable level of safety as the requirements of the AD.

Special Airworthiness Information Bulletin (SAIB)

A Special Airworthiness Information Bulletin (SAIB) is an information tool that the FAA uses to alert, educate, and make recommendations to the aviation community. SAIBs contain non-regulatory information and guidance that does not meet the criteria for an AD.

Aircraft Specifications

Specifications were originated during implementation of the Air Commerce Act of 1926. Specifications are FAA recordkeeping documents issued for both type-certificated and non-type-certificated products that have been found eligible for U.S. airworthiness certification. Although they are no longer issued, specifications remain in effect and will be further amended. Specifications covering type-certificated products may be converted to a TCDS at the option of the TC holder. However, to do so requires the TC holder to provide an equipment list. A specification is not part of a TC. Specifications are subdivided into five major groups as follows:

  1. Group I—Type Certificate Aircraft, Engines, and Propellers. Covering standard, restricted, and limited types issued for domestic, foreign, and military surplus products.
  1. Group II—Aircraft, Engine, and Propeller Approvals. Covering domestic, foreign, and military surplus products constructed or modified between October 1, 1927, and August 22, 1938. All have met minimum airworthiness requirements without formal type certification. Such products are eligible for standard airworthiness certification as though they are type- certificated products.
  1. Group III—Aircraft, Engine, and Propeller Approvals. Covering domestic products manufactured prior to October 1, 1927, foreign products manufactured prior to June 20, 1931, and certain military surplus engines and propellers. All have met minimum airworthiness requirements of the Air Commerce Act of 1926 and implementing Air Commerce Regulations without formal type certification. Such products are eligible for standard airworthiness certification as though they are type-certificated products.
  1. Group IV—Engine Ratings. Covering unapproved engines rated for maximum power and speed only, their use being limited to specific aircraft with maximum gross weights less than 1,000 pounds. Such engines are not eligible for independent airworthiness certification. These ratings are no longer issued.
  1. Group V—Engine Approvals. Covering military surplus engines meeting CAR 13 design requirements without formal type certification. Such engines are eligible for airworthiness certification as though they are type-certificated engines.

Supplemental Type Certificates (STC)

When an aircraft is designed and that design is formally approved for manufacturing, the manufacturer is issued a Type Certificate (TC). The TC is issued by the FAA to signify the airworthiness of an aircraft design and may not be changed except by formal authorization of the FAA. This formal authorization supplements the original TC and is called the Supplemental Type Certificate (STC). Therefore, the STC issued by the FAA approves a product (aircraft, engine, or propeller) modification. The STC defines the product design change, states how the modification affects the existing type design, and lists serial number effectivity. It also identifies the certification basis listing specific regulatory compliance for the design change. Information contained in the certification basis is helpful for those applicants proposing subsequent product modifications and evaluating certification basis compatibility with other STC modifications. Type Certificate Data Sheets (TCDS)

The TCDS is a formal description of the aircraft, engine, or propeller. It lists limitations and information required for type certification including airspeed limits, weight limits, thrust limitations, and so forth.

TCDSs and specifications set forth essential factors and other conditions that are necessary for U.S. airworthiness certification. Aircraft, engines, and propellers that conform to a U.S. TC are eligible for U.S. airworthiness certification when found to be in a condition for safe operation and ownership requisites are fulfilled. TCDSs were originated and first published in January 1958. Title 14 of the CFR part 21, section 21.41 indicates they are part of the TC. As such, a TCDS is evidence the product has been type certificated. Generally, TCDSs are compiled from details supplied by the TC holder; however, the FAA may request and incorporate additional details when conditions warrant. Under federal law, no civil aircraft registered in the United States can operate without a valid airworthiness certificate. This certificate must be approved and issued by the FAA; and it is only issued if the aircraft and its engines, propellers, and appliances are found to be airworthy and meet the requirements of an FAA-approved TC. The FAA issues a TC when a new aircraft, engine, propeller, and so forth, is found to meet safety standards set forth by the FAA. The TCDS lists the specifications, conditions, and limitations that the airworthiness requirements were met under for the specified product, such as engine make and model, fuel type, engine limits, airspeed limits, maximum weight, minimum crew, and so forth. TCDSs are issued and revised as necessary to accommodate new models or other major changes in the certified product. TCDSs are categorized by TC holder and product type.

咨询通告（AC）

AC 指 FAA 提供的一类出版物，用于就如何符合适航规章提供指导。它们提供的是局长可接受的、用于符合规章的方法、程序和做法等指导。ACs 还可能包含规章解释、其他指导材料、最佳做法，或对航空界有用的信息。它们不创设也不改变规章要求。AC 体系于 1962 年生效。它提供一个单一、统一、覆盖全机构的体系，FAA 用它向 FAA 的服务对象、业界、航空界和公众提供咨询材料。

除非通过引用被纳入某项规章，ACs 的内容对公众不具有约束力。ACs 以编号—主题体系发布，与 FARs（14 CFR 第 1 章联邦航空管理局，以及第 3 章商业航天运输、联邦航空管理局、运输部，Part 400–450）的主题领域相对应。发布 AC 是为在指定主题领域提供指导与信息，或展示局长可接受的、用于符合相关联邦航空规章的方法。

AC 编号体系

AC 编号由三部分组成，例如 25-42-C。

• 编号的第一部分标出该 AC 的主题领域，与 FAA 规章的某一部分相对应。上例中即为 part 25。

适航指令（AD）

依据 14 CFR part 39，FAA 针对在航空器、发动机、螺旋桨或其他航空器零件上发现的缺陷和/或不安全状况发布 ADs。ADs 要求在同类型设计的全部航空器或航空器零件上纠正相关问题。ADs 通过《联邦公报》（Federal Register）以拟定、纠正或最终（电报）形式启动。《联邦公报》是美国政府的官方每日出版物，是向公众告知已颁布或将要颁布的法律的印制方式。ADs 的电子版本可从《联邦公报》和法规与指导文库（Regulatory and Guidance Library）获取，可按制造商、型号或 AD 编号检索。所有 ADs 均以“引用纳入”的方式并入 part 39，并被视为最终规则。必须遵守 ADs 才算符合 FAA 的要求。AD 一经发布，只有当受影响的航空器或零件已按该 AD 完成纠正时，个人/公司才被授权使用它。

适航指令（AD）的类型

发布的 ADs 有三种类型：

• 拟定规则通告（NPRM），随后为最终规则（Final Rule）

• 最终规则；征求意见

• 紧急 ADs 标准的 AD 程序是先发布 NPRM，再发布最终规则。发现不安全状况后，拟定的解决方案以 NPRM 形式公布，并就拟议措施征求公众意见。意见征询期结束后即编制最终规则，其中考虑收到的所有实质性意见，规则也可能按意见的合理要求而作修改。最终规则 AD 的序言对实质性意见作出答复，或说明未收到任何意见。某些情况下，不安全状况的严重性可能要求不经事先通知和征求意见而立即采纳规则，这是对标准程序的例外。若完成终止性措施的时间太短、不足以进行公众评议（即少于 60 天），则可对该终止性措施作出不可行性的认定，并可作为立即采纳的规则发布。立即采纳的规则在《联邦公报》上发布并附征求意见的要求。若收到实质性意见，最终规则 AD 日后可能被修改。

当存在需要机主/运营人立即采取行动的不安全状况时，即发布紧急 AD。紧急 AD 的目的是迅速纠正紧急的安全缺陷。紧急 AD 可通过传真、信函或其他方式分发，它只对实际收到的人发布并生效，这称为“实际通知”。所有已知的受影响美国登记航空器的机主和运营人，或已知装有受影响产品的航空器的机主和运营人，都会收到紧急 AD 的副本。要使 AD 对所有人有效，随后在《联邦公报》上发布最终规则 AD 至关重要。该最终规则 AD 必须与紧急 AD 内容完全相同，通常在紧急 AD 发布后 30 天内在《联邦公报》上公布。

一般而言，ADs 包括：

• 不安全状况的描述

• AD 所适用的产品

• 要求的纠正措施或使用限制，或两者兼有

• AD 的生效日期

• 到何处获取更多信息

• 关于符合 AD 要求的替代方法的说明

ADs 的编号由三部分组成。第一部分是发布的日历年。第二部分是当年分配该编号的双周期序号。第三部分是在每个双周期内按顺序发出的序号。

适用性与符合性

AD 的主题行明确标出受该 AD 影响的航空器或产品的 TC 持有人。受影响的具体型号及任何特殊考虑，例如已安装的特定零件号或改装，列在 AD 的适用性一节。要找出某产品的全部适用 ADs，必须就该产品、航空器、发动机、螺旋桨或任何已安装附件逐一检索。若航空器或发动机型号之下还有多个系列，还必须检索适用于该型号以及该型号具体系列的 ADs。某一产品适用哪些 ADs 的最终判定，只能通过彻底检查 ADs 和产品履历本来作出。任何人不得运行受 AD 适用、却未按该 AD 要求执行的产品。此外，14 CFR part 91 第 91.403 节要求航空器机主或运营人使航空器保持符合所有 ADs。AD 规定与生效日期相关的符合时间，该符合时间决定何时必须完成相应措施。

替代符合方法

未在 AD 中规定的不同做法或技术，经 FAA 批准后可用来纠正航空器或航空产品上的不安全状况。尽管在 AD 发布时并不知道这种替代方法，但它仍可能被接受，只要能达到该 AD 的意图。若修订后的时间期限与获批准的替代方法能提供与 AD 要求同等可接受的安全水平，也可以批准不同于 AD 要求的符合时间。

特别适航信息通告（SAIB）

特别适航信息通告（SAIB）是 FAA 用来提醒、教育航空界并向其提出建议的信息工具。SAIBs 载有不属于规章的信息和指导，其内容不符合作为 AD 的判定标准。

航空器规范（Aircraft Specifications）

航空器规范始创于 1926 年《航空商务法》的实施过程中。规范是 FAA 的存档文件，为已认定有资格取得美国适航审定的型号合格审定产品和非型号合格审定产品而发布。虽然已不再发布，规范仍然有效，并将继续修订。涉及型号合格审定产品的规范，可由 TC 持有人选择转换为 TCDS。但这样做需要 TC 持有人提供设备清单。规范不是 TC 的一部分。规范分为以下五个大组：

  1. 第 I 组——型号合格证航空器、发动机和螺旋桨。涵盖为国内、国外和军余产品颁发的标准类、限制类和有限类。
  1. 第 II 组——航空器、发动机和螺旋桨批准。涵盖 1927 年 10 月 1 日至 1938 年 8 月 22 日期间制造或改装的国内、国外和军余产品。它们都已在未经正式型号合格审定的情况下满足最低适航要求，可按型号合格审定产品对待，取得标准适航证。
  1. 第 III 组——航空器、发动机和螺旋桨批准。涵盖 1927 年 10 月 1 日之前制造的国内产品、1931 年 6 月 20 日之前制造的国外产品，以及某些军余发动机和螺旋桨。它们都已在未经正式型号合格审定的情况下满足 1926 年《航空商务法》及其实施性航空商务规章的最低适航要求，可按型号合格审定产品对待，取得标准适航证。
  1. 第 IV 组——发动机额定值。仅涵盖按最大功率和转速评定的未批准发动机，其使用限于最大全重小于 1,000 磅的特定航空器。此类发动机不能单独取得适航证。这些额定值已不再颁发。
  1. 第 V 组——发动机批准。涵盖未经正式型号合格审定、但满足 CAR 13 设计要求的军余发动机。此类发动机可按型号合格审定发动机对待，取得适航证。

型号合格证数据单（TCDS）

TCDS 是对航空器、发动机或螺旋桨的正式描述。它列出型号合格审定所需的限制和信息，包括空速限制、重量限制、推力限制等。

TCDS 和规范载明了取得美国适航审定所必需的关键要素和其他条件。符合美国 TC 的航空器、发动机和螺旋桨，在经检查处于安全运行状态、且满足所有权要件后，即有资格取得美国适航证。TCDS 始创并首次发布于 1958 年 1 月。CFR 第 14 编 part 21 第 21.41 节表明它们属于 TC 的一部分。因此，TCDS 是产品已取得型号合格审定的证据。TCDS 一般由 TC 持有人提供的细节汇编而成；但在情况需要时，FAA 可要求并纳入额外的细节。按联邦法律，在美国登记的民用航空器，没有有效适航证不得运行。该证必须由 FAA 批准并颁发，且只有在航空器及其发动机、螺旋桨和附件被认定适航、并满足经 FAA 批准的 TC 的要求时才颁发。当新的航空器、发动机、螺旋桨等被认定符合 FAA 规定的安全标准时，FAA 即颁发 TC。TCDS 列出该特定产品满足适航要求所依据的规范、条件和限制，例如发动机厂牌与型号、燃油类型、发动机限制、空速限制、最大重量、最少机组人数等。TCDS 按需要颁发和修订，以适应已审定产品的新型号或其他重大更改。TCDS 按 TC 持有人和产品类型分类。

4. 语法解析

① 状语前置 + 过去分词短语作后置定语

原句：In accordance with 14 CFR part 39, the FAA issues ADs in response to deficiencies and/or unsafe conditions found in aircraft, engines, propellers, or other aircraft parts. 拆解：In accordance with 14 CFR part 39 为介词短语作状语提到句首；主语 the FAA，谓语 issues，宾语 ADs；in response to… 表原因或针对；found in aircraft… 为过去分词短语作后置定语，修饰 deficiencies and/or unsafe conditions。 要点：in accordance with + 规章 = 「依据」，in response to = 「针对……而作出的回应」，两个介词短语不能互换。found 引导的后置定语又长又夹着四项并列宾语，阅读时先抓 found，再回到被修饰的 conditions。

② unless + 过去分词（省略结构）+ be binding on

原句：Unless incorporated into a regulation by reference, the content of ACs are not binding on the public. 拆解：Unless incorporated into a regulation by reference 是省略形式的条件状语（完整形式 Unless it is incorporated…）；主句主语 the content of ACs，谓语 are not binding on the public，binding 为形容词作表语，on 引出约束对象。 要点：unless 相当于 if not，后接过去分词时省略与主句相同的主语及 be 动词，翻译时补成「除非被纳入」。be binding on sb 的介词固定用 on。原文此处用 are 与单数主语 content 不一致，属原书笔误，规范写法应为 is。

③ require that 从句 + 现在分词短语作定语

原句：ADs require that the relevant problem must be corrected on all aircraft or aircraft parts using the same design. 拆解：主语 ADs，谓语 require，that 引导宾语从句；从句为被动结构 must be corrected，on all aircraft or aircraft parts 为状语，using the same design 为现在分词短语作后置定语，修饰 aircraft 与 parts。 要点：require that 之后的从句常以 (should) be done 表达「要求被做」，本句用 must be corrected 强化强制性；require 不能写成 require sb to be done 来表达「要求某事被做」。using 的逻辑主语是 aircraft or parts，不是 ADs。

④ once 引导的时间状语从句 + only if 条件从句

原句：Once an AD has been issued, a person/company is authorized to use the affected aircraft or part only if it has been corrected in accordance with the AD. 拆解：Once 引导时间状语从句，用现在完成被动 has been issued，强调动作完成在先；主句 a person/company is authorized to use…（被动语态 + 不定式作主语补足语）；only if 引导条件从句，用现在完成被动 has been corrected。 要点：once 意为「一旦」，从句用现在完成而不用将来时；authorize sb to do 变为被动后仍保留不定式 to use。only if 提到句首时主句需部分倒装，本句置于句末故用正常语序。

⑤ 三项并列谓语 + how 引导的宾语从句

原句：The STC defines the product design change, states how the modification affects the existing type design, and lists serial number effectivity. 拆解：主语 The STC，三个并列谓语 defines / states / lists；states 后接 how 引导的宾语从句（how the modification affects the existing type design）；lists 的宾语是名词短语 serial number effectivity。 要点：三项并列时前两项用逗号、最后一项前加 and，且三个动词保持同一时态与人称。how 从句在此是「如何影响」的陈述，不是感叹句。effectivity 是航空术语「适用范围」，serial number effectivity 即「适用序列号范围」。

⑥ 不定式目的状语前置 + make 的复合宾语

原句：To make the AD effective to all persons, a follow up publication of the Final Rule AD in the Federal Register is critical. 拆解：To make the AD effective to all persons 为不定式短语作目的状语，提到句首并用逗号隔开；主语 a follow up publication（of the Final Rule AD in the Federal Register 为后置定语），谓语 is critical（系表结构）。 要点：make + 宾语 + 形容词（make the AD effective）是复合宾语结构，effective 作宾语补足语，不能写成 make the AD effectively。不定式作目的状语置于句首时必须有逗号，否则易被误读为句子主语。
