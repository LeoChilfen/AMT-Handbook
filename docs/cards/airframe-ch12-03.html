Types of Hydraulic Fluids

1. 原文

To assure proper system operation and to avoid damage to nonmetallic components of the hydraulic system, the correct fluid must be used. When adding fluid to a system, use the type specified in the aircraft manufacturer’s maintenance manual or on the instruction plate affixed to the reservoir or unit being serviced.

The three principal categories of hydraulic fluids are:

When servicing a hydraulic system, the technician must be certain to use the correct category of replacement fluid. Hydraulic fluids are not necessarily compatible. For example, contamination of the fire-resistant fluid MIL-H-83282 with MIL-H-5606 may render the MIL-H-83282 non-fire-resistant.

Mineral-Based Fluids

Mineral oil-based hydraulic fluid (MIL-H-5606) is the oldest, dating back to the 1940s. It is used in many systems, especially where the fire hazard is comparatively low. MIL-H-6083 is simply a rust-inhibited version of MIL-H-5606. They are completely interchangeable. Suppliers generally ship hydraulic components with MIL-H-6083. Mineral-based hydraulic fluid (MIL–H-5606) is processed from petroleum. It has an odor similar to penetrating oil and is dyed red. Some synthetic hydraulic fluids are dyed purple and even green, depending on the identity of the fluid. Synthetic rubber seals are used with petroleum-based fluids.

Polyalphaolefin-Based Fluids

MIL-H-83282 is a fire-resistant hydrogenated polyalphaolefin-based fluid developed in the 1960s to overcome the flammability characteristics of MIL-H-5606. MIL-H-83282 is significantly more flame resistant than MIL-H-5606, but a disadvantage is the high viscosity at low temperature. It is generally limited to –40 °F. However, it can be used in the same system and with the same seals, gaskets, and hoses as MIL-H-5606. MIL-H-46170 is the rust-inhibited version of MIL-H-83282. Small aircraft predominantly use MIL-H-5606, but some have switched to MIL-H-83282 if they can accommodate the high viscosity at low temperature.

Phosphate Ester-Based Fluid

These fluids are used in most commercial transport category aircraft and are extremely fire-resistant. However, they are not fireproof and under certain conditions, they burn. In addition, these fluids are very susceptible to contamination from water in the atmosphere. The earliest generation of these fluids was developed after World War II as a result of the growing number of aircraft hydraulic brake fires that drew the collective concern of the commercial aviation industry. Progressive development of these fluids occurred as a result of performance requirements of newer aircraft designs. The airframe manufacturers dubbed these new generations of hydraulic fluid, such as Skydrol® and Hyjet®, as types based on their performance.

Today, types IV and V fluids are used. Two distinct classes of type IV fluids exist based on their density: class I fluids are low density and class II fluids are standard density. The class I fluids provide weight savings advantages versus class II. In addition to the type IV fluids that are currently in use, type V fluids are being developed in response to industry demands for a more thermally stable fluid at higher operating temperatures. Type V fluids will be more resistant to hydrolytic and oxidative degradation at high temperature than the type IV fluids.

Intermixing of Fluids

Due to the difference in composition, petroleum-based and phosphate ester-based fluids will not mix; neither are the seals for any one fluid usable with or tolerant of any of the other fluids. Should an aircraft hydraulic system be serviced with the wrong type fluid, immediately drain and flush the system and maintain the seals according to the manufacturer’s specifications.

Compatibility with Aircraft Materials

Aircraft hydraulic systems designed around phosphate ester-based fluids should be virtually trouble-free if properly serviced. Phosphate ester-based fluids do not appreciably affect common aircraft metals—aluminum, silver, zinc, magnesium, cadmium, iron, stainless steel, bronze, chromium, and others—as long as the fluids are kept free of contamination. Thermoplastic resins, including vinyl compositions, nitrocellulose lacquers, oil-based paints, linoleum, and asphalt may be softened chemically due to phosphate ester-based fluids. However, this chemical action usually requires longer than just momentary exposure, and spills that are wiped up with soap and water do not harm most of these materials. Paints that are resistant to phosphate ester-based fluids include epoxies and polyurethanes. Today, polyurethanes are the standard of the aircraft industry because of their ability to keep a bright, shiny finish for long periods of time and for the ease with which they can be removed.

Hydraulic systems require the use of special accessories that are compatible with the hydraulic fluid. Appropriate seals, gaskets, and hoses must be specifically designated for the type of fluid in use. Care must be taken to ensure that the components installed in the system are compatible with the fluid. When gaskets, seals, and hoses are replaced, positive identification should be made to ensure that they are made of the appropriate material. Phosphate ester-based type V fluid is compatible with natural fibers and with a number of synthetics, including nylon and polyester, which are used extensively in most aircraft. Petroleum oil hydraulic system seals of neoprene or Buna-N are not compatible with phosphate ester-based fluids and must be replaced with seals of butyl rubber or ethylene-propylene elastomers.

Hydraulic Fluid Contamination

Experience has shown that trouble in a hydraulic system is inevitable whenever the liquid is allowed to become contaminated. The nature of the trouble, whether a simple malfunction or the complete destruction of a component, depends to some extent on the type of contaminant. Two general contaminants are:

• Abrasives, including such particles as core sand, weld spatter, machining chips, and rust.

• Nonabrasives, including those resulting from oil oxidation and soft particles worn or shredded from seals and other organic components.

Contamination Check

Whenever it is suspected that a hydraulic system has become contaminated or the system has been operated at temperatures in excess of the specified maximum, a check of the system should be made. The filters in most hydraulic systems are designed to remove most foreign particles that are visible to the naked eye. Hydraulic liquid that appears clean to the naked eye may be contaminated to the point that it is unfit for use. Thus, visual inspection of the hydraulic liquid does not determine the total amount of contamination in the system. Large particles of impurities in the hydraulic system are indications that one or more components are being subjected to excessive wear. Isolating the defective component requires a systematic process of elimination. Fluid returned to the reservoir may contain impurities from any part of the system. To determine which component is defective, liquid samples should be taken from the reservoir and various other locations in the system. Samples should be taken in accordance with the applicable manufacturer’s instructions for a particular hydraulic system. Some hydraulic systems are equipped with permanently installed bleed valves for taking liquid samples, whereas on other systems, lines must be disconnected to provide a place to take a sample.

• Routine sampling—each system should be sampled at least once a year, or every 3,000 flight hours, or whenever the airframe manufacturer suggests.

• Unscheduled maintenance—when malfunctions may have a fluid related cause, samples should be taken.

• Suspicion of contamination—if contamination is suspected, fluids should be drained and replaced, with samples taken before and after the maintenance procedure.

• Pressurize and operate hydraulic system for 10 to 15 minutes. During this period, operate various flight controls to activate valves and thoroughly mix hydraulic fluid.

• Shut down and depressurize the system.

• Before taking samples, always be sure to wear the proper personal protective equipment that should include, at the minimum, safety glasses and gloves.

• Wipe off sampling port or tube with a lint-free cloth. Do not use shop towels or paper products that could produce lint. Generally speaking, the human eye can see particles down to about 40 microns in size. Since we are concerned with particles down to 5 microns in size, it is easy to contaminate a sample without ever knowing it.

• Place a waste container under the reservoir drain valve and open valve so that a steady, but not forceful, stream is running.

• Allow approximately 1 pint (250 ml) of fluid to drain. This purges any settled particles from the sampling port.

• Insert a precleaned sample bottle under the fluid stream and fill, leaving an air space at the top. Withdraw the bottle and cap immediately.

• Fill out sample identification label supplied in sample kit, making sure to include customer name, aircraft type, aircraft tail number, hydraulic system sampled, and date sampled. Indicate on the sample label under remarks if this is a routine sample or if it is being taken due to a suspected problem.

• Service system reservoirs to replace the fluid that was removed.

• Submit samples for analysis to laboratory.

Contamination Control

Filters provide adequate control of the contamination problem during all normal hydraulic system operations. Control of the size and amount of contamination entering the system from any other source is the responsibility of the people who service and maintain the equipment. Therefore, precautions should be taken to minimize contamination during maintenance, repair, and service operations. If the system becomes contaminated, the filter element should be removed and cleaned or replaced. As an aid in controlling contamination, the following maintenance and servicing procedures should be followed at all times:

• Maintain all tools and the work area (workbenches and test equipment) in a clean, dirt-free condition.

• A suitable container should always be provided to receive the hydraulic liquid that is spilled during component removal or disassembly procedures.

• Before disconnecting hydraulic lines or fittings, clean the affected area with dry-cleaning solvent.

• All hydraulic lines and fittings should be capped or plugged immediately after disconnecting.

• Before assembly of any hydraulic components, wash all parts in an approved dry-cleaning solvent, i.e., Stoddard solvent.

• After cleaning the parts in the dry-cleaning solution, dry the parts thoroughly and lubricate them with the recommended preservative or hydraulic liquid before assembly. Use only clean, lint-free cloths to wipe or dry the component parts.

• All seals and gaskets should be replaced during the reassembly procedure. Use only those seals and gaskets recommended by the manufacturer.

• All parts should be connected with care to avoid stripping metal slivers from threaded areas. All fittings and lines should be installed and torqued in accordance with applicable technical instructions.

• All hydraulic servicing equipment should be kept clean and in good operating condition.

Contamination, both particulate and chemical, is detrimental to the performance and life of components in the aircraft hydraulic system. Contamination enters the system through normal wear of components, by ingestion through external seals during servicing, or maintenance, when the system is opened to replace/repair components, etc. To control the particulate contamination in the system, filters are installed in the pressure line, in the return line, and in the pump case drain line of each system. The filter rating is given in microns as an indication of the smallest particle size that is filtered out. The replacement interval of these filters is established by the manufacturer and is included in the maintenance manual. In the absence of specific replacement instructions, a recommended service life of the filter elements is:

• Pressure filters—3,000 hours.

• Return Filters—1,500 hours.

• Case drain filters—600 hours.

Hydraulic System Flushing

When inspection of hydraulic filters or hydraulic fluid evaluation indicates that the fluid is contaminated, flushing the system may be necessary. This must be done according to the manufacturer’s instructions; however, a typical procedure for flushing is as follows:

  1. Connect a ground hydraulic test stand to the inlet and outlet test ports of the system. Verify that the ground unit fluid is clean and contains the same fluid as the aircraft.
  1. Change the system filters.
  1. Pump clean, filtered fluid through the system, and operate all subsystems until no obvious signs of contamination are found during inspection of the filters. Dispose of contaminated fluid and filter. Note: A visual inspection of hydraulic filters is not always effective.
  1. Disconnect the test stand and cap the ports.
  1. Ensure that the reservoir is filled to the full line or proper service level.

It is very important to check if the fluid in the hydraulic test stand, or mule, is clean before the flushing operation starts. A contaminated hydraulic test stand can quickly contaminate other aircraft if used for ground maintenance operations.

Health & Handling

Some phosphate ester-based fluids are blended with performance additives. Phosphate esters are good solvents and dissolve away some of the fatty materials of the skin. Repeated or prolonged exposure may cause drying of the skin, which if unattended, could result in complications, such as dermatitis or even secondary infection from bacteria. Phosphate ester-based fluids could cause itching of the skin but have not been known to cause allergic-type skin rashes. Always use the proper gloves and eye protection when handling any type of hydraulic fluid. When phosphate ester-based mist or vapor exposure is possible, a respirator capable of removing organic vapors and mists must be worn. Ingestion of any hydraulic fluid should be avoided. Although small amounts do not appear to be highly hazardous, any significant amount should be tested in accordance with manufacturer’s direction, followed with hospital supervised stomach treatment.

2. 中文翻译

为确保系统正常工作，并避免损坏液压系统的非金属部件，必须使用正确的油液。给系统加注油液时，应使用飞机制造商维护手册中规定的，或贴在油箱、被维护部件上的说明标牌所指定的油液类型。

液压油主要有三大类：

维护液压系统时，技术人员必须确保使用正确类别的补充油液。各种液压油未必相容。例如，用 MIL-H-5606 污染抗燃液 MIL-H-83282，就可能使 MIL-H-83282 失去抗燃性。

矿物基油液

矿物油基液压油（MIL-H-5606）是最老的一种，可追溯到 20 世纪 40 年代。它用于许多系统，尤其是在火灾危险性相对较低之处。MIL-H-6083 只是 MIL-H-5606 的防锈版本，两者完全可以互换。供应商通常用 MIL-H-6083 灌注液压部件后发运。矿物基液压油（MIL–H-5606）由石油加工而成，气味类似渗透油，并被染成红色。有些合成液压油被染成紫色甚至绿色，视油液品种而定。石油基油液配用合成橡胶密封件。

聚α-烯烃基油液

MIL-H-83282 是一种抗燃的加氢聚α-烯烃基油液，于 20 世纪 60 年代研制，用以克服 MIL-H-5606 的可燃性。MIL-H-83282 的抗燃性明显优于 MIL-H-5606，但缺点是低温黏度高，一般限于 –40 °F 以上使用。不过，它可以用于与 MIL-H-5606 相同的系统，配用相同的密封件、垫片和软管。MIL-H-46170 是 MIL-H-83282 的防锈版本。小型飞机主要使用 MIL-H-5606，但有些飞机若能适应低温下的高黏度，已改用 MIL-H-83282。

磷酸酯基油液

这类油液用于大多数商用运输类飞机，并且极其抗燃。然而它们并非防火，在某些条件下仍会燃烧。此外，这类油液极易受大气中水分的污染。最早的一代此类油液是二战之后研制出来的，起因是飞机液压刹车起火事件日益增多，引起了商用航空业的共同关注。随着更新机型的设计提出性能要求，这类油液也逐步发展。机体制造商把这些新一代液压油（如 Skydrol® 和 Hyjet®）按其性能称为若干类型。

目前使用的是 IV 型和 V 型油液。IV 型油液按密度分为两个不同等级：I 级为低密度，II 级为标准密度。I 级油液比 II 级具有减重优势。除目前正在使用的 IV 型油液外，V 型油液也正在研制，以回应对在更高工作温度下热稳定性更好的油液的需求。V 型油液在高温下的抗水解降解和抗氧化降解能力将优于 IV 型油液。

油液的互混

由于成分不同，石油基油液与磷酸酯基油液不能混合；任一种油液所用的密封件，也不能用于或耐受其他任何油液。若飞机液压系统被加注了错误类型的油液，应立即排空并冲洗系统，并按制造商规范维护密封件。

与飞机材料的相容性

以磷酸酯基油液为基础设计的飞机液压系统，只要维护得当，几乎不会出问题。只要油液不受污染，磷酸酯基油液就不会明显影响飞机常用金属——铝、银、锌、镁、镉、铁、不锈钢、青铜、铬等。热塑性树脂，包括乙烯基合成物、硝化纤维素漆、油基漆、油毡和沥青，可能被磷酸酯基油液化学软化。不过，这种化学作用通常需要比短暂接触更长的时间，用肥皂水擦净的泼溅不会损害大多数这类材料。耐磷酸酯基油液的漆包括环氧漆和聚氨酯漆。如今，聚氨酯是航空工业的标准用漆，因为它能长期保持光亮饰面，而且易于去除。

液压系统需要使用与液压油相容的专用附件。密封件、垫片和软管必须专门指定用于所用油液类型。必须注意确保装入系统的部件与油液相容。更换垫片、密封件和软管时，应做确切的识别，确保其由合适的材料制成。磷酸酯基 V 型油液与天然纤维以及多种合成材料相容，包括大多数飞机上广泛使用的尼龙和聚酯。石油基液压系统所用的氯丁橡胶（neoprene）或丁腈橡胶（Buna-N）密封件，与磷酸酯基油液不相容，必须更换为丁基橡胶或乙烯-丙烯弹性体密封件。

液压油污染

经验表明，只要让液体受到污染，液压系统出故障就不可避免。故障的性质——无论是简单的功能失常还是部件的彻底损坏——在一定程度上取决于污染物的类型。污染物一般有两类：

• 磨料，包括芯砂、焊渣、机加工切屑和锈蚀等颗粒。

• 非磨料，包括油液氧化产生的物质，以及密封件和其他有机部件磨损或剥落下来的软颗粒。

污染检查

只要怀疑液压系统受到污染，或系统曾在超过规定最高温度的条件下工作过，就应对系统进行检查。大多数液压系统的过滤器设计用于滤除肉眼可见的大部分外来颗粒。肉眼看来清洁的液压液，可能已污染到不适合使用的程度。因此，目视检查液压液并不能确定系统中污染的总量。液压系统中有大颗粒杂质，说明一个或多个部件正承受过度磨损。要隔离出有缺陷的部件，需要有系统地逐一排除。回到油箱的油液可能带来自系统任何部位的杂质。为确定哪个部件有缺陷，应从油箱以及系统其他各个部位取样。取样应按特定液压系统适用的制造商说明进行。有些液压系统装有永久安装的取样放泄阀，而另一些系统则必须拆开管路才有取样处。

• 例行取样——每个系统至少每年取样一次，或每飞行 3,000 小时取样一次，或按机体制造商的建议取样。

• 非计划维修——当故障可能由油液原因引起时，应取样。

• 怀疑受到污染——如怀疑污染，应排空并更换油液，并在维护工序前后各取样一次。

• 给液压系统增压并运行 10 至 15 分钟。在此期间操作各种飞行操纵装置，使活门动作，把液压油充分混合。

• 关断系统并卸压。

• 取样前务必佩戴合适的个人防护装备，至少应包括护目镜和手套。

• 用无绒布擦净取样口或取样管。不要使用可能产生绒毛的车间抹布或纸制品。一般来说，人眼能看到小至约 40 微米的颗粒。由于我们关注的是小至 5 微米的颗粒，很容易在浑然不觉的情况下污染样品。

• 在油箱排放阀下方放置废液容器，打开阀门，使液流稳定而不猛烈。

• 放出约 1 品脱（250 毫升）油液，以冲净取样口处沉积的颗粒。

• 将预先清洗过的样品瓶伸入液流中接取，装满时在瓶口留出空气空间。取出瓶子并立即盖好。

• 填写取样工具包中提供的样品标识标签，务必包括客户名称、飞机型号、飞机尾号、所取样的液压系统和取样日期。在标签的备注栏注明该样品是例行取样，还是因怀疑有问题而取样。

• 对系统油箱进行补充，补回放出的油液。

• 将样品送实验室分析。

污染控制

在所有正常的液压系统运行中，过滤器为污染问题提供了充分的控制。对从其他任何来源进入系统的污染物的尺寸和数量加以控制，则要由维护和保养设备的人员负责。因此，在维护、修理和维护性服务作业中应采取预防措施，尽量减少污染。如果系统受到污染，应取出滤芯清洗或更换。为有助于控制污染，应始终遵守下列维护和保养程序：

• 保持所有工具和工作区域（工作台和测试设备）清洁无污。

• 应始终备有合适的容器，承接拆卸部件或分解作业中溢出的液压液。

• 断开液压管路或接头之前，用干洗溶剂清洁相关部位。

• 所有液压管路和接头在断开后应立即加盖或堵头。

• 组装任何液压部件之前，用经批准的干洗溶剂（即斯托达德溶剂）清洗所有零件。

• 零件在干洗溶液中清洗后，应彻底干燥，并在组装前用推荐的防腐剂或液压液润滑。只能用清洁的无绒布擦拭或擦干零件。

• 重新装配过程中应更换所有密封件和垫片。只使用制造商推荐的密封件和垫片。

• 连接所有零件时应小心，避免从螺纹部位削下金属碎屑。所有接头和管路应按适用技术说明安装，并按规定力矩拧紧。

• 所有液压维护设备应保持清洁并处于良好工作状态。

颗粒污染和化学污染都会损害飞机液压系统部件的性能和寿命。污染物通过部件正常磨损、维护时经外部密封件侵入，或维修时打开系统更换、修理部件等途径进入系统。为控制系统中的颗粒污染，每个系统都在压力管路、回油管路和泵壳体回油管路中装有过滤器。过滤器的额定值以微米给出，表示能被滤除的最小颗粒尺寸。这些过滤器的更换周期由制造商确定，并列入维护手册。在没有具体更换说明时，滤芯的建议使用寿命为：

• 压力过滤器——3,000 小时。

• 回油过滤器——1,500 小时。

• 壳体回油过滤器——600 小时。

液压系统冲洗

当液压过滤器的检查或液压油的评定表明油液已受污染时，可能需要冲洗系统。冲洗必须按制造商说明进行；不过，典型的冲洗程序如下：

  1. 把地面液压试验台连接到系统的进口和出口测试接口。确认地面设备的油液清洁，且与飞机所用油液相同。
  1. 更换系统过滤器。
  1. 将清洁、经过滤的油液泵送通过系统，并操作所有子系统，直到检查过滤器时未发现明显的污染迹象。废弃受污染的油液和过滤器。注：对液压过滤器做目视检查并不总是有效。
  1. 断开试验台并封盖接口。
  1. 确保油箱加满至满位线或适当的维护油位。

在开始冲洗作业之前，检查液压试验台（俗称“骡子”，mule）中的油液是否清洁非常重要。受污染的液压试验台若用于地面维护作业，会很快污染其他飞机。

健康与操作处置

有些磷酸酯基油液调入了性能添加剂。磷酸酯是良好的溶剂，会溶解掉皮肤上的一些脂肪物质。反复或长时间接触可能使皮肤干燥，若不加以处理，还会引起并发症，如皮炎，甚至细菌引起的继发感染。磷酸酯基油液可能引起皮肤瘙痒，但尚未发现会引起过敏性皮疹。接触任何类型的液压油时，都应始终使用合适的手套和眼睛防护。可能存在磷酸酯基油雾或蒸气暴露时，必须佩戴能滤除有机蒸气和油雾的呼吸器。应避免吞入任何液压油。虽然少量吞入似乎危害不大，但任何较大量的吞入都应按制造商指示进行检测，并在医院监护下洗胃处理。

4. 语法解析

① 分词状语 + 祈使句 + 层层后置定语

原句：When adding fluid to a system, use the type specified in the aircraft manufacturer’s maintenance manual or on the instruction plate affixed to the reservoir or unit being serviced. 拆解：When adding fluid to a system 为“连词 + 现在分词”的时间状语（= When you add ...）；主句是祈使句 use the type；specified in ... or on ... 为过去分词短语后置修饰 the type；affixed to the reservoir or unit 为过去分词短语修饰 the instruction plate；being serviced 为现在分词被动式修饰 the unit。 要点：状语从句省略主语时，主句必须是祈使句或主语与从句一致，这里主句是祈使句，adding 的逻辑主语就是“你”。三个分词定语一层套一层（type—plate—unit），翻译要按中文从后往前的顺序拆开。being serviced 是“正在被维护的”，进行体被动不能省成 serviced。

② render + 宾语 + 形容词补足语

原句：For example, contamination of the fire-resistant fluid MIL-H-83282 with MIL-H-5606 may render the MIL-H-83282 non-fire-resistant. 拆解：For example 为插入的举例状语；主语 contamination of A with B（用 B 污染 A），谓语 may render，宾语 the MIL-H-83282，补足语 non-fire-resistant 为形容词，说明宾语变成的状态。 要点：render 意为“使……变得”，接复合宾语时补足语用形容词或过去分词，不接 to be。contamination of A with B 的介词搭配是“用 B 污染 A”，容易误读成“A 对 B 的污染”。

③ 过去分词后置定语 + 不定式目的状语

原句：MIL-H-83282 is a fire-resistant hydrogenated polyalphaolefin-based fluid developed in the 1960s to overcome the flammability characteristics of MIL-H-5606. 拆解：主句为主系表结构，表语 a fire-resistant hydrogenated polyalphaolefin-based fluid 由三个前置修饰语叠加而成；developed in the 1960s 为过去分词短语后置定语（= which was developed）；to overcome ... 为不定式作目的状语，修饰 developed。 要点：前置修饰语顺序是“抗燃 + 加氢 + 聚α-烯烃基”，中文要按“抗燃的加氢聚α-烯烃基油液”排。to overcome 的逻辑主语是研制者，不是 MIL-H-83282 本身，翻译时补出“研制以克服……”才顺。

④ 破折号同位语 + as long as 条件从句

原句：Phosphate ester-based fluids do not appreciably affect common aircraft metals—aluminum, silver, zinc, magnesium, cadmium, iron, stainless steel, bronze, chromium, and others—as long as the fluids are kept free of contamination. 拆解：主句 Phosphate ester-based fluids do not appreciably affect common aircraft metals；两个破折号之间是一串金属名称，作 metals 的同位语，逐项列举；as long as 引导条件状语从句，从句为被动结构 are kept free of contamination，free of 为形容词短语作主语补足语。 要点：破折号内的同位语把谓语和条件从句隔开，读时先跳过列举、抓住“不影响金属，只要不污染”这条主干。keep sth. free of ... 变被动后 free of 成了主语补足语，不能理解成定语。

⑤ because of 并列 + 介词 which 定语从句

原句：Today, polyurethanes are the standard of the aircraft industry because of their ability to keep a bright, shiny finish for long periods of time and for the ease with which they can be removed. 拆解：主句 polyurethanes are the standard of the aircraft industry；because of 后并列两个宾语——their ability to keep ... 和 the ease with which they can be removed；with which 引导定语从句修饰 the ease，they 指聚氨酯漆。 要点：because of 的两个宾语由 and 连接，第二个宾语前的 for 不能省，否则会被误读成 ability 的并列成分。the ease with which ... 是“做某事的容易程度”，介词 with 因 ease 的搭配而前置。

⑥ 虚拟条件倒装 Should ...

原句：Should an aircraft hydraulic system be serviced with the wrong type fluid, immediately drain and flush the system and maintain the seals according to the manufacturer’s specifications. 拆解：Should an aircraft hydraulic system be serviced ... 为省略 if 的虚拟条件从句，把助动词 Should 提到句首（= If an aircraft hydraulic system should be serviced ...）；主句是祈使句，两个并列动词 drain and flush 与 maintain，宾语分别为 the system 和 the seals。 要点：Should 置于句首且主语在后，是书面语的条件倒装，不能理解成疑问句；主句用祈使句表示“就应立即……”。service 在这里作动词，意为“维护、加注”，与名词 service 含义相关但词性不同。
