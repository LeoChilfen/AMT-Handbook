1. 原文

Composition of the Atmosphere

The mixture of gases that make up the earth’s atmosphere is commonly called air. It is composed principally of 78 percent nitrogen and 21 percent oxygen. The remaining 1 percent is made up of various gases in smaller quantities. Some of these are important to human life, such as carbon dioxide, water vapor, and ozone. Figure 16-1 indicates the respective percentage of the quantity of each gas in its relation to the total mixture.

As altitude increases, the total quantity of all the atmospheric gases reduces rapidly. However, the relative proportions of nitrogen and oxygen remain unchanged up to about 50 miles above the surface of the earth. The percentage of carbon dioxide is also fairly stable. The amounts of water vapor and ozone vary.

Nitrogen is an inert gas that is not used directly by man for life processes; however, many compounds containing nitrogen are essential to all living matter.

The small quantity of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere is utilized by plants during photosynthesis. Thus, the food supply for all animals, including man, depends on it. Carbon dioxide also helps control breathing in man and other animals.

The amount of water vapor in the atmosphere is variable but, even under humid conditions at sea level, it rarely exceeds 5 percent. Water also occurs in the atmosphere as ice crystals. All forms of water in the atmosphere absorb far more energy from the sun than do the other gases. Water plays an important role in the formation of weather.

Ozone is a form of oxygen. It contains three oxygen atoms per molecule, rather than the usual two. Most of the atmosphere’s ozone is formed by the interaction of oxygen and the sun’s rays near the top of the stratosphere in an area called the ozone layer. This is important to living organisms because ozone filters out most of the sun’s harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation. Ozone is also produced by electrical discharges, such as lightning strikes. It has a faint odor, somewhat like that of weak chlorine, that may be detected after a thunderstorm. Auroras and cosmic rays may also produce ozone. Ozone is of great consequence to living creatures on earth and to the circulation of the upper atmosphere.

Human Respiration & Circulation

Oxygen & Hypoxia

The second most prevalent substance in the atmosphere, oxygen, is essential for most living processes. Without oxygen, humans and animals die very rapidly. A reduction in the normal oxygen supply alters the human condition. It causes important changes in body functions, thought processes, and the maintainable degree of consciousness. The resultant sluggish condition of mind and body produced by insufficient oxygen is called hypoxia.

There are several scenarios that can result in hypoxia. During aircraft operations, it is brought about by a decrease in the pressure of oxygen in the lungs at high altitudes. The air contains the typical 21 percent of oxygen, but the rate at which oxygen can be absorbed into the blood depends upon the oxygen pressure. Greater pressure pushes the oxygen from the lung alveoli into the bloodstream. As the pressure is reduced, less oxygen is forced into and absorbed by the blood.

At sea level, oxygen pressure in the lungs is approximately three pounds per square inch (psi). This is sufficient to saturate the blood with oxygen and permit the mind and body to function normally. As altitude is increased, this pressure decreases. Below 7,000 feet above sea level, the available oxygen quantity and pressure remain sufficient for saturation of the blood with oxygen. Above 7,000 feet, however, the oxygen pressure becomes increasingly insufficient to saturate the blood. At 10,000 feet mean sea level (MSL), saturation of the blood with oxygen is only about 90 percent of normal. Long durations at this altitude can result in headache and fatigue, both symptoms of hypoxia. At 15,000 feet MSL, oxygen transfer to the bloodstream drops to 81 percent of saturation. This typically results in sleepiness, headache, blue lips and fingernails, and increased pulse and respiration. Worse yet, vision and judgment become impaired and safe operation of an aircraft becomes compromised. Higher in the atmosphere, decreasing pressure causes even less oxygen to enter the bloodstream; only 68 percent saturation at 22,000 feet MSL. Remaining at 25,000 feet MSL for 5 minutes, where oxygen transfer to the blood is reduced to approximately 50 percent saturation, causes unconsciousness.

Hyperventilation

Another physiological phenomenon of interest to aviators is hyperventilation. Its symptoms greatly resemble hypoxia. When various cells in the body use oxygen and food delivered to them by the blood, carbon dioxide is a by-product. Blood carries this carbon dioxide to the lungs where it is exhaled.

Carbon dioxide functions in the body to regulate the depth and frequency of breathing. A high level of carbon dioxide in the blood triggers rapid, deep breathing to expel it. This also promotes the intake of a greater amount of oxygen for active cells to use. A low carbon dioxide level causes more relaxed breathing resulting in less oxygen intake. Therefore, an oxygen/carbon dioxide balance exists in the blood.

Occasionally, fear, panic, or pain triggers excessive rapid breathing in a person. With it comes a reduction of carbon dioxide in the blood, even though the body does not need this. The lower carbon dioxide level signals the body that there is enough oxygen available and blood vessels constrict, causing hypoxia-like symptoms because insufficient oxygen is being delivered to the cells. Note that the onset of hypoxia described in the previous section occurs without the rapid breathing that accompanies hyperventilation. Hyperventilation can often be alleviated by having the person calm down and breathe normally, which restores the oxygen/carbon dioxide balance in the bloodstream.

Carbon Monoxide Poisoning

Carbon monoxide is a colorless, odorless gas produced by incomplete combustion of hydrocarbon fuels, such as those used in aviation. The human body does not require this gas to function. Its presence, however, can prevent a sufficient level of oxygen to be maintained in the body, resulting in hypoxia. This is also known as carbon monoxide poisoning. As with all forms of oxygen deprivation, extended exposure to carbon monoxide can result in unconsciousness and even death.

Hemoglobin is the substance in the blood that attaches to oxygen in the lungs and circulates it to cells in the body for use. Carbon monoxide more readily attaches itself to hemoglobin than oxygen. If carbon monoxide is present in the lungs, hemoglobin attaches to it and not oxygen. This results in cells not receiving the amount of oxygen they need. The insufficient oxygen level results in hypoxia-like symptoms.

A real danger of carbon monoxide poisoning is that long exposure to slight traces of carbon monoxide can result in oxygen deprivation just as easily as short-term exposure to a concentrated amount. The onset of its effects can be very subtle.

Carbon monoxide detectors are used in flight decks and cabins to provide the crew a warning of the silent danger. There are many types of carbon monoxide detectors available to alert aviators of the presence of this gas. Some are made to be permanently installed in the instrument panel, while others are portable. The simplest carbon monoxide detectors are chemical tabs mounted on cardboard that hang on or adhere to something in the flight deck. When carbon monoxide is present, the tab changes color due to a chemical reaction. More sophisticated detectors provide a digital output in parts per million of carbon monoxide present or illuminate a light and/or an audible alarm sounds. If contaminated, a carbon monoxide portable test unit can be returned to service by installing a new indicating element.

Aircraft that utilize exhaust shroud-type heating systems or combustion heaters are more likely to have carbon monoxide introduced into the cabin from these devices. It is very important to discover the source of carbon monoxide if it is detected. Various leak checks and testing for cracks are performed regularly whenever a combustion source is also the source for cabin heat.

大气组成

构成地球大气的气体混合物通常称为空气。它主要由78%的氮和21%的氧组成。其余1%由含量更少的不同气体构成，其中一些对人体生命很重要，例如二氧化碳、水蒸气和臭氧。图16-1以占总混合物的比例，给出了各种气体各自的百分比。

随着高度增加，所有大气气体的总量迅速减少。但在距地表约50英里以下，氮与氧的相对比例保持不变。二氧化碳的百分比也相当稳定，而水蒸气和臭氧的含量则有所变化。

氮是一种惰性气体，不直接被人用于生命过程；但许多含氮化合物对所有生物都是必不可少的。

大气中少量的二氧化碳在光合作用中被植物利用。因此，包括人在内所有动物的食物供应都依赖它。二氧化碳还有助于调节人和其他动物的呼吸。

大气中水蒸气的含量是可变的，但即使在海平面潮湿条件下，也很少超过5%。水在大气中也以冰晶形式存在。大气中各种形态的水从太阳吸收的能量，远远多于其他气体。水在天气形成中起着重要作用。

臭氧是氧的一种形态，每个分子含三个氧原子，而不是通常的两个。大气中大部分臭氧，是在平流层顶部附近一个称为臭氧层的区域，由氧与太阳射线相互作用形成的。这对生物很重要，因为臭氧滤掉了太阳大部分有害的紫外线（UV）辐射。臭氧也可由放电产生，例如雷击。它有一种淡淡的气味，有点像弱氯气，雷雨过后可能闻到。极光和宇宙射线也可能产生臭氧。臭氧对地球上的生物以及上层大气的环流都极为重要。

人的呼吸与循环

氧与缺氧

大气中含量第二多的物质——氧，是大多数生命过程所必需的。没有氧，人和动物会很快死亡。正常供氧减少会改变人的状态，引起身体机能、思维过程和可维持的意识程度发生重要变化。供氧不足所导致的身心迟钝状态称为缺氧（hypoxia）。

有几种情况可导致缺氧。在航空器运行中，缺氧是由高空肺内氧压下降引起的。空气中仍含通常的21%的氧，但氧能被血液吸收的速率取决于氧压。压力越大，越能把氧从肺泡推入血流；压力降低时，被压入并被血液吸收的氧就减少。

在海平面，肺内氧压约为3磅/平方英寸（psi）。这足以使血液氧饱和，使身心正常运作。随着高度增加，这一压力下降。在海平面以上7,000英尺以下，可用的氧量和氧压仍足以使血液氧饱和。但在7,000英尺以上，氧压越来越不足以使血液饱和。在平均海平面（MSL）10,000英尺处，血液氧饱和仅为正常值的约90%。在此高度长时间停留可导致头痛和疲劳，二者都是缺氧症状。在15,000英尺MSL，向血流的氧输送降至81%饱和度。这通常导致困倦、头痛、嘴唇和指甲发紫、脉搏和呼吸加快。更糟的是，视力和判断力受损，飞机的安全操作受到威胁。更高处，压力下降使进入血流的氧更少；在22,000英尺MSL饱和度仅68%。在25,000英尺MSL停留5分钟（此时向血液的氧输送降至约50%饱和度）会导致失去意识。

过度换气

飞行员关心的另一种生理现象是过度换气（hyperventilation）。其症状与缺氧非常相似。当体内各种细胞利用血液送来的氧和养分时，二氧化碳是副产物；血液把这些二氧化碳带到肺部呼出。

二氧化碳在体内起调节呼吸深度和频率的作用。血液中二氧化碳水平高会触发快速而深长的呼吸以把它排出，同时也促使摄入更多氧，供活跃的细胞使用。二氧化碳水平低则使呼吸变得平缓，导致氧摄入减少。因此，血液中存在氧与二氧化碳的平衡。

有时恐惧、惊慌或疼痛会引发人呼吸过速，随之而来的是血液中二氧化碳减少，尽管身体并不需要这样。较低的二氧化碳水平向身体发出"可用氧已足够"的信号，血管因而收缩，导致类似缺氧的症状，因为输送到细胞的氧不足。注意，上一节所述的缺氧发生时不伴有过度换气那样的快速呼吸。过度换气通常可通过让当事人镇静下来、恢复正常呼吸而缓解，这能恢复血流中的氧／二氧化碳平衡。

一氧化碳中毒

一氧化碳是一种无色无味的气体，由碳氢燃料（如航空所用燃料）不完全燃烧产生。人体并不需要这种气体，但它的存在会妨碍体内维持足够的氧水平，导致缺氧。这也称为一氧化碳中毒。与所有形式的缺氧一样，长时间接触一氧化碳可导致失去意识甚至死亡。

血红蛋白是血液中附着肺内氧、并将其循环到体内细胞供使用的物质。一氧化碳比氧更容易附着到血红蛋白上。若肺内存在一氧化碳，血红蛋白就会附着它而不附着氧，这导致细胞得不到所需的氧量。氧不足会产生类似缺氧的症状。

一氧化碳中毒的真正危险在于：长时间接触微量一氧化碳，与短时间接触高浓度一样容易造成缺氧，其影响的发生可能非常隐蔽。

驾驶舱和客舱内使用一氧化碳探测器，向机组警告这一无声的危险。现有多种类型的一氧化碳探测器可提醒飞行员该气体的存在：有些永久安装在仪表板上，有些则是便携式。最简单的一氧化碳探测器是装在硬纸板上的化学试片，挂在或粘在驾驶舱内某处；当存在一氧化碳时，试片因化学反应而变色。更复杂的探测器以百万分率（ppm）给出数字输出，或点亮指示灯和／或发出声响报警。便携式一氧化碳测试装置若被污染，可通过更换新的指示元件恢复使用。

采用排气罩式加温系统或燃烧式加热器的飞机，更容易由这些装置把一氧化碳带入客舱。一旦检测到一氧化碳，查明其来源非常重要。凡燃烧源同时又是客舱热源时，都要定期进行各种泄漏检查和裂纹检查。

4. 语法解析

① 比较级 far more ... than 引起的倒装

原句：All forms of water in the atmosphere absorb far more energy from the sun than do the other gases. 拆解：主语 All forms of water + 介词短语 in the atmosphere 作后置定语；谓语 absorb，宾语 far more energy，状语 from the sun；than 引导的比较从句中，主语 the other gases 后置，助动词 do 提前，形成 than do the other gases 的倒装。 要点：than 从句若主语较长或为了强调，常把 do / does / did 提到主语前，本质仍是 the other gases do (absorb)。这里 far 修饰比较级 more，表示程度差别很大，不能译成"更多一点"。

② 过去分词短语作后置定语 + 被动谓语

原句：The resultant sluggish condition of mind and body produced by insufficient oxygen is called hypoxia. 拆解：主语中心词 condition，of mind and body 说明范围，过去分词短语 produced by insufficient oxygen 作后置定语修饰 condition；谓语为被动语态 is called，补语 hypoxia 是术语的定义。 要点：produced 的逻辑主语是 condition（状态由缺氧"造成"），所以用过去分词；若误当成句子谓语，就会把 is called 读丢。本句是典型的"术语 + 被动定义"句式，翻译常处理成"……称为……"。

③ 表语从句 + as ... as 同级比较

原句：A real danger of carbon monoxide poisoning is that long exposure to slight traces of carbon monoxide can result in oxygen deprivation just as easily as short-term exposure to a concentrated amount. 拆解：主语 A real danger of carbon monoxide poisoning；系动词 is 后接 that 引导的表语从句。从句主语 long exposure to slight traces of carbon monoxide，谓语 can result in，宾语 oxygen deprivation；just as easily as 引出同级比较对象 short-term exposure to a concentrated amount。 要点：that 从句在 is 之后作表语，说明"危险"的具体内容，不是定语从句。just 加强 as ... as 的语气，意为"同样轻易"；比较的两端是"长时间接触微量"与"短时间接触高浓度"，读时不要漏掉任一端。

④ 时间状语从句 + 过去分词短语作定语

原句：When various cells in the body use oxygen and food delivered to them by the blood, carbon dioxide is a by-product. 拆解：When 引导时间状语从句，从句主语 various cells in the body，谓语 use，宾语为并列的 oxygen and food；过去分词短语 delivered to them by the blood 作后置定语修饰 food（them 指 cells）。主句 carbon dioxide is a by-product 为主系表结构。 要点：delivered 只修饰紧邻的 food，不修饰 oxygen——氧由血液"运送"的是结合在血红蛋白上的氧，而养分才是"送达"细胞的。分词短语紧跟名词时，先判断它修饰哪个词再翻译。

⑤ 双宾语动词 + 分词短语作结果状语

原句：The lower carbon dioxide level signals the body that there is enough oxygen available and blood vessels constrict, causing hypoxia-like symptoms because insufficient oxygen is being delivered to the cells. 拆解：主语 The lower carbon dioxide level，谓语 signals（双宾语动词），间接宾语 the body，直接宾语为 that 引导的宾语从句 there is enough oxygen available；and 连接并列分句 blood vessels constrict；逗号后 causing ... 为现在分词短语作结果状语，其中又套 because 引导的原因状语从句（用现在进行时被动 is being delivered）。 要点：signals the body that ... 是 signal sb. that ... 的搭配，that 从句是直接宾语而非同位语。causing 的逻辑主语是前面整件事（血管收缩），不是 blood vessels 单独一个词；分词结果状语常译成"从而……"。
