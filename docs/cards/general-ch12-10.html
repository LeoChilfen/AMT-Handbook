Across the terminals of the typical aircraft battery, voltage can be measured as the potential difference of 12 volts or 24 volts. That is to say that between the two terminal posts of the battery, there is an emf of 12 or 24 volts available to push current through a circuit. Relatively free electrons in the negative terminal move toward the excessive number of positive charges in the positive terminal. Recall from the discussion on static electricity that like charges repel each other but opposite charges attract each other. The net result is a flow or current through a conductor. There cannot be a flow in a conductor unless there is an applied voltage from a battery, generator, or ground power unit. The illustration shows the level of water in tank A to be at a higher level, reading 10 psi (higher potential energy) than the water level in tank B, reading 2 psi (lower potential energy). Between the two tanks, there is 8-psi potential difference. If the valve in the interconnecting line between the tanks is opened, water flows from tank A into tank B until the level of water (potential energy) of both tanks is equalized. It is important to note that it was not the pressure in tank A that caused the water to flow; rather, it was the difference in pressure between tank A and tank B that caused the flow.

This comparison illustrates the principle that electrons move, when a path is available, from a point of excess electrons (higher potential energy) to a point deficient in electrons (lower potential energy). The force that causes this movement is the potential difference in electrical energy between the two points. This force is called the electrical pressure or the potential difference or the electromotive force (electron moving force).

Current Electrons in motion make up an electric current. This electric current is usually referred to as “current” or “current flow,” no matter how many electrons are moving. Current is a measurement of a rate at which a charge flows through some region of space or a conductor. The moving charges are the free electrons found in conductors, such as copper, silver, aluminum, and gold. The term “free electron” describes a condition in some atoms where the outer electrons are loosely bound to their parent atom. These loosely bound electrons can be easily motivated to move in a given direction when an external source, such as a battery, is applied to the circuit. These electrons are attracted to the positive terminal of the battery, while the negative terminal is the source of the electrons. The greater amount of charge moving through the conductor in a given amount of time translates into a current.

Current = ChargeTime or I = Qt Where:

I = current in amperes (A)

Q = charge in coulombs (C)

T = time A B The System International (SI) unit for current is the ampere (A), where 1A = 1 Cs One ampere (A) of current is equivalent to 1 coulomb (C) of charge passing through a conductor in 1 second. One coulomb of charge equals 6.28 billion electrons. The symbol used to indicate current in formulas or on schematics is the capital letter “I.”

When current flow is one direction, it is called direct current (DC). Later in the handbook, the form of current that periodically oscillates back and forth within the circuit is discussed. The present discussion is only concerned with the use of DC.

The velocity of the charge is actually an average velocity and is called drift velocity. To understand the idea of drift velocity, think of a conductor in which the charge carriers are free electrons. These electrons are always in a state of random motion similar to that of gas molecules. When a voltage is applied across the conductor, an emf creates an electric field within the conductor and a current is established. The electrons do not move in a straight direction but undergo repeated collisions with other nearby atoms. These collisions usually knock other free electrons from their atoms, and these electrons move on toward the positive end of the conductor with an average velocity called the drift velocity, which is relatively a slow speed. To understand the nearly instantaneous speed of the effect of the current, it is helpful to visualize a long tube filled with steel balls as shown in of the tube, which represents the conductor, will immediately cause a ball to be emitted at the opposite end of the tube. Thus, electric current can be viewed as instantaneous, even though it is the result of a relatively slow drift of electrons.

电流 运动的电子构成电流。无论有多少电子在运动，这种电流通常就称为“电流”或“电流流动”。电流是对电荷流过空间某一区域或导体的速率的度量。运动的电荷是导体中的自由电子，如铜、银、铝和金中的自由电子。“自由电子”一词描述某些原子中外层电子与母原子结合松散的状态。当把电池等外部电源接入电路时，这些结合松散的电子很容易被驱动沿给定方向运动。这些电子被电池的正端子吸引，而负端子则是电子的来源。在给定时间内通过导体的电荷量越大，电流就越大。

电流 = 电荷量 / 时间，即 I = Q/t。式中：

I = 电流，单位为安培（A）。

Q = 电荷量，单位为库仑（C）。

当电流只朝一个方向流动时，称为直流电（DC）。本手册后文将讨论在电路中周期性往复振荡的电流形式。本节只讨论直流电的应用。

电荷的速度实际上是平均速度，称为漂移速度。要理解漂移速度的概念，可以设想一种电荷载流子为自由电子的导体。这些电子始终处于类似气体分子的随机运动状态。当在导体两端施加电压时，电动势在导体内建立电场，电流随之形成。电子并不沿直线运动，而是与附近其他原子反复碰撞。这些碰撞通常把其他自由电子从原子中撞出，这些电子以称为漂移速度的平均速度向导体正端运动，该速度相对较慢。要理解电流效应的近乎瞬时性，不妨设想一根装满钢球的长管：在管的一端推入一个球（管代表导体），管的另一端就会立即弹出一个球。因此，尽管电流是电子相对缓慢漂移的结果，仍可视为瞬时发生。

4. 语法解析

① 同位语插入 + 情态动词被动语态

原句：The potential difference, or the voltage across any two points in an electrical system, can be determined by: 拆解：主语 The potential difference 后接由 or 引出的同位语 the voltage across any two points in an electrical system；谓语为 can be determined（情态动词 + 被动语态）；句末 by 引出计算所依据的关系式。 要点：or 在这里不是“或者”，而是“亦即”，用来把术语换成更通俗的说法，翻译时可作“即”；情态被动 can be determined 隐去执行者，是技术说明的惯用写法；主语与谓语之间插入长同位语时，要先跳过同位语找到谓语。

② 强调句 + rather 的对比结构

原句：It is important to note that it was not the pressure in tank A that caused the water to flow; rather, it was the difference in pressure between tank A and tank B that caused the flow. 拆解：主句 It is important to note that… 中 it 为形式主语，真正主语是 to note 短语；that 从句内是两个强调句：it was not the pressure in tank A that caused the water to flow 与 it was the difference in pressure…that caused the flow，分号后的 rather 引出被肯定的那一项。 要点：It was X that… 是强调句型，去掉 It was 和 that 后句子成分仍完整；否定强调 it was not X that… 与 rather 连用，构成“不是……而是……”；注意强调句中 that 不作成分，不能用 which 以外的关系词随意替换。

③ unless 引导的真实条件状语从句

原句：There cannot be a flow in a conductor unless there is an applied voltage from a battery, generator, or ground power unit. 拆解：主句为 there be 存在句 There cannot be a flow in a conductor；unless 引导条件状语从句，从句同样是 there is 存在句，表语 an applied voltage 后接 from 短语说明电压来源。 要点：unless 相当于 if…not，自身已含否定，从句中不可再加 not；there be 与情态动词连用表示“不可能存在”；并列三个来源时前两项用逗号、最后一项前用 or，表示全部可能。

④ 介词 + which 引导的定语从句

原句：Current is a measurement of a rate at which a charge flows through some region of space or a conductor. 拆解：主语 Current + 系动词 is + 表语 a measurement of a rate；at which 引导定语从句修饰先行词 rate，从句主语 a charge，谓语 flows through，宾语 some region of space or a conductor。 要点：at which 来自固定搭配 at a rate（以某一速率），介词提到 which 之前是正式书面语的标志；此处不能用 that 或 what；翻译时把介词还原到从句中，译成“电荷以该速率流过……”。
