Maintaining the Aircraft

机体 Ch 01 · Maintaining the Aircraft · 原文 PDF 第 62 页

1. 原文

Maintenance of an aircraft is of the utmost importance for safe flight. Certificated technicians are committed to perform timely maintenance functions in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions and under Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations (14 CFR). At no time is an act of aircraft maintenance taken lightly or improvised. The consequences of such action could be fatal, and the technician could lose their certificate and face criminal charges.

Airframe, engine, and aircraft component manufacturers are responsible for documenting the maintenance procedures that guide managers and technicians on when and how to perform maintenance on their products. A small aircraft may only require a few manuals, including the aircraft maintenance manual. This volume usually contains the most frequently used information required to maintain the aircraft properly. The Type Certificate Data Sheet (TCDS) for an aircraft also contains critical information. Complex and large aircraft require several manuals to convey correct maintenance procedures adequately. In addition to the maintenance manual, manufacturers may produce such volumes as structural repair manuals, overhaul manuals, wiring diagram manuals, component manuals, and more.

Note that the use of the word “manual” is meant to include electronic as well as printed information. Also, proper maintenance extends to the use of designated tools and fixtures called out in the manufacturer’s maintenance documents. In the past, not using the proper tooling has caused damage to critical components, which subsequently failed and led to aircraft crashes and the loss of human life. The technician is responsible for using the correct information, procedures, and tools needed to perform appropriate maintenance or repairs.

Standard aircraft maintenance procedures do exist and can be used by the technician when performing maintenance or a repair. These are found in the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) approved advisory circulars (AC) 43.13-2, Acceptable Methods, Techniques, and Practices - Aircraft Alterations and AC 43.13-1, Acceptable Methods, Techniques, and Practices - Aircraft Inspection and Repair . If not addressed by the manufacturer’s literature, the technician may use the procedures outlined in these manuals to complete the work in an acceptable manner. These procedures are not specific to any aircraft or component and typically cover methods used during maintenance of all aircraft. Note that the manufacturer’s instructions supersede the general procedures found in AC 43.13-2 and AC 43.13-1.

All maintenance related actions on an aircraft or component are required to be documented by the performing technician in the aircraft or component logbook. Light aircraft may have only one logbook for all work performed. Some aircraft may have a separate engine logbook for any work performed on the engine(s). Other aircraft have separate propeller logbooks. Large aircraft require volumes of maintenance documentation comprised of thousands of procedures performed by hundreds of technicians. Electronic dispatch and recordkeeping of maintenance performed on large aircraft such as airliners is common. The importance of correct maintenance recordkeeping should not be overlooked.

Location Numbering Systems

Even on small, light aircraft, a method of precisely locating each structural component is required. Various numbering systems are used to facilitate the location of specific wing frames, fuselage bulkheads, or any other structural members on an aircraft. Most manufacturers use some system of station marking. For example, the nose of the aircraft may be designated “zero station,” and all other stations are located at measured distances in inches behind the zero station. Thus, when a blueprint reads “fuselage frame station 137,” that particular frame station can be located 137 inches behind the nose of the aircraft.

To locate structures to the right or left of the center line of an aircraft, a similar method is employed. Many manufacturers consider the center line of the aircraft to be a zero station from which measurements can be taken to the right or left to locate an airframe member. This is often used on the horizontal stabilizer and wings.

The applicable manufacturer’s numbering system and abbreviated designations or symbols should always be reviewed before attempting to locate a structural member. They are not always the same. The following list includes location designations typical of those used by many manufacturers.

• Fuselage stations (Fus. Sta. or FS) are numbered in inches from a reference or zero point known as the reference datum. The reference datum is an imaginary vertical plane at or near the nose of the aircraft from which all fore and aft distances are measured. The distance to a given point is measured in inches parallel to a center line extending through the aircraft from the nose through the center of the tail cone. Some manufacturers may call the fuselage station a body station, abbreviated BS.

• Buttock line or butt line (BL) is a vertical reference plane down the center of the aircraft from which measurements left or right can be made.

• Water line (WL) is the measurement of height in inches perpendicular from a horizontal plane usually located at the ground, cabin floor, or some other easily referenced location.

• Aileron station (AS) is measured outboard from, and parallel to, the inboard edge of the aileron, perpendicular to the rear beam of the wing.

• Flap station (KS) is measured perpendicular to the rear beam of the wing and parallel to, and outboard from, the inboard edge of the flap.

• Nacelle station (NC or Nac. Sta.) is measured either forward of or behind the front spar of the wing and perpendicular to a designated water line.

In addition to the location stations listed above, other measurements are used, especially on large aircraft. Thus, there may be horizontal stabilizer stations (HSS), vertical stabilizer stations (VSS) or powerplant stations (PPS). In every case, the manufacturer’s terminology and station location system should be consulted before locating a point on a particular aircraft.

Another method is used to facilitate the location of aircraft components on air transport aircraft. This involves dividing the aircraft into zones. These large areas or major zones are further divided into sequentially numbered zones and subzones. The digits of the zone number are reserved and indexed to indicate the location and type of system of which the component is a part. Access & Inspection Panels

Quick access to the accessories and other equipment carried in the fuselage is provided for by numerous access doors, inspection plates, landing wheel wells, and other openings.

Servicing diagrams showing the arrangement of equipment and location of access doors are supplied by the manufacturer in the aircraft maintenance manual.

Knowing where a particular structure or component is located on an aircraft needs to be combined with gaining access to that area to perform the required inspections or maintenance. To facilitate this, access and inspection panels are located on most surfaces of the aircraft. Small panels that are hinged or removable allow inspection and servicing. Large panels and doors allow components to be removed and installed, as well as human entry for maintenance purposes.

The underside of a wing, for example, sometimes contains dozens of small panels through which control cable components can be monitored and fittings greased. Various drains and jack points may also be on the underside of the wing. The upper surface of the wings typically have fewer access panels because a smooth surface promotes better laminar airflow, which causes lift. On large aircraft, walkways are sometimes designated on the wing upper surface to permit safe navigation by mechanics and inspectors to critical structures and components located along the wing’s leading and trailing edges. Wheel wells and special component bays are places where numerous components and accessories are grouped together for easy maintenance access.

Panels and doors on aircraft are numbered for positive identification. On large aircraft, panels are usually numbered sequentially containing zone and subzone information in the panel number. Designation for a left or right side location on the aircraft is often indicated in the panel number. This could be with an “L” or “R,” or panels on one side of the aircraft could be odd numbered and the other side even numbered. The manufacturer’s maintenance manual explains the panel numbering system and often has numerous diagrams and tables showing the location of various components and under which panel they may be found. Each manufacturer is entitled to develop its own panel numbering system.

2. 中文翻译

飞机的维修对安全飞行至关重要。持证技术人员承诺按照制造商的说明、并依据《联邦法规》第14编（14 CFR）及时执行维修工作。任何飞机维修行为都绝不可掉以轻心或临时应付。此类行为的后果可能是致命的，技术人员可能因此被吊销执照并面临刑事指控。

机体、发动机及飞机部件制造商有责任编制维修程序文件，用以指导管理人员和技术人员何时以及如何对其产品进行维修。小型飞机可能只需要几本手册，其中包括飞机维修手册（aircraft maintenance manual）。该手册通常收录正确维修飞机最常用的信息。飞机的型号合格证数据表（TCDS）也包含关键信息。复杂的大型飞机需要多本手册才能充分传达正确的维修程序。除维修手册外，制造商还可能编制结构修理手册、翻修手册、线路图手册、部件手册等。

请注意，“手册”一词的使用意在同时涵盖电子版与纸质版信息。此外，正确维修还包括使用制造商维修文件中明确规定的专用工具和夹具。过去，未使用正确工具曾导致关键部件损坏，进而失效并造成飞机坠毁和人员伤亡。技术人员有责任使用正确的信息、程序和工具，以完成适当的维修或修理。

标准的飞机维修程序确实存在，技术人员在实施维修或修理时可以使用。这些程序见于美国联邦航空管理局（FAA）批准的咨询通告（AC）43.13-2《可接受的方法、技术和实践——飞机改装》和 AC 43.13-1《可接受的方法、技术和实践——飞机检查与修理》。如果制造商的技术资料未涉及，技术人员可采用这些手册中概述的程序，以可接受的方式完成工作。这些程序并不针对某一特定飞机或部件，通常涵盖所有飞机维修时所使用的方法。请注意，制造商的说明优先于 AC 43.13-2 和 AC 43.13-1 中的通用程序。

飞机或部件上所有与维修相关的操作，都要求由实施维修的技术人员记录在飞机或部件记录簿（logbook）中。轻型飞机可能只有一本记录簿，记录所有已完成的工作。有些飞机可能另设一本发动机记录簿，记录在发动机上所做的任何工作。其他飞机还有单独的螺旋桨记录簿。大型飞机需要成卷的维修文件，其中包含由数百名技术人员执行的数千项程序。诸如客机这样的大型飞机，其维修工作的电子签派与记录保存已很普遍。正确维修记录的重要性不容忽视。

定位编号系统

即使是小型轻型飞机，也需要一种精确确定每个结构部件位置的方法。人们使用各种编号系统，以便于确定飞机上特定机翼隔框、机身隔框（bulkhead）或任何其他结构构件的位置。大多数制造商采用某种形式的站位标记（station marking）系统。例如，机头可被指定为“零站位”，其余所有站位都位于零站位之后、以英寸为单位测得的距离处。因此，当图纸上标注“机身隔框站位 137”时，该隔框站位即可在机头之后 137 英寸处找到。

要确定飞机中心线左右两侧的结构位置，也采用类似的方法。许多制造商把飞机中心线视为零站位，由此可向右或向左测量，以确定机体构件的位置。这种方法常用于水平安定面和机翼。

在试图确定某一结构构件的位置之前，应始终查阅适用的制造商编号系统以及缩写代号或符号。它们并不总是相同的。下列清单列出了许多制造商常用的一类定位代号。

• 机身站位（Fus. Sta. 或 FS）以英寸为单位，从称为基准面（reference datum）的参考点或零点起编号。基准面是一个假想的垂直平面，位于机头处或机头附近，所有前后距离均从该平面量起。到给定点的距离，以英寸为单位，平行于一条从机头穿过尾锥中心延伸至机尾的中心线测量。有些制造商把机身站位称为机体站位（body station），缩写为 BS。

• 侧向站位线，或称对接线（BL），是沿飞机中心线向下的一个垂直参考平面，可由此向左或向右进行测量。

• 水线（WL）是以英寸为单位的高度测量值，自某一水平面垂直量起，该水平面通常位于地面、客舱地板或其他易于参照的位置。

• 副翼站位（AS）从副翼内侧边缘向外、并平行于该边缘测量，且垂直于机翼后梁。

• 襟翼站位（KS）垂直于机翼后梁测量，并平行于襟翼内侧边缘、自该边缘向外量取。

• 发动机短舱站位（NC 或 Nac. Sta.）在机翼前梁之前或之后测量，并垂直于指定的水线。

除上述定位站位外，还使用其他测量方式，尤其是在大型飞机上。因此，可能会有水平安定面站位（HSS）、垂直安定面站位（VSS）或动力装置站位（PPS）。在任何情况下，在特定飞机上确定某点位置之前，都应查阅制造商的术语和站位定位系统。

在运输类飞机上，还采用另一种方法便于确定飞机部件的位置。这种方法把飞机划分为若干区域（zone）。这些大区域或主区域又进一步划分为按顺序编号的区域和子区域。区域编号的各位数字各有预留含义并被编入索引，用以指示部件所属系统的位置和类型。图 1-92 示出了运输类飞机上的这些区域和子区域。

接近口盖与检查口盖

机身内所载附件和其他设备的快速接近，由众多接近门、检查盖板、起落架轮舱及其他开口提供。

显示设备布置和接近门位置的维护示意图，由制造商提供在飞机维修手册中。

了解飞机上某一特定结构或部件位于何处，必须与进入该区域实施所需检查或维修结合起来。为便于做到这一点，飞机大多数表面上都设有接近口盖和检查口盖。带铰链或可拆卸的小口盖便于检查和维护。大的口盖和舱门则允许拆装部件，也允许人员进入进行维修。

例如，机翼下表面有时设有数十个小的口盖，通过它们可以监测操纵钢索部件并为接头加注润滑脂。机翼下表面还可能设有各种排泄口和顶升点。机翼上表面的接近口盖通常较少，因为光滑的表面能促进层流气流，而层流正是产生升力的原因。在大型飞机上，机翼上表面有时会标出行走通道，以便机械员和检查员安全地到达沿机翼前后缘布置的关键结构和部件。轮舱和专用部件舱是把众多部件和附件集中布置、便于维修接近的地方。

飞机上的口盖和舱门都编号，以便明确识别。在大型飞机上，口盖通常按顺序编号，口盖编号中包含区域和子区域信息。飞机左侧或右侧位置的代号，常常也体现在口盖编号中。这可以用“L”或“R”表示，也可以把飞机一侧的口盖编为奇数、另一侧编为偶数。制造商的维修手册会说明口盖编号系统，并且通常附有大量图表，示出各种部件的位置以及它们位于哪个口盖之下。每个制造商都有权制定自己的口盖编号系统。

4. 语法解析

① 否定状语置句首引起倒装

原句：At no time is an act of aircraft maintenance taken lightly or improvised. 拆解：否定介词短语 At no time 置于句首，引起主谓部分倒装：助动词 is 提到主语 an act of aircraft maintenance 之前，过去分词 taken 与 improvised 并列作主语补足语（被动语态的核心动词）。 要点：At no time、Never、Under no circumstances 等否定成分置于句首时，必须把助动词/be 动词移到主语前，形成“助动词 + 主语 + 过去分词”。此处 taken 与 improvised 共用一个 is，不能理解为两个谓语。

② be responsible for doing + that 定语从句

原句：Airframe, engine, and aircraft component manufacturers are responsible for documenting the maintenance procedures that guide managers and technicians on when and how to perform maintenance on their products. 拆解：主语为并列的三类 manufacturers；谓语 are responsible for，介词后接动名词 documenting；宾语 the maintenance procedures 后接 that 引导的定语从句，从句谓语 guide，宾语 managers and technicians，on when and how to perform... 说明指导的内容。 要点：be responsible for 后接动名词而非不定式。that 从句修饰 procedures，从句中 guide sb. on sth. 是固定搭配；when and how to perform 为“疑问词 + 不定式”结构，作介词 on 的宾语，不要误当成两个从句。

③ 动名词短语作主语 + 非限制性定语从句

原句：In the past, not using the proper tooling has caused damage to critical components, which subsequently failed and led to aircraft crashes and the loss of human life. 拆解：主语是动名词短语 not using the proper tooling，谓语 has caused（现在完成时），宾语 damage to critical components；逗号后 which 引导非限制性定语从句，先行词为 critical components，从句内 failed 与 led to 并列。 要点：动名词短语作主语时谓语用单数 has，不能因为 tooling 之外还有复数名词而误用 have。which 前有逗号，说明是补充信息而非限定；其先行词是紧邻的 components，不是 damage。

④ 介词 + which 引导的定语从句

原句：The reference datum is an imaginary vertical plane at or near the nose of the aircraft from which all fore and aft distances are measured. 拆解：主句为主系表结构，表语 an imaginary vertical plane 后接两个后置修饰：at or near the nose of the aircraft 表位置，from which 引导定语从句修饰 plane；从句为被动语态，主语 all fore and aft distances，谓语 are measured。 要点：from which 中的 which 指代 plane，介词 from 由从句谓语 measure ... from 决定，不能换成 in which。fore and aft 是固定搭配“前后的”，作 distances 的定语，不分开理解。

⑤ 动名词短语作主语 + where 宾语从句

原句：Knowing where a particular structure or component is located on an aircraft needs to be combined with gaining access to that area to perform the required inspections or maintenance. 拆解：主语是动名词短语 Knowing where...，其中 where 引导宾语从句作 Knowing 的宾语；谓语 needs to be combined with，with 后接动名词 gaining access to that area；to perform... 为目的状语，说明进入该区域的目的。 要点：needs 的单复数由动名词主语决定，用单数。be combined with 前后要求形式对称：Knowing ... 对 gaining ...，若后项写成 to gain 就破坏了平行。where 从句在此是宾语从句，不是定语从句。
