1. 原文

Heat is a form of energy. It is produced only by the conversion of one of the other forms of energy. Heat may also be defined as the total kinetic energy of the molecules of any substance.

Some forms of energy which can be converted into heat energy are as follows:

• Mechanical Energy—this includes all methods of producing increased motion of molecules such as friction, impact of bodies, or compression of gases.

• Electrical Energy—electrical energy is converted to heat energy when an electric current flows through any form of resistance such as an electric iron, electric light, or an electric blanket.

• Chemical Energy—most forms of chemical reaction convert stored potential energy into heat. Some examples are the explosive effects of gunpowder, the burning of oil or wood, and the combining of oxygen and grease.

• Radiant Energy—electromagnetic waves of certain frequencies produce heat when they are absorbed by the bodies they strike such as x-rays, light rays, and infrared rays.

• Nuclear Energy—energy stored in the nucleus of atoms is released during the process of nuclear fission in a nuclear reactor or atomic explosion.

• Sun—all heat energy can be directly or indirectly traced to the nuclear reactions occurring in the sun.

When a gas is compressed, work is done and the gas becomes warm or hot. Conversely, when a gas under high pressure is allowed to expand, the expanding gas becomes cool. In the first case, work was converted into energy in the form of heat; in the second case heat energy was expended. Since heat is given off or absorbed, there must be a relationship between heat energy and work. Also, when two surfaces are rubbed together, the friction develops heat. However, work was required to cause the heat, and by experimentation, it has been shown that the work required and the amount of heat produced by friction is proportional. Thus, heat can be regarded as a form of energy.

According to this theory of heat as a form of energy, the molecules, atoms, and electrons in all bodies are in a continual state of motion. In a hot body, these small particles possess relatively large amounts of kinetic energy, but in cooler bodies they have less. Because the small particles are given motion, and hence kinetic energy, work must be done to slide one body over the other. Mechanical energy apparently is transformed, and what we know as heat is really kinetic energy of the small molecular subdivisions of matter.

Heat Energy Units

Two different units are used to express quantities of heat energy. They are the calorie and the BTU. One calorie is equal to the amount of heat required to change the temperature of 1 gram of water 1 degree Centigrade.

This term “calorie” (spelled with a lower case c) is 1/1,000 of the Calorie (spelled with a capital C) used in the measurement of the heat energy in foods. One BTU is defined as the amount of heat required to change the temperature of 1 lb of water 1 degree Fahrenheit (1 °F). The calorie and the gram are seldom used in discussing aviation maintenance. The BTU, however, is commonly referred to in discussions of engine thermal efficiencies and the heat content of aviation fuel.

A device known as the calorimeter is used to measure quantities of heat energy. In application, it may be used to determine the quantity of heat energy available in 1 pound of aviation gasoline. A given weight of the fuel is burned in the calorimeter, and the heat energy is absorbed by a large quantity of water. From the weight of the water and the increase in its temperature, it is possible to compute the heat yield of the fuel. A definite relationship exists between heat and mechanical energy. This relationship has been established and verified by many experiments which show that:

One BTU of heat energy = 778 ft-lb of work As discussed earlier in this chapter under the topic “Potential Energy,” one pound of aviation gasoline contains 18,900 BTU of heat energy. Since each BTU is capable of 778 ft-lb of work, 1 lb of aviation gasoline is capable of 14,704,200 ft-lb of work.

Heat Energy and Thermal Efficiency

Thermal efficiency is the relationship between the potential for power contained in a specific heat source, and how much usable power is created when that heat source is used. The formula for calculating thermal efficiency is:

Thermal Efficiency = Horsepower Produced ÷ Potential Horsepower in Fuel For example, consider the piston engine used in a small general aviation airplane, which typically consumes 0.5 lb of fuel per hour for each horsepower it creates. Imagine that the engine is creating 200 hp. If we multiply 0.5 by the horsepower of 200, we find the engine is consuming 100 lb of fuel per hour, or 1.67 lb per minute. Earlier in this chapter, one horsepower was found to be 33,000 ft-lb of work per minute. The potential horsepower in the fuel burned for this example engine would be:

Hp = 33,000 ft-lb/min 1.67 lb/minute × 18,900 BTU/lb × 778 ft lb/BTU Hp = 744 The example engine is burning enough fuel that it has the potential to create 744 horsepower, but it is only creating 200. The thermal efficiency of the engine would be:

Thermal Efficiency = Hp Produced ÷ Hp in Fuel = 200 ÷ 744 = .2688 or 26.88% More than 70 percent of the energy in the fuel is not being used to create usable horsepower. The wasted energy is in the form of friction and heat. A tremendous amount of heat is given up to the atmosphere and not used inside the engine to create power.

There are three methods by which heat is transferred from one location to another or from one substance to another. These three methods are conduction, convection, and radiation.

Heat transfer always takes place by areas of high heat energy migrating to areas of low heat energy. Heat transfer by conduction requires that there be physical contact between an object that has a large amount of heat energy and one that has a smaller amount of heat energy.

Everyone knows from experience that the metal handle of a heated pan can burn the hand. A plastic or wood handle, however, remains relatively cool even though it is in direct contact with the pan. The metal transmits the heat more easily than the wood because it is a better conductor of heat. Different materials conduct heat at different rates. Some metals are much better conductors of heat than others. Aluminum and copper are used in pots and pans because they conduct heat very rapidly. Woods and plastics are used for handles because they conduct heat very slowly.

Different metals conduct heat at different rates. Of those listed, silver is the best conductor and lead is the poorest. As mentioned previously, copper and aluminum are used in pots and pans because they are good conductors. It is interesting to note that silver, copper, and aluminum are also excellent conductors of electricity.

Liquids are poorer conductors of heat than metals. Notice that the ice in the test tube is not melting rapidly even though the water at the top is boiling. The water conducts heat so poorly that not enough heat reaches the ice to melt it.

Gases are even poorer conductors of heat than liquids. It is possible to stand quite close to a stove without being burned because air is such a poor conductor. Since conduction is a process whereby the increase in molecular energy is passed along by actual contact, gases are poor conductors.

At the point of application of the heat source, the molecules become violently agitated. These molecules strike adjacent molecules causing them to become agitated. This process continues until the heat energy is distributed evenly throughout the substance. The gases are much poorer conductors of heat because molecules are farther apart in gases than in solids.

Convection is the process by which heat is transferred by movement of a heated fluid (gas or liquid). For example, an incandescent light bulb will, when heated, become increasingly hotter until the air surrounding it begins to move. The motion of the air is upward. This upward motion of the heated air carries the heat away from the hot light bulb by convection. Transfer of heat by convection may be hastened by using a ventilating fan to move the air surrounding a hot object. The rate of cooling of a hot electronics component, such as the CPU in a computer, can be increased if it is provided with copper fins that conduct heat away from the hot surface. The fins provide large surfaces against which cool air can be blown.

A convection process may take place in a liquid as well as in a gas. A good example of this is a pan of water sitting on the stove. The bottom of the pan becomes hot because it conducts heat from the surface it is in contact with. The water on the bottom of the pan also heats up because of conduction. As the heated water starts to rise and cooler water moves in to take its place, the convection process begins.

When the circulation of gas or liquid is not rapid enough to remove sufficient heat, fans or pumps are used to accelerate the motion of the cooling material. In some installations, pumps are used to circulate water or oil to help cool large equipment. In airborne installations, electric fans and blowers are used to aid convection.

An aircraft air-cooled piston engine is a good example of convection being used to transfer heat. The engine is a Continental IO-520, with six heavily finned air-cooled cylinders. This engine does not depend on natural convection for cooling, but rather forced air convection coming from the propeller on the engine. The heat generated inside the engine finds its way to the cylinder cooling fins by conduction, meaning transfer within the metal of the cylinder. Once the heat gets to the fins, forced air flowing around the cylinders carries the heat away.

Conduction and convection cannot wholly account for some of the phenomena associated with heat transfer. For example, the heat one feels when sitting in front of an open fire cannot be transferred by convection because the air currents are moving toward the fire. It cannot be transferred through conduction because the conductivity of the air is very small, and the cooler currents of air moving toward the fire would more than overcome the transfer of heat outward. Therefore, there must be some way for heat to travel across space other than by conduction and convection.

The existence of another process of heat transfer is still more evident when the heat from the sun is considered. Since conduction and convection take place only through some medium, such as a gas or a liquid, heat from the sun must reach the earth by another method, since space is an almost perfect vacuum. Radiation is the name given to this third method of heat transfer.

The term “radiation” refers to the continual emission of energy from the surface of all bodies. This energy is known as “radiant energy.” It is in the form of electromagnetic waves, radio waves, or x-rays, which are all alike except for a difference in wave length. These waves travel at the velocity of light and are transmitted through a vacuum more easily than through air because air absorbs some of them. Most forms of energy can be traced back to the energy of sunlight. Sunlight is a form of radiant heat energy that travels through space to reach the earth. These electromagnetic heat waves are absorbed when they come in contact with nontransparent bodies. The result is that the motion of the molecules in the body is increased as indicated by an increase in the temperature of the body.

The differences between conduction, convection, and radiation may now be considered. First, although conduction and convection are extremely slow, radiation takes place at the speed of light. This fact is evident at the time of an eclipse of the sun when the shutting off of the heat from the sun takes place at the same time as the shutting off of the light. Second, radiant heat may pass through a medium without heating it. In application, the air inside a greenhouse may be much warmer than the glass through which the sun’s rays pass. Third, although heat transfer by conduction or convection may travel in roundabout routes, radiant heat always travels in a straight line. For example, radiation can be cut off with a screen placed between the source of heat and the body to be protected.

One important way in which substances differ is in the requirement of different quantities of heat to produce the same temperature change in a given mass of the substance. Each substance requires a quantity of heat, called its specific heat capacity, to increase the temperature of a unit of its mass 1 °C. The specific heat of a substance is the ratio of its specific heat capacity to the specific heat capacity of water. Specific heat is expressed as a number which, because it is a ratio, has no units and applies to both the English and the metric systems.

It is fortunate that water has a high specific heat capacity. The larger bodies of water on the earth keep the air and solid matter on or near the surface of the earth at a constant

. A great quantity of heat is required to change the temperature of a large lake or river. Therefore, when the temperature falls below that of such bodies of water, they give off large quantities of heat. This process keeps the atmospheric temperature at the surface of the earth from changing rapidly.

Different materials have different specific heat values.

Temperature Temperature is a dominant factor affecting the physical properties of fluids. It is of particular concern when calculating changes in the state of gases.

The three temperature scales used extensively are the Centigrade, the Fahrenheit, and the absolute or Kelvin scales. The Centigrade scale is constructed by using the freezing and boiling points of water, under standard conditions, as fixed points of zero and 100, respectively, with 100 equal divisions between. The Fahrenheit scale uses 32° as the freezing point of water and 212° as the boiling point, and has 180 equal divisions between. The absolute or Kelvin scale is constructed with its zero point established as minus 273 °C, meaning 273° below the freezing point of water.

When working with temperatures, always make sure which system of measurement is being used and know how to convert from one to another. The conversion formulas are as follows:

Degrees Fahrenheit = (1.8 × Degrees Celsius) + 32 Degrees Celsius = (Degrees Fahrenheit – 32) × 5⁄9 Degrees Kelvin = Degrees Celsius + 273 Degrees Rankine = Degrees Fahrenheit + 460 For purposes of calculations, the Rankine scale is commonly used to convert Fahrenheit to absolute. For Fahrenheit readings above zero, 460° is added. Thus, 72 °F equals 460° plus 72°, or 532° absolute. If the Fahrenheit reading is below zero, it is subtracted from 460°. Thus −40 °F equals 460° minus 40°, or 420° absolute. Thermal Expansion/Contraction

Thermal expansion takes place in solids, liquids, and gases when they are heated. With few exceptions, solids will expand when heated and contract when cooled. The expansion of solids when heated is very slight in comparison to the expansion in liquids and gases because the molecules of solids are much closer together and are more strongly attracted to each other. The expansion of fluids is discussed in the study of Boyle’s law. Thermal expansion in solids must be explained in some detail because of its close relationship to aircraft metals and materials.

It is necessary to measure experimentally the exact rate of expansion of each one because some substances expand more than others. The amount that a unit length of any substance expands for a one degree rise in temperature is known as the coefficient of linear expansion for that substance. To estimate the expansion of any object, such as a steel rail, it is necessary to know three things about it: its length, the rise in temperature to which it is subjected, and its coefficient of expansion. This relationship is expressed by the equation:

Expansion = (coefficient) × (length) × (rise in temperature)

If a steel rod measures exactly 9 ft at 21 °C, what is its length at 55 °C? The coefficient of expansion for steel is 11 × 10−6.

Expansion = (1 1 × 10−6) × (9 feet) × 34° Expansion = 0.003366 feet This amount, when added to the original length of the rod, makes the rod 9.003366 ft long. Its length has only increased by 4⁄100 of an inch.

The increase in the length of the rod is relatively small, but if the rod were placed where it could not expand freely, there would be a tremendous force exerted due to thermal expansion. Thus, thermal expansion must be taken into consideration when designing airframes, power plants, or related equipment.

2. 中文翻译

热是能量的一种形式。它只能由其他形式的能量之一转换而来。热也可以定义为任何物质全部分子的总动能。

可以转换为热能的能量形式有下列几种：

• 机械能——包括一切使分子运动加剧的方法，如摩擦、物体撞击或气体受压缩。

• 电能——当电流流过任何形式的电阻时，电能就转换为热能，例如电熨斗、电灯或电热毯。

• 化学能——大多数形式的化学反应把储存的势能转化为热。例如火药的爆炸效应、油或木材的燃烧，以及氧气与润滑脂的结合。

• 辐射能——某些频率的电磁波被所照射的物体吸收时产生热，例如 X 射线、光线和红外线。

• 核能——储存在原子核中的能量在核反应堆的核裂变过程或原子爆炸中释放出来。

• 太阳——一切热能都可以直接或间接追溯到太阳中发生的核反应。

气体被压缩时要对其做功，气体随之变暖或变热。反过来，高压气体被允许膨胀时，膨胀的气体变冷。第一种情况下，功被转换成热这种形式的能量；第二种情况下，热能则被消耗。既然热被放出或被吸收，热能与功之间必然存在某种关系。此外，两个表面相互摩擦时，摩擦会产生热。不过，产生热需要做功；而且经实验证明，所需的功与摩擦产生的热量成正比。因此，热可以看作能量的一种形式。

按照这种「热是能量的一种形式」的理论，一切物体中的分子、原子和电子都处于持续运动状态。在热的物体中，这些小粒子具有相对较大的动能，而在较冷的物体中它们具有的动能较少。因为要使这些小粒子运动起来、从而获得动能，就必须做功使一个物体在另一个物体上滑动。机械能显然被转变了，而我们所称的热，实际上就是物质微小分子组成部分的动能。

热能单位

表达热能数量时使用两种不同的单位，即卡和 BTU。一卡等于使 1 克水的温度变化 1 摄氏度所需的热量。

这个「卡」（calorie，小写 c）是测量食物热能时所用「大卡」（Calorie，大写 C）的 1/1,000。一个 BTU 定义为使 1 lb 水的温度变化 1 华氏度（1 °F）所需的热量。在讨论航空维修时很少使用卡和克。然而，在讨论发动机热效率和航空燃油含热量时，BTU 却是常用单位。

一种称为量热计的装置用来测量热能的数量。使用时，可以用它测定 1 磅航空汽油中可用的热能。一定重量的燃油在量热计中燃烧，热能就被大量的水吸收。由水的重量及其温升，就可以算出该燃油的发热量。热与机械能之间存在着确定的关系。这一关系已经由许多实验确立并验证，实验表明：

一 BTU 的热能 = 778 ft-lb 的功 正如本章前面在「势能」这一主题下所讨论的，1 磅航空汽油含有 18,900 BTU 的热能。由于每个 BTU 能做出 778 ft-lb 的功，1 lb 航空汽油能做出 14,704,200 ft-lb 的功。

热能与热效率

热效率是指某一热源中所含的功率潜力与该热源被利用时实际产生多少可用功率之间的关系。计算热效率的公式为：

热效率 = 发出的马力 ÷ 燃油中的潜在马力 例如，考虑一架小型通用航空飞机所用的活塞发动机，它通常每发出 1 马力每小时消耗 0.5 lb 燃油。设想该发动机发出 200 hp。如果把 0.5 乘以马力数 200，就会发现该发动机每小时消耗 100 lb 燃油，即每分钟 1.67 lb。本章前面已经求得，1 马力是每分钟 33,000 ft-lb 的功。这个示例发动机所烧燃油中的潜在马力为：

Hp = 33,000 ft-lb/min　1.67 lb/min × 18,900 BTU/lb × 778 ft-lb/BTU　Hp = 744 这个示例发动机所烧的燃油足以产生 744 马力的潜力，但它只发出 200 马力。该发动机的热效率则为：

热效率 = 发出马力 ÷ 燃油中马力 = 200 ÷ 744 = .2688，即 26.88% 燃油中 70% 以上的能量并未用来产生可用马力。浪费掉的能量以摩擦和热的形式存在。大量热被排到大气中，而没有在发动机内部用于产生功率。

热从一个位置转移到另一位置、或从一种物质转移到另一种物质，有三种方式。这三种方式是传导、对流和辐射。

热传递总是由热能高的区域向热能低的区域迁移。通过传导进行的传热要求一个有大量热能的物体与一个热能较少的物体之间存在物理接触。

从经验中人人都知道，受热锅的金属手柄会烫手。然而，塑料或木制手柄即使直接与锅接触，也仍然相对较凉。金属比木材更容易传热，因为它是更好的导热体。不同材料传导热的速率不同。有些金属的导热能力远优于其他金属。铝和铜被用于锅具，因为它们传热非常快。木材和塑料被用于手柄，因为它们传热非常慢。

不同金属的导热速率不同。在所列的材料中，银是最好的导体，铅最差。如前所述，铜和铝被用于锅具，因为它们是良导体。有趣的是，银、铜和铝同时也是极好的导电体。

液体的导热能力比金属差。请注意，尽管试管里的水正在沸腾，试管中的冰并没有迅速融化。水导热太差，传到冰上的热不足以使它融化。

气体的导热能力比液体更差。可以站得离炉子相当近而不被烫伤，因为空气的导热性很差。既然传导是通过实际接触把分子能量的增加传递出去的过程，气体自然是差的导体。

在热源作用处，分子被剧烈扰动。这些分子撞击相邻分子，使它们也被扰动。这一过程持续下去，直到热能在整个物质中均匀分布。气体导热能力差得多，因为气体中分子之间的距离比固体中大。

对流是依靠受热流体（气体或液体）的流动来传递热的过程。例如，白炽灯泡受热后会越来越热，直到周围的空气开始流动。空气的运动方向向上。受热空气的这种向上运动把热带离炽热的灯泡，这就是对流传热。利用通风风扇使热物体周围的空气流动，可以加快对流传热。电脑 CPU 这类发热电子元件的冷却速率，可以通过给它加装能把热从热表面导走的铜散热片来提高。散热片提供了大面积的表面，可以对着它吹冷空气。

对流过程不仅发生在气体中，也发生在液体中。一个很好的例子是炉子上的一锅水。锅底变热，因为它从所接触的表面传导热量。锅底的水也因传导而升温。当受热的水开始上升、较冷的水过来占据它的位置时，对流过程就开始了。

当气体或液体的循环不够快、不能带走足够的热量时，就用风扇或泵来加快冷却介质的运动。在某些装置中，用泵使水或油循环，帮助冷却大型设备。在机载装置中，则用电风扇和鼓风机来助长对流。

飞机的气冷活塞发动机是对流传热的一个好例子。这台发动机是大陆 IO-520，有六个带密集散热片的气冷气缸。这台发动机不依赖自然对流冷却，而是依靠来自螺旋桨的强迫空气对流。发动机内部产生的热通过传导到达气缸散热片，也就是在气缸金属内部传递。一旦热到达散热片，绕气缸流动的强迫气流就把热带走。

传导和对流并不能完全解释与传热有关的某些现象。例如，坐在敞开的火堆前所感到的热，不可能通过对流传递，因为气流是朝火堆方向流动的。它也不可能通过传导传递，因为空气的导热率很小，而朝火堆流动的较冷气流会远远抵消向外的传热。因此，热必然还有别的途径能够穿越空间。

考虑太阳传来的热，另一种传热过程的存在就更加明显。既然传导和对流只能通过某种介质（如气体或液体）进行，而空间几乎是完美的真空，太阳的热必然通过另一种方式到达地球。辐射就是这第三种传热方式的名称。

「辐射」一词指一切物体表面持续不断地发射能量。这种能量称为「辐射能」。它以电磁波、无线电波或 X 射线的形式存在，这些波除了波长不同之外彼此完全相同。这些波以光速传播，并且穿过真空比穿过空气更容易，因为空气会吸收其中一些波。大多数形式的能量都可以追溯到太阳光的能量。太阳光是辐射热能的一种形式，它穿越太空到达地球。这些电磁热波遇到不透明物体时就被吸收。结果是物体中分子的运动加剧，表现为物体温度升高。

现在可以考虑传导、对流和辐射之间的区别。第一，传导和对流极其缓慢，而辐射以光速进行。这一点在日食时表现得很明显：来自太阳的热被切断与光被切断是同时发生的。第二，辐射热可以穿过某种介质而不使其升温。举例来说，温室内的空气可能比阳光穿过的玻璃热得多。第三，传导或对流传递的热可以绕行迂回，而辐射热总是沿直线传播。例如，在热源与需要保护的物体之间放一块挡板，就可以隔断辐射。

物质彼此不同的一个重要方面，是使一定质量的物质产生同样的温度变化所需的热量不同。每种物质都需要一定的热量（称为其比热容），才能使其单位质量的温度升高 1 °C。物质的比热是它的比热容与水的比热容之比。比热表示为数值，由于它是比值，所以没有单位，英制与公制都适用。

幸亏水的比热容很高。地球上较大的水体使地球表面或接近表面的空气和固体物质保持恒定的

。要使大湖或大河的温度改变，需要大量的热。因此，当气温降到这类水体温度以下时，它们就放出大量的热。这一过程使地表附近的大气温度不致迅速变化。

不同材料具有不同的比热值。

温度 温度是影响流体物理性质的主要因素。在计算气体状态变化时尤其需要关注。

广泛使用的温标有三种：摄氏温标、华氏温标和绝对温标（即开尔文温标）。摄氏温标以标准条件下水的冰点和沸点分别作为零点与 100 点，其间分为 100 个等分。华氏温标以 32° 作为水的冰点、212° 作为沸点，其间分为 180 个等分。绝对温标（即开尔文温标）的零点定为零下 273 °C，也就是比水的冰点低 273°。

处理温度时，务必弄清所用的是哪一种度量体系，并知道如何从一种换算成另一种。换算公式如下：

华氏度 = （1.8 × 摄氏度）+ 32 摄氏度 = （华氏度 − 32）× 5⁄9 开尔文 = 摄氏度 + 273 兰氏度 = 华氏度 + 460 为了计算方便，常用兰氏温标把华氏温度换算成绝对温度。华氏读数高于零时，加上 460°。因此，72 °F 等于 460° 加 72°，即 532° 绝对温度。如果华氏读数低于零，则从 460° 中减去。因此，−40 °F 等于 460° 减 40°，即 420° 绝对温度。应当强调，兰氏温标并不像开尔文温标那样指示绝对温度读数，但这些换算可用于计算（此处夹有表格数据：Material / Specific Heat——Lead 0.031、Mercury 0.033、Brass 0.094、Copper 0.095、Iron or Steel 0.113、Glass 0.195、Alcohol 0.547、Aluminum 0.712、Water 1.000；刻线数值 100、0、−273、212、32、−460、373、273、0、672、492、0；标注 Pure water boils、Pure water freezes、Celsius (centigrade)、Kelvin、Rankine、Fahrenheit）

开尔文温标和摄氏温标在科学工作中使用得更广泛；因此，一些技术手册在给出说明和操作指示时可能使用这两种温标。华氏温标在美国普遍使用，大多数人都熟悉它。因此，本书大多数地方使用华氏温标。

热膨胀／热收缩

固体、液体和气体受热时都会发生热膨胀。除少数例外，固体受热膨胀、冷却收缩。与液体和气体中的膨胀相比，固体受热时的膨胀非常微小，因为固体的分子彼此靠得近得多，相互吸引也强得多。流体的膨胀在波义耳定律的研究中讨论。固体的热膨胀必须较为详细地说明，因为它与航空金属和材料关系密切。

由于某些物质膨胀得比另一些多，必须用实验测定每种物质的确切膨胀率。任何物质单位长度在温度升高一度时的膨胀量，称为该物质的线膨胀系数。要估算任何物体（例如钢轨）的膨胀量，必须知道关于它的三件事：它的长度、它所受的温升，以及它的膨胀系数。这一关系用以下方程表示：

膨胀量 = （系数）×（长度）×（温升）

如果一根钢棒在 21 °C 时正好长 9 ft，那么它在 55 °C 时长度是多少？钢的膨胀系数为 11 × 10−6。

膨胀量 = （11 × 10−6）×（9 英尺）× 34° 膨胀量 = 0.003366 英尺 把这个量加到棒的原始长度上，棒就变成 9.003366 ft 长。它的长度只增加了 4⁄100 英寸。

棒的长度增加量相对很小，但如果把棒放在不能自由膨胀的位置，热膨胀就会产生巨大的力。因此，在设计机体、动力装置或相关设备时，必须考虑热膨胀。

4. 语法解析

① so … that 引出的结果状语从句

原句：The water conducts heat so poorly that not enough heat reaches the ice to melt it.

拆解：主句 The water conducts heat；so poorly 为程度状语（so 修饰副词 poorly）；that not enough heat reaches the ice to melt it 为结果状语从句，其中 not enough heat 作从句主语，to melt it 是不定式作结果/目的补足。

要点：so + 形容词/副词 + that 与 such + 名词 + that 分工明确，此处 poorly 是副词，只能用 so；enough 修饰名词时前置（enough heat），修饰形容词时后置（good enough）。

② it 作形式主语 + 真实主语后置

原句：It is necessary to measure experimentally the exact rate of expansion of each one because some substances expand more than others.

拆解：It 为形式主语，真正主语是 to measure experimentally the exact rate …；experimentally 是方式副词插在动词与宾语之间（measure … the rate）；because 引导原因状语从句，some substances expand more than others 中 more 为比较级。

要点：形式主语 it 让长不定式后置，避免头重脚轻；翻译时不译「它」，直接译「必须用实验测定……」，这是技术英语的常规处理。

③ that 定语从句作主语 + be known as

原句：The amount that a unit length of any substance expands for a one degree rise in temperature is known as the coefficient of linear expansion for that substance.

拆解：主句主语 The amount 后接 that 定语从句（that 作 expands 的宾语，可省略）；主句谓语 is known as + 表语 the coefficient of linear expansion；for a one degree rise in temperature 为定语从句内的条件状语。

要点：that 在从句中作宾语，因此不能换成 where 或 when；be known as（被称为）与 be known for（因……而闻名）要分清，此处是给术语下定义。

④ 与现在事实相反的虚拟条件句

原句：The increase in the length of the rod is relatively small, but if the rod were placed where it could not expand freely, there would be a tremendous force exerted due to thermal expansion.

拆解：前一分句为系表结构；but 引出并列句，if 条件从句用过去式 were placed（被动），where it could not expand freely 为地点状语从句；主句 there would be a tremendous force exerted 为 there be 句式 + 虚拟语气，exerted 是过去分词作后置定语。

要点：were 在此是虚拟标志（不用 was）；there would be + 名词 + 过去分词 是技术文本说明「会产生某种作用」的紧凑写法，翻译时补出「产生」即可。

⑤ 过去分词短语作后置定语

原句：Radiation is the name given to this third method of heat transfer.

拆解：主句 Radiation is the name；given to this third method of heat transfer 为过去分词短语作 name 的后置定语，相当于 which is given to …。

要点：name given to sth. 是固定表达，意为「给某事物起的名称」；过去分词后置定语含有被动意义，若改写成定语从句必须补上 be 动词。

⑥ much 修饰比较级 + than 引出比较对象

原句：Some metals are much better conductors of heat than others.

拆解：主句 Some metals are much better conductors of heat；much 修饰比较级 better；than others 引出比较对象，others 指「其他金属」；conductors of heat 为名词短语作表语。

要点：比较级前可用 much、far、even、a lot 等程度词加强，但不能用 very；others 是代词，须与主语同类（金属对金属），不可换成 other。
