# Mechanical Movement Indicators

> 机体 Ch 10 · Mechanical Movement Indicators · 原文 PDF 第 547 页

## 1. 原文

There are many instruments on an aircraft that indicate the mechanical motion of a component, or even the aircraft itself. Some utilize the synchro remote-sensing and indicating systems described above. Other means for capturing and displaying mechanical movement information are also used. This section discusses some unique mechanical motion indicators and groups instruments by function. All give valuable feedback to the pilot on the condition of the aircraft in flight.

### Tachometers

The tachometer, or tach, is an instrument that indicates the speed of the crankshaft of a reciprocating engine. It can be a direct-or remote-indicating instrument, the dial of which is calibrated to indicate revolutions per minutes (rpm). On reciprocating engines, the tach is used to monitor engine power and to ensure the engine is operated within certified limits.

Gas turbine engines also have tachometers. They are used to monitor the speed(s) of the compressor section(s) of the engine. Turbine engine tachometers are calibrated in percentage of rpm with 100 percent corresponding to optimum turbine speed. This allows similar operating procedures despite the varied actual engine rpm of different engines.

In addition to the engine tachometer, helicopters use a tachometer to indicator main rotor shaft rpm. It should also be noted that many reciprocating-engine tachometers also have built-in numeric drums that are geared to the rotational mechanism inside. These are hour meters that keep track of the time the engine is operated. There are two types of tachometer system in wide use today: mechanical and electrical.

#### Mechanical Tachometers

Mechanical tachometer indicating systems are found on small, single-engine light aircraft in which a short distance exists between the engine and the instrument panel. They consist of an indicator connected to the engine by a flexible drive shaft. The drive shaft is geared into the engine so that when the engine turns, so does the shaft. The indicator contains a flyweight assembly coupled to a gear mechanism that drives a pointer. As the drive shaft rotates, centrifugal force acts on the flyweights and moves them to an angular position. This angular position varies with the rpm of the engine. The amount of movement of the flyweights is transmitted through the gear mechanism to the pointer. The pointer rotates to indicate this movement on the tachometer indicator, which is directly related to the rpm of the engine.

A more common variation of this type of mechanical tachometer uses a magnetic drag cup to move the pointer in the indicator. As the drive shaft turns, it rotates a permanent magnet in a close-tolerance aluminum cup. A shaft attached to the indicating point is attached to the exterior center of the cup. As the magnet is rotated by the engine flex drive cable, its magnetic field cuts through the conductor surrounding it, creating eddy currents in the aluminum cup. This current flow creates its own magnetic field, which interacts with the rotating magnet’s flux field. The result is that the cup tends to rotate, and with it, the indicating pointer. A calibrated restraining spring limits the cup’s rotation to the arc of motion of the pointer across the scale on the instrument face.

#### Electric Tachometers

It is not practical to use a mechanical linkage between the engine and the rpm indicator on aircraft with engines not mounted in the fuselage just forward of the instrument panel. Greater accuracy with lower maintenance is achieved through the use of electric tachometers. A wide variety of electric tachometer systems can be employed, so manufacturer’s instructions should be consulted for details of each specific tachometer system.

A popular electric tachometer system makes use of a small AC generator mounted to a reciprocating engine’s gear case or the accessory drive section of a turbine engine. As the engine turns, so does the generator. The frequency output of the generator is directly proportional to the speed of the engine. It is connected via wires to a synchronous motor in the indicator that mirrors this output. A drag cup, or drag disc link, is used to drive the indicator as in a mechanical tachometer. Two different types of generator units, distinguished by their type of mounting system, are shown in Figure 10-56 .

The dual tachometer consists of two tachometer indicator units housed in a single case. The indicator pointers show simultaneously, on one or two scales, the rpm of two engines. A dual tachometer on a helicopter often shows the rpm of the engine and the rpm of the main rotor. A comparison of the voltages produced by the two tach generators of this type of helicopter indicator gives information concerning clutch slippage. Athird indication showing this slippage is sometimes included in the helicopter tachometer. [ Figure 10-57]

Some turbine engines use tachometer probes for rpm indication, rather than a tach generator system. They provide a great advantage in that there are no moving parts. The probes are sealed units that are mounted on a flange and protrude into the compressor section of the engine. A magnetic field is set up inside the probe that extends through pole pieces and out the end of the probe. A rotating gear wheel, which moves at the same speed as the engine compressor shaft, alters the magnetic field flux density as it moves past the pole pieces at close proximity. This generates voltage signals in coils inside the probe. The amplitude (voltage) of the signals vary directly with the speed of the engine.

The tachometer probe’s output signals need to be processed in a remotely located module. They must also be amplified to drive a servo motor type indicator in the flight deck. They may also be used as input for an autothrottle or flight data acquisition system.

### Synchroscope

The synchroscope is an instrument that indicates whether two or more rotating devices, such as engines, are synchronized. Since synchroscopes compare rpm, they utilize the output from tachometer generators. The instrument consists of a small electric motor that receives electrical current from the generators of both engines. Current from the faster running engine controls the direction in which the synchroscope motor rotates.

If both engines are operating at exactly the same speed, the synchroscope motor does not operate. If one engine operates faster than the other, its tach generator signal causes the synchroscope motor to turn in a given direction. Should the speed of the other engine then become greater than that of the first engine, the signal from its tach generator causes the synchroscope motor to reverse itself and turn in the opposite direction. The pilot makes adjustments to steady the pointer so it does not move.

One use of synchroscope involve designating one of the engines as a master engine. The rpm of the other engine(s) is always compared to the rpm of this master engine. The dial face of the synchroscope indicator looks like Figure 10-59 . “Slow” and “fast” represent the other engine’s rpm relative to the master engine, and the pilot makes adjustments accordingly.

### Accelerometers

An accelerometer is an instrument that measures acceleration. It is used to monitor the forces acting upon an airframe. Accelerometers are also used in inertial reference navigation systems. The installation of accelerometers is usually limited to high-performance and aerobatic aircraft.

Simple accelerometers are mechanical, direct-reading instruments calibrated to indicate force in Gs. One G is equal to one times the force of gravity. The dial face of an accelerometer is scaled to show positive and negative forces. When an aircraft initiates a rapid climb, positive G force tends to push one back into one’s seat. Initiating a rapid decent causes a force in the opposite direction, resulting in a negative G force.

Most accelerometers have three pointers. One is continuously indicating the acceleration force experienced. The other two contain ratcheting devices. The positive G pointer follows the continuous pointer and stay at the location on the dial where the maximum positive force is indicated. The negative G pointer does the same for negative forces experienced. Both max force pointers can be reset with a knob on the instrument face.

The accelerometer operates on the principle of inertia. A mass, or weight, inside is free to slide along a shaft in response to the slightest acceleration force. When a maneuver creates an accelerating force, the aircraft and instrument move, but inertia causes the weight to stay at rest in space. As the shaft slides through the weight, the relative position of the weight on the shaft changes. This position corresponds to the force experienced. Through a series of pulleys, springs, and shafts, the pointers are moved on the dial to indicate the relative strength of the acceleration force. [ Figure 10-60] Forces can act upon an airframe along the three axes of flight. Single and multi-axis accelerometers are available, although most flight deck gauges are of the single-axis type. Inertial reference navigation systems make use of multi-axis accelerometers to continuously, mathematically calculate the location of the aircraft in a three dimensional plane.

Electric and digital accelerometers also exist. Solid-state sensors are employed, such as piezoelectric crystalline devices. In these instruments, when an accelerating force is applied, the amount of resistance, current flow, or capacitance changes in direct relationship to the size of the force. Micro-electric signals integrate well with digital computers designed to process and display information in the flight deck.

### Stall Warning & Angle of Attack (AOA) Indicators

An aircraft’s angle of attack (AOA) is the angle formed between the wing cord centerline and the relative wind. At a certain angle, airflow over the wing surfaces is insufficient to create enough lift to keep the aircraft flying, and a stall occurs. An instrument that monitors the AOA allows the pilot to avoid such a condition.

The simplest form of AOA indicator is a stall warning device that does not have a gauge located in the flight deck. It uses an aural tone to warn of an impending stall due to an increase in AOA. This is done by placing a reed in a cavity just aft of the leading edge of the wing. The cavity has an open passage to a precise point on the leading edge.

In flight, air flows over and under a wing. The point on the wing leading edge where the oncoming air diverges is known as the point of stagnation. As the AOA of the wing increases, the point of stagnation moves down below the open passage that leads inside the wing to the reed. Air flowing over the curved leading edge speeds up and causes a low pressure. This causes air to be sucked out of the inside of the wing through the passage. The reed vibrates as the air rushes by making a sound audible in the flight deck.

Another common device makes use of an audible tone as the AOA increases to near the point where the aircraft will stall. This stall warning device includes an electric switch that opens and closes a circuit to a warning horn audible in the flight deck. It may also be wired into a warning light circuit. The switch is located near the point of stagnation on the wing leading edge. A small lightly sprung tab activates the switch. At normal AOA, the tab is held down by air that diverges at the point of stagnation and flows under the wing. This holds the switch open so the horn does not sound nor the warning light illuminate. As the AOA increases, the point of stagnation moves down. The divergent air that flows up and over the wing now pushes the tab upward to close the switch and complete the circuit to the horn or light.

A true AOA indicating system detects the local AOA of the aircraft and displays the information on a flight deck indicator. It also may be designed to furnish reference information to other systems on high-performance aircraft. The sensing mechanism and transmitter are usually located on the forward side of the fuselage. It typically contains a heating element to ensure ice-free operation. Signals are sent from the sensor to the flight deck or computer(s) as required. An AOA indicator may be calibrated in actual angle degrees, arbitrary units, percentage of lift used, symbols, or even fast/ slow.

There are two main types of AOA sensors in common use. Both detect the angular difference between the relative wind and the fuselage, which is used as a reference plane. One uses a vane, known as an alpha vane, externally mounted to the outside of the fuselage. It is free to rotate in the wind. As the AOA changes, air flowing over the vane changes its angle. The other uses two slots in a probe that extends out of the side of the fuselage into the airflow. The slots lead to different sides of movable paddles in a chamber of the unit just inside the fuselage skin. As the AOA varies, the air pressure ported by each of the slots changes and the paddles rotate to neutralize the pressures. The shaft upon which the paddles rotate connects to a potentiometer wiper contact that is part of the unit. The same is true of the shaft of the alpha vane. The changing resistance of the potentiometer is used in a balanced bridge circuit to signal a motor in the indicator to move the pointer proportional to the AOA. [Figures 10-64 and 10-65]

Modern aircraft AOA sensor units send output signals to the ADC. There, the AOA data is used to create an AOA indication, usually on the primary flight display. AOA information can also be integrated with flap and slat position information to better determine the point of stall. Additionally, AOA sensors of the type described are subject to position error since airflow around the alpha vane and slotted probe changes somewhat with airspeed and aircraft attitude. The errors are small but can be corrected in the ADC.

To incorporate a warning of an impending stall, many AOA systems signal a stick shaker motor that literally shakes the control column to warn the pilot as the aircraft approaches a stall condition. Electrical switches are actuated in the AOA indicator at various preset AOA to activate the motor that drives an unbalanced weighted ring, causing the column to shake. Some systems include a stick pusher actuator that pushes the control yoke forward, lowering the nose of the aircraft when the critical AOA is approached. Regardless of the many existing variations for warning of an impending stall, the AOA system triggers all stall warnings in high performance aircraft.

## 2. 中文翻译

飞机上有很多仪表用来指示某个部件、甚至飞机本身的机械运动。有些采用上文所述的同步器遥测与指示系统，也使用捕获并显示机械运动信息的其他手段。本节讨论几种独特的机械运动指示器，并按功能对仪表进行归类。它们都为飞行员提供飞机飞行状态的有价值反馈。

### 转速表

转速表（tachometer，简称 tach）是指示往复式发动机曲轴转速的仪表。它可以是直读式或遥控式仪表，其表盘按每分钟转数（rpm）标定。在往复式发动机上，转速表用于监控发动机功率，并确保发动机在审定限制内工作。

燃气涡轮发动机也有转速表，用于监控发动机压气机部分的转速。涡轮发动机转速表以 rpm 的百分比标定，100% 对应最佳涡轮转速。这样，尽管不同发动机的实际转速各异，仍可采用相似的操作程序。

除发动机转速表外，直升机还用转速表指示主旋翼轴转速。还应指出，许多往复式发动机转速表内部还带有与转动机构联动的数字鼓轮，即记录发动机工作时间的计时表。目前广泛使用的转速表系统有两类：机械式和电动式。

#### 机械式转速表

机械式转速表指示系统见于发动机与仪表板之间距离较短的小型单发轻型飞机。它由指示器和一根连接发动机的柔性传动轴组成。传动轴通过齿轮接入发动机，发动机转动时轴也随之转动。指示器内有一个飞重组件，与驱动指针的齿轮机构相连。传动轴旋转时，离心力作用于飞重，使其转到某一角度位置，该角度位置随发动机转速而变化。飞重的位移量经齿轮机构传给指针，指针在转速表指示器上转动显示这一位移，而位移与发动机转速直接相关。

这类机械转速表更常见的一种变型，是用磁滞杯（drag cup）来带动指示器指针。传动轴转动时，带动一个永久磁铁在公差很小的铝杯中旋转。一根与指示针相连的轴固定在杯的外侧中心。发动机柔性传动索带动磁铁旋转时，其磁场切割周围的导体，在铝杯中产生涡流。该电流产生自己的磁场，与旋转磁铁的磁通场相互作用，结果使杯趋于转动，指示指针也随之转动。一根经标定的限位游丝把杯的转动限制在指针沿表盘刻度扫过的弧段之内。

#### 电动式转速表

当发动机不是装在仪表板正前方的机身内时，在发动机与转速指示器之间采用机械连杆并不现实。使用电动转速表可获得更高精度和更低维护量。电动转速表系统种类很多，具体细节应查阅各型系统的制造商说明。

一种常见的电动转速表系统，使用一台小型交流发电机，装在往复式发动机的齿轮箱或涡轮发动机的附件传动段上。发动机转动时发电机随之转动，发电机输出频率与发动机转速成正比。它通过导线连接到指示器中的同步电动机，由该电动机反映这一输出。与机械式转速表一样，用磁滞杯或磁滞盘联结来驱动指示器。图10-56示出两种不同安装方式的发电机组件。

双转速表由两个转速表指示器组件装在同一壳体内构成。指示器指针在一个或两个刻度上同时显示两台发动机的转速。直升机上的双转速表常同时显示发动机转速和主旋翼转速。比较这类直升机指示器两台转速发电机所产生的电压，可获得离合器打滑的信息。直升机转速表中有时还包含显示这种打滑的第三项指示。[图10-57]

有些涡轮发动机用转速表探头而非转速发电机系统来指示转速。其突出优点是没有运动部件。探头是密封组件，装在法兰上，伸入发动机压气机段。探头内部建立磁场，磁场穿过极靴并从探头端部伸出。一个随发动机压气机轴同速转动的齿轮，在近距离掠过极靴时改变磁通密度，从而在探头内部线圈中产生电压信号。信号的幅值（电压）随发动机转速直接变化。

转速表探头的输出信号需要送到远处的模块中处理，还必须放大以驱动驾驶舱中的伺服电动机式指示器。它们也可以作为自动油门或飞行数据采集系统的输入。

### 同步指示器

同步指示器是一种指示两个或多个旋转装置（如发动机）是否同步的仪表。由于同步指示器比较转速，它利用转速发电机的输出。该仪表由一台小型电动机组成，电动机从两台发动机的发电机获得电流。转速较快那台发动机的电流控制同步指示器电动机的旋转方向。

如果两台发动机转速完全相同，同步指示器电动机不动作。若一台发动机比另一台快，其转速发电机信号使同步指示器电动机朝某一方向转动。若另一台发动机的转速随后超过第一台，其转速发电机的信号使同步指示器电动机反转，朝相反方向转动。飞行员进行调整，使指针稳定不动。

同步指示器的一种用法是把其中一台发动机指定为主发动机，其他发动机的转速始终与该主发动机转速比较。同步指示器刻度盘如图10-59所示。“慢”和“快”表示另一台发动机相对主发动机的转速，飞行员据此进行调整。

### 加速度计

加速度计是测量加速度的仪表，用于监控作用于机体上的力。加速度计也用于惯性基准导航系统。加速度计的安装通常限于高性能飞机和特技飞机。

简单的加速度计是机械式直读仪表，按 G 值标定指示力的大小。1 G 等于一倍重力。加速度计表盘刻度同时显示正力和负力。飞机急剧爬升时，正 G 力会把人压向座椅；急剧下降则产生方向相反的力，形成负 G 力。

大多数加速度计有三个指针。一个连续指示所经历的加速度力，另外两个带有棘轮机构。正 G 指针跟随连续指示指针，停留在刻度盘上最大正力所指的位置；负 G 指针同样停留在所经历最大负力的位置。两个最大力指针都可以用表盘上的旋钮复位。

加速度计依据惯性原理工作。内部的质量块（重物）可沿一根轴自由滑动，以响应极微小的加速度力。机动产生加速度力时，飞机和仪表都在运动，但惯性使质量块在空间中保持静止。轴从质量块中滑过，质量块在轴上的相对位置就发生变化，这一位置对应所受到的力。通过一系列滑轮、弹簧和轴，指针在刻度盘上移动，指示加速度力的相对大小。[图10-60] 力可沿飞行的三个轴向作用于机体。加速度计有单轴和多轴两种，不过大多数驾驶舱仪表是单轴型。惯性基准导航系统使用多轴加速度计，以数学方式连续计算飞机在三维空间中的位置。

也有电动和数字式加速度计，采用压电晶体器件等固态传感器。在这类仪表中，施加加速度力时，电阻、电流或电容的变化量与力的大小直接相关。微电信号与用于处理和显示驾驶舱信息的数字计算机很容易集成。

### 失速警告与迎角（AOA）指示器

飞机的迎角（AOA）是机翼弦线中心线与相对气流之间形成的夹角。当迎角达到某一角度时，流过机翼表面的气流不足以产生维持飞机飞行的升力，于是发生失速。监控迎角的仪表可使飞行员避免这种状态。

最简单的迎角指示器是一种没有驾驶舱表盘的失速警告装置，它用音响提示来警告因迎角增大而即将发生的失速。做法是在机翼前缘稍后方的一个空腔内装一片簧片，空腔有一条开口通道通向机翼前缘的某一精确位置。

飞行中，气流从机翼上下流过。机翼前缘上来流分流的那一点称为驻点。随着机翼迎角增大，驻点下移到通向机翼内部簧片的开口通道下方。流过弯曲前缘的气流加速，形成低压，把空气从机翼内部经通道吸出。气流急速通过时使簧片振动，发出驾驶舱内可听到的声音。

另一种常见装置在迎角增大到接近飞机失速点时发出音响。这种失速警告装置包含一个电开关，接通或断开驾驶舱内警告喇叭的电路，也可能接入警告灯电路。开关装在机翼前缘驻点附近，由一片弹力很轻的小舌片触动。正常迎角下，在驻点分流并从机翼下方流过的空气把舌片压住，使开关保持断开，喇叭不响、警告灯不亮。迎角增大时，驻点下移，向上流过机翼上方的偏转气流把舌片向上推，使开关闭合，接通喇叭或灯的电路。

真正的迎角指示系统检测飞机的局部迎角，并在驾驶舱指示器上显示。它还可以设计成向高性能飞机上的其他系统提供基准信息。传感机构和发送器通常装在机身前端侧面，一般内含加热元件以保证无冰工作。信号按要求从传感器送往驾驶舱或计算机。迎角指示器可按实际角度、任意单位、所用升力百分比、符号甚至“快／慢”来标定。

常用的迎角传感器主要有两种，都以机身作为基准面，检测相对气流与机身之间的角度差。一种使用外部装在机身外侧的叶片，称为迎角叶片（alpha vane），可在风中自由转动；迎角变化时，流过叶片的气流改变其角度。另一种使用从机身侧面伸入气流的探头上开的两条缝。两条缝通向壳体（紧贴机身蒙皮内侧）内腔中可动桨叶的两侧。迎角变化时，两条缝所传递的空气压力改变，桨叶转动以平衡压力。桨叶转动的轴与属于该组件的一个电位器滑动触点相连，迎角叶片的轴也是如此。电位器的电阻变化用于平衡电桥电路，向指示器中的电动机发出信号，使指针按迎角成比例移动。[图10-64 和 10-65]

现代飞机的迎角传感器组件把输出信号送往 ADC。在那里，迎角数据用于生成迎角指示，通常显示在主飞行显示器上。迎角信息还可以与襟翼和缝翼位置信息综合，以更准确地判断失速点。此外，上述类型的迎角传感器存在位置误差，因为迎角叶片和开缝探头周围的气流会随空速和飞机姿态有所变化。误差虽小，但可在 ADC 中修正。

为加入即将失速的警告，许多迎角系统会向抖杆电动机发信号，在飞机接近失速状态时真正抖动操纵杆以警告飞行员。迎角指示器内设有电开关，在若干预设迎角下动作，驱动带动不平衡配重环的电动机，使驾驶杆抖动。有些系统还包含推杆作动器，在接近临界迎角时把操纵盘前推，使机头下俯。尽管现有失速警告形式多样，高性能飞机上的所有失速警告都由迎角系统触发。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 被动语态 + in which 定语从句
**原句**：Mechanical tachometer indicating systems are found on small, single-engine light aircraft in which a short distance exists between the engine and the instrument panel.
**拆解**：主句为被动语态 are found，主语 Mechanical tachometer indicating systems，on small, single-engine light aircraft 为地点状语；in which 引导定语从句修饰 aircraft，从句中 a short distance 是主语，exists 为谓语，between...and... 为范围状语。
**要点**：in which 相当于 in the aircraft，不能换成 where 之外的随意介词；从句是 there be 式的存在句变体，真正的主语是 a short distance，不要把 which 当成主语。

### ② as 时间状语从句 + 现在分词结果状语
**原句**：As the magnet is rotated by the engine flex drive cable, its magnetic field cuts through the conductor surrounding it, creating eddy currents in the aluminum cup.
**拆解**：As 引导时间状语从句，从句为被动语态 is rotated by...；主句主语 its magnetic field，谓语 cuts through，宾语 the conductor，surrounding it 为现在分词短语作后置定语；creating eddy currents in the aluminum cup 为现在分词短语作结果状语。
**要点**：creating 的逻辑主语是主句主语 its magnetic field，表示“因而产生”的自然结果，不是伴随动作。surrounding 修饰紧挨着的 conductor，形式上与 creating 一样都是 -ing，但一个作定语、一个作状语，功能完全不同。

### ③ it 作形式主语 + with 复合结构
**原句**：It is not practical to use a mechanical linkage between the engine and the rpm indicator on aircraft with engines not mounted in the fuselage just forward of the instrument panel.
**拆解**：It 为形式主语，真正主语是不定式 to use a mechanical linkage...；is not practical 为系表结构；between...and... 修饰 linkage；with engines not mounted in the fuselage... 是 with 复合结构，作 aircraft 的后置定语，其中 engines 与 mount 是被动关系，用过去分词。
**要点**：这里的 with 短语不是伴随状语而是定语，说明是哪一类飞机。not mounted 是过去分词否定式，若换成 not mounting 就变成“发动机不安装别的东西”，意思全错。

### ④ in that 引导原因状语从句
**原句**：They provide a great advantage in that there are no moving parts.
**拆解**：主句 They provide a great advantage 为主谓宾结构；in that 引导原因状语从句，从句是 there be 存在句，真正主语为 no moving parts。
**要点**：in that 是固定连词，意为“在于／因为”，不能理解成“在……里面”。它比 because 更书面，常用于说明某项优点具体体现在哪里。

### ⑤ 非限制性定语从句 + as 时间状语从句
**原句**：A rotating gear wheel, which moves at the same speed as the engine compressor shaft, alters the magnetic field flux density as it moves past the pole pieces at close proximity.
**拆解**：主语 A rotating gear wheel 后接 which 引导的非限制性定语从句；主句谓语 alters，宾语 the magnetic field flux density；as it moves past the pole pieces at close proximity 为时间状语从句。定语从句内 the same speed as 是同级比较。
**要点**：the same...as 中的 as 是关系代词性质的比较结构，与后面引导状语从句的 as 不是一回事，句中两个 as 各司其职。非限制性定语从句用逗号隔开，删去不影响主句成立。

### ⑥ 虚拟条件倒装 Should
**原句**：Should the speed of the other engine then become greater than that of the first engine, the signal from its tach generator causes the synchroscope motor to reverse itself and turn in the opposite direction.
**拆解**：Should 提到句首构成倒装的条件状语从句，等于 If the speed of the other engine should then become greater...；主句主语 the signal，后接 from its tach generator 定语，谓语 causes，宾语 the synchroscope motor，宾补为并列不定式 to reverse itself and turn in the opposite direction。
**要点**：should 置于句首、省略 if 是书面语中的虚拟条件倒装，语气比 if 从句更正式。greater than that of... 中的 that 替代 the speed，避免重复。
