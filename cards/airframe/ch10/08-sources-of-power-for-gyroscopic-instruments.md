# Sources of Power for Gyroscopic Instruments

## 1. 原文

Gyroscopic instruments are essential instruments used on all aircraft. They provide the pilot with critical attitude and directional information and are particularly important while flying under IFR. The sources of power for these instruments can vary. The main requirement is to spin the gyroscopes at a high rate of speed. Originally, gyroscopic instruments were strictly vacuum driven. A vacuum source pulled air across the gyro inside the instruments to make the gyros spin. Later, electricity was added as a source of power. The turning armature of an electric motor doubles as the gyro rotor. In some aircraft, pressure, rather than vacuum, is used to induce the gyro to spin. Various systems and powering configurations have been developed to provide reliable operation of the gyroscopic instruments.

### Vacuum Systems

Vacuum systems are very common for driving gyro instruments. In a vacuum system, a stream of air directed against the rotor vanes turns the rotor at high speed. The action is similar to a water wheel. Air at atmospheric pressure is first drawn through a filter(s). It is then routed into the instrument and directed at vanes on the gyro rotor. A suction line leads from the instrument case to the vacuum source. From there, the air is vented overboard. Either a venturi or a vacuum pump can be used to provide the vacuum required to spin the rotors of the gyro instruments.

The amount of vacuum required for instrument operation is usually between 3½ inches to 4½ inches of mercury. It is usually adjusted by a vacuum relief valve located in the supply line. Some turn-and-bank indicators require a lower vacuum setting. This can be obtained through the use of an additional regulating valve in the turn and bank vacuum supply line.

#### Venturi Tube Systems

The velocity of the air rushing through a venturi can create sufficient suction to spin instrument gyros. A line is run from the gyro instruments to the throat of the venturi mounted on the outside of the airframe. The low pressure in the venturi tube pulls air through the instruments, spins the gyros, and expels the air overboard through the venturi. This source of gyro power is used on many simple, early aircraft.

A light, single-engine aircraft can be equipped with a 2-inch venturi (2 inches of mercury vacuum capacity) to operate the turn and bank indicator. It can also have a larger 8-inch venturi to power the attitude and heading indicators. Simplified illustrations of these venturi vacuum systems are shown in Figure 10-87 . Normally, air going into the instruments is filtered.

The advantages of a venturi as a suction source are its relatively low cost and its simplicity of installation and operation. It also requires no electric power. But there are serious limitations. A venturi is designed to produce the desired vacuum at approximately 100 mph at standard sea level conditions. Wide variations in airspeed or air density cause the suction developed to fluctuate. Airflow can also be hampered by ice that can form on the venturi tube. Additionally, since the rotor does not reach normal operating speed until after takeoff, preflight operational checks of venturi powered gyro instruments cannot be made. For these reasons, alternate sources of vacuum power were developed.

#### Engine-Driven Vacuum Pump

The vane-type engine-driven pump is the most common source of vacuum for gyros installed in general aviation, light aircraft. One type of engine-driven pump is geared to the engine and is connected to the lubricating system to seal, cool, and lubricate the pump. Another commonly used pump is a dry vacuum pump. It operates without external lubrication and installation requires no connection to the engine oil supply. It also does not need the air oil separator or gate check valve found in wet pump systems. In many other respects, the dry pump system and oil lubricated system are the same.

When a vacuum pump develops a vacuum (negative pressure), it also creates a positive pressure at the outlet of the pump. This pressure is compressed air. Sometimes, it is utilized to operate pressure gyro instruments. The components for pressure systems are much the same as those for a vacuum system as listed below. Other times, the pressure developed by the vacuum pump is used to inflate de-ice boots or inflatable seals or it is vented overboard.

An advantage of engine-driven pumps is their consistent performance on the ground and in flight. Even at low engine rpm, they can produce more than enough vacuum so that a regulator in the system is needed to continuously provide the correct suction to the vacuum instruments. As long as the engine operates, the relatively simple vacuum system adequately spins the instrument gyros for accurate indications. However, engine failure, especially on single-engine aircraft, could leave the pilot without attitude and directional information at a critical time. To thwart this shortcoming, often the turn and bank indicator operates with an electrically driven gyro that can be driven by the battery for a short time. Thus, when combined with the aircraft’s magnetic compass, sufficient attitude and directional information is still available.

Multiengine aircraft typically contain independent vacuum systems for the pilot and copilot instruments driven by separate vacuum pumps on each of the engines. Should an engine fail, the vacuum system driven by the still operating engine supplies a full complement of gyro instruments. An interconnect valve may also be installed to connect the failed instruments to the still operational pump.

#### Typical Pump-Driven System

The following components are found in a typical vacuum system for gyroscopic power supply. A brief description is given of each. Refer to the figures for detailed illustrations.

Air-oil separator—oil and air in the vacuum pump are exhausted through the separator, which separates the oil from the air; the air is vented overboard, and the oil is returned to the engine sump. This component is not present when a dry-type vacuum pump is used. The self-lubricating nature of the pump vanes requires no oil.

Vacuum regulator or suction relief valve—since the system capacity is more than is needed for operation of the instruments, the adjustable vacuum regulator is set for the vacuum desired for the instruments. Excess suction in the instrument lines is reduced when the spring-loaded valve opens to atmospheric pressure.

Gate check valve—prevents possible damage to the instruments by engine backfire that would reverse the flow of air and oil from the pump.

Pressure relief valve—since a reverse flow of air from the pump would close both the gate check valve and the suction relief valve, the resulting pressure could rupture the lines. The pressure relief valve vents positive pressure into the atmosphere.

Selector valve—In twin-engine aircraft having vacuum pumps driven by both engines, the alternate pump can be selected to provide vacuum in the event of either engine or pump failure, with a check valve incorporated to seal off indicator operates on less vacuum than that required by the the failed pump. other instruments, the vacuum in the main line must be reduced for use by this instrument. An inline restrictor valve Restrictor valve—Since the turn needle of the turn and bank performs this function. This valve is either a needle valve or a spring-loaded regulating valve that maintains a constant, reduced vacuum for the turn-and-bank indicator.

Air filter—A master air filter screens foreign matter from the air flowing through all the gyro instruments. It is an extremely important filter requiring regular maintenance. Clogging of the master filter reduces airflow and causes a lower reading on the suction gauge. Each instrument is also provided with individual filters. In systems with no master filter that rely only upon individual filters, clogging of a filter does not necessarily show on the suction gauge.

Suction gauge—a pressure gauge which indicates the difference between the pressure inside the system and atmospheric or flight deck pressure. It is usually calibrated in inches of mercury. The desired vacuum and the minimum and maximum limits vary with gyro system design. If the desired vacuum for the attitude and heading indicators is 5 inches and the minimum is 4.6 inches, a reading below the latter value indicates that the airflow is not spinning the gyros fast enough for reliable operation. In many aircraft, the system provides a suction gauge selector valve permitting the pilot to check the vacuum at several points in the system.

Suction/vacuum pressures discussed in conjunction with the operation of vacuum systems are actually negative pressures, indicated as inches of mercury below that of atmospheric pressure. The minus sign is usually not presented, as the importance is placed on the magnitude of the vacuum developed. In relation to an absolute vacuum (0 psi or 0 "Hg), instrument vacuum systems have positive pressure.

Figure 10-91 shows a typical engine-driven pump vacuum system containing the above components. A pump capacity of approximately 10 "Hg at engine speeds above 1,000 rpm is normal. Pump capacity and pump size vary in different aircraft, depending on the number of gyros to be operated.

#### Twin-Engine Aircraft Vacuum System Operation

Twin-engine aircraft vacuum systems are more complicated. They contain an engine-driven vacuum pump on each engine. The associated lines and components for each pump are isolated from each other and act as two independent vacuum systems. The vacuum lines are routed from each vacuum pump through a vacuum relief valve and through a check valve to the vacuum four-way selector valve. The four-way valve permits either pump to supply a vacuum manifold. From the manifold, flexible hoses connect the vacuum-operated instruments into the system. To reduce the vacuum for the turn and bank indicators, needle valves are included in both lines to these units. Lines to the artificial horizons and the directional gyro receive full vacuum. From the instruments, lines are routed to the vacuum gauge through a turn and bank selector valve. This valve has three positions: main, left turn and bank (T&B), and right T&B. In the main position, the vacuum gauge indicates the vacuum in the lines of the artificial horizons and directional gyro. In the other positions, the lower value of vacuum for the turn and bank indicators can be read.

A schematic of this twin-engine aircraft vacuum system is shown in Figure 10-92 . Note the following components: two engine-driven pumps, two vacuum relief valves, two flapper type check valves, a vacuum manifold, a vacuum restrictor for each turn and bank indicator, an engine four-way selector valve, one vacuum gauge, and a turn-and-bank selector valve. Not shown are system and individual instrument filters. A drain line may also be installed at the low point in the system.

### Pressure-Driven Gyroscopic Instrument Systems

Gyroscopic instruments are finely balanced devices with jeweled bearings that must be kept clean to perform properly. When early vacuum systems were developed, only oil- lubricated pumps were available. Even with the use of air-oil separators, the pressure outputs of these pumps contain traces of oil and dirt. As a result, it was preferred to draw clean air through the gyro instruments with a vacuum system, rather than using pump output pressure that presented the risk of contamination. The development of self-lubricated dry pumps greatly reduced pressure output contaminates. This made pressure gyro systems possible.

At high altitudes, the use of pressure-driven gyros is more efficient. Pressure systems are similar to vacuum systems and make use of the same components, but they are designed for pressure instead of vacuum. Thus, a pressure regulator is used instead of a suction relief valve. Filters are still extremely important to prevent damage to the gyros. Normally, air is filtered at the inlet and outlet of the pump in a pressure gyro system.

### Electrically-Driven Gyroscopic Instrument Systems

A spinning motor armature can act as a gyroscope. This is the basis for electrically driven gyroscopic instruments in which the gyro rotor spin is powered by an electric motor.

Electric gyros have the advantage of being powered by battery for a limited time if a generator fails or an engine is lost. Since air is not sent through the gyro to spin the rotor, contamination worries are also reduced. Also, elimination of vacuum pumps, plumbing, and vacuum system components saves weight.

On many small, single-engine aircraft, electric turn-and-bank or turn coordinators are combined with vacuum-powered attitude and directional gyro instruments as a means for redundancy. The reverse is also possible. By combining both types of instruments in the instrument panel, the pilot has more options. On more complex multiengine aircraft, reliable, redundant electrical systems make use of all electric-powered gyro instruments possible.

It should be noted that electric gyro instruments have a failure flag. The flag will come in if the gyro is not powered or is spinning at too slow of a speed. Usually, this is in the form of a red flag with the word “off” or “gyro” written on it.

## 2. 中文翻译

陀螺仪表是所有飞机上都有的重要仪表。它们为飞行员提供关键的姿态与方向信息，在仪表飞行（IFR）条件下飞行时尤为重要。这些仪表的动力来源各不相同。主要要求是以很高的转速使陀螺旋转。最早，陀螺仪表完全由真空驱动：真空源把空气抽过仪表内的陀螺，使陀螺旋转。后来，电也被用作动力源，电动机转动的电枢同时充当陀螺转子。有些飞机则用压力而非真空来驱使陀螺旋转。为保证陀螺仪表可靠工作，已研制出多种系统和供能配置。

### 真空系统

真空系统在驱动陀螺仪表方面非常普遍。在真空系统中，一股气流吹向转子叶片，使转子高速旋转，其作用类似水轮。处于大气压力的空气先被抽过过滤器，然后引入仪表并吹向陀螺转子上的叶片。一根抽气管从仪表壳体通到真空源，空气再从那里排出机外。可用文氏管或真空泵来提供使陀螺仪表转子旋转所需的真空。

仪表工作所需的真空量通常在 3½ 至 4½ 英寸汞柱之间。一般由供气管路中的真空释压活门调节。有些转弯侧滑仪需要较低的真空设定值，这可通过在转弯侧滑仪真空供气管路中增设一个调节活门来获得。

#### 文氏管系统

急速流过文氏管的空气速度可产生足够吸力使仪表陀螺旋转。一根管路从陀螺仪表接到安装在机体外部的文氏管喉部。文氏管内的低压把空气抽过仪表，使陀螺旋转，并使空气经文氏管排出机外。这种陀螺动力源用于许多简单的早期飞机。

一架轻型单发飞机可装一个 2 英寸文氏管（真空能力 2 英寸汞柱）来带动转弯侧滑仪，还可装一个较大的 8 英寸文氏管来带动姿态指示器和航向指示器。这些文氏管真空系统的简化示意图见图 10-87。通常进入仪表的空气要经过滤。

文氏管作为吸力源的优点是成本相对较低、安装与使用简单，而且不需要电力。但它有严重局限。文氏管是按标准海平面条件下约 100 mph 的空速来设计产生所需真空的。空速或空气密度的较大变化会使产生的吸力波动。气流还可能因文氏管上结冰而受阻。此外，由于起飞后转子才能达到正常工作转速，文氏管驱动的陀螺仪表无法在飞行前做工作检查。由于这些原因，人们又研制了别的真空动力源。

#### 发动机驱动的真空泵

叶片式发动机驱动泵是通用航空轻型飞机陀螺仪最常用的真空源。一种发动机驱动泵由发动机齿轮驱动，并接入润滑系统以密封、冷却和润滑泵体。另一种常用泵是干式真空泵，它无需外部润滑即可工作，安装时不必连接发动机滑油源，也不需要湿泵系统中所用的油气分离器或单向活门。在许多其他方面，干泵系统与油润滑系统是相同的。

当真空泵产生真空（负压）时，也会在泵的出口处产生正压，这部分压力是压缩空气。有时利用它来驱动压力式陀螺仪表。压力系统的部件与下面列出的真空系统部件大体相同。另一些时候，真空泵产生的压力用于给除冰套或充气密封件充气，或者直接排到机外。

发动机驱动泵的优点是地面和空中性能稳定。即使在发动机低转速时，它们也能产生绰绰有余的真空，因此系统中需要装调节器，以持续向真空仪表提供正确的吸力。只要发动机在运转，这个相对简单的真空系统就能充分驱动仪表陀螺，给出准确指示。然而，发动机失效——尤其是单发飞机——可能在关键时刻使飞行员失去姿态与方向信息。为弥补这一缺陷，转弯侧滑仪通常采用电驱动陀螺，可由蓄电池短时驱动。这样，再配合飞机的磁罗盘，仍可获得足够的姿态与方向信息。

多发飞机通常为机长和副驾驶仪表设置各自独立的真空系统，分别由各台发动机上的独立真空泵驱动。若一台发动机失效，仍在工作的发动机所驱动的真空系统可提供全套陀螺仪表。也可能装有一个互联活门，把失效一侧的仪表接到仍在工作的泵上。

#### 典型的泵驱动系统

典型的陀螺供电真空系统包含下列部件，下面对每一部件作简要说明。详细图示请参见各图。

油气分离器——真空泵中的油和空气经分离器排出，分离器把油与空气分开：空气排到机外，油回到发动机油底壳。使用干式真空泵时没有此部件，因为泵叶片自润滑，不需要油。

真空调节器或吸力释压活门——由于系统能力超过仪表工作需要，可调真空调节器被设定为仪表所需的真空值。当弹簧加载活门向大气开启时，仪表管路中多余的吸力便被释放。

单向活门——防止发动机回火使泵中空气和油的流向逆转而可能损坏仪表。

释压活门——由于从泵倒流的空气会同时关闭单向活门和吸力释压活门，由此产生的压力可能使管路破裂。释压活门把正压释放到大气中。

选择活门——在由两台发动机驱动的真空泵的双发飞机上，任一发动机或泵失效时可选择另一台泵提供真空，并设有单向活门以隔离失效的泵。由于转弯侧滑仪的工作真空低于其他仪表所需，主管路中的真空必须降低后才能供该仪表使用。管路中的限流活门即起此作用。该活门为针形活门或弹簧加载调节活门，可为转弯侧滑仪保持恒定而较低的真空。

空气过滤器——主空气过滤器滤除流经所有陀螺仪表的空气中的杂质。它是极为重要的过滤器，需要定期维护。主过滤器堵塞会减少气流，使吸力表读数偏低。每个仪表还各自配有过滤器。在没有主过滤器、只依靠各仪表单独过滤器的系统中，某个过滤器堵塞不一定会在吸力表上反映出来。

吸力表——一种压力表，指示系统内压力与大气压力或驾驶舱压力之差，通常以英寸汞柱标定。所需真空以及最小、最大限值随陀螺系统设计而异。若姿态和航向指示器所需真空为 5 英寸、最小值为 4.6 英寸，则读数低于后者说明气流不足以使陀螺转得足够快，工作不可靠。许多飞机上，系统设有吸力表选择活门，使飞行员能检查系统若干点的真空。

与真空系统工作相关的吸力/真空压力实际上是负压，以低于大气压力的英寸汞柱表示。通常不写出负号，因为重要的是所产生真空的大小。相对于绝对真空（0 psi 或 0 "Hg），仪表真空系统具有正压。

图 10-91 示出包含上述部件的典型发动机驱动泵真空系统。发动机转速高于 1,000 rpm 时，约 10 "Hg 的泵能力属正常。泵能力和泵的尺寸随机型而异，取决于要驱动的陀螺数量。

#### 双发飞机真空系统的工作

双发飞机的真空系统更为复杂。每台发动机上各有一台发动机驱动真空泵，各泵相关的管路和部件彼此隔离，构成两个独立的真空系统。真空管路从每台真空泵经真空释压活门、再经单向活门通向真空四通选择活门。四通活门使任一台泵都能向真空总管供真空。从总管引出软管，把真空驱动的仪表接入系统。为降低转弯侧滑仪的真空，通往这两个组件的管路中都装有针形活门。通往姿态仪和方向陀螺仪的管路则获得全真空。从各仪表引出的管路经转弯侧滑仪选择活门通到真空表。该活门有三个位置：主、左转弯侧滑仪（T&B）和右 T&B。在“主”位，真空表指示姿态仪和方向陀螺仪管路中的真空；在其他位置，可读出转弯侧滑仪较低的真空值。

### 压力驱动的陀螺仪表系统

陀螺仪表是装有宝石轴承、经过精细平衡的装置，必须保持清洁才能正常工作。早期研制真空系统时，只有油润滑泵可用。即使使用油气分离器，这类泵的压力输出中仍含有微量油和污物。因此，人们更愿意用真空系统把清洁空气抽过陀螺仪表，而不使用有污染风险的泵输出压力。自润滑干式泵的出现大大减少了压力输出中的污染物，这才使压力陀螺系统成为可能。

在高空，使用压力驱动的陀螺更为有效。压力系统与真空系统相似，使用相同的部件，但按压力而非真空设计。因此用压力调节器代替吸力释压活门。过滤器仍然极为重要，以防损坏陀螺。通常压力陀螺系统在泵的进口和出口都对空气过滤。

### 电驱动陀螺仪表系统

旋转的电动机电枢可充当陀螺仪，这就是电驱动陀螺仪表的基础——陀螺转子的旋转由电动机提供动力。

电陀螺的优点是：若发电机失效或发动机失去动力，可由蓄电池供电一段时间。由于不让空气流过陀螺来驱动转子，污染方面的顾虑也减少了。此外，省去真空泵、管路和真空系统部件还可减轻重量。

在许多小型单发飞机上，电动转弯侧滑仪或转弯协调器与真空驱动的姿态、方向陀螺仪表配合使用，作为冗余手段。反过来也可以。把两类仪表都装在仪表板上，飞行员就有更多选择。在更复杂的多发飞机上，可靠而冗余的电气系统使全部采用电动陀螺仪表成为可能。

应当注意，电陀螺仪表设有失效旗。若陀螺未通电或转速过慢，旗子就会出现。通常是一面写有 “off” 或 “gyro” 字样的红旗。

| four-way selector valve | n. | 四通选择活门 | 双发真空系统中选择任一泵向真空总管供真空 |
| suction gauge | n. | 吸力表 | 以英寸汞柱指示系统内负压与大气压之差 |
| turn and bank indicator | n. | 转弯侧滑仪 | 需较低真空设定、常由独立管路供能的陀螺仪表 |

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 过去分词短语作后置定语
**原句**：In a vacuum system, a stream of air directed against the rotor vanes turns the rotor at high speed.
**拆解**：介词短语 In a vacuum system 作状语；主语中心词 a stream of air，过去分词短语 directed against the rotor vanes 作后置定语修饰 a stream；谓语 turns（第三人称单数），宾语 the rotor，at high speed 为方式状语。
**要点**：谓语 turns 紧跟其后的是复数名词 vanes，容易被误当成主语，实际主语是单数的 a stream，故用 turns。directed 与 a stream 是被动关系（气流被引向叶片），所以用过去分词而不是 directing。

### ② since 引导原因状语从句 + could 表推测
**原句**：since a reverse flow of air from the pump would close both the gate check valve and the suction relief valve, the resulting pressure could rupture the lines.
**拆解**：Since 引导原因状语从句，从句主语 a reverse flow of air from the pump，谓语 would close，宾语 both A and B；主句主语 the resulting pressure，谓语 could rupture，宾语 the lines。
**要点**：这里的 since 表原因（“由于”），不是“自从”，判断依据是从句与主句构成因果而非时间关系。would / could 在此不表过去，而是对“倒流一旦发生”的假设性推测，属情态动词的虚拟用法。

### ③ so that 引导结果状语从句 + 被动不定式
**原句**：Even at low engine rpm, they can produce more than enough vacuum so that a regulator in the system is needed to continuously provide the correct suction to the vacuum instruments.
**拆解**：Even at low engine rpm 为让步性介词短语作状语；主句主语 they，谓语 can produce，宾语 more than enough vacuum；so that 引导结果状语从句，从句为被动 is needed，不定式 to provide... 作目的状语。
**要点**：more than enough 是“绰绰有余”，不能拆成比较结构理解。so that 从句的主语 a regulator 与主句主语 they 不同，所以不能简化为 so as to；若要简化须主从句主语一致。

### ④ 现在分词短语作后置定语 + permit sb. to do
**原句**：In many aircraft, the system provides a suction gauge selector valve permitting the pilot to check the vacuum at several points in the system.
**拆解**：主语 the system，谓语 provides，宾语 a suction gauge selector valve；现在分词短语 permitting the pilot to check... 作后置定语修饰 valve，其中 permit sb. to do 为复合宾语结构；at several points in the system 为地点状语。
**要点**：permitting 的逻辑主语是 a suction gauge selector valve（活门使飞行员能检查），不是 the system。现在分词作定语表主动、进行；若改用 permitted 则变成“被允许”，意思就错了。

### ⑤ it 作形式主语 + that 主语从句
**原句**：It should be noted that electric gyro instruments have a failure flag.
**拆解**：It 为形式主语，真正主语是 that 引导的主语从句 electric gyro instruments have a failure flag；谓语为被动式 should be noted。
**要点**：这是技术文献中极常见的“It should be noted that...”句型，译成“应当注意……”。that 只起引导作用，不作成分；谓语用被动，是因为施动者（读者）不必说出。
