# Direction Indicating Instruments

> 机体 Ch 10 · Direction Indicating Instruments · 原文 PDF 第 560 页

## 1. 原文

A myriad of techniques and instruments exist to aid the pilot in navigation of the aircraft. An indication of direction is part of this navigation. While the next chapter deals with communication and navigation, this section discusses some of the magnetic direction indicating instruments. Additionally, a common, reliable gyroscopic direction indicator is discussed in the gyroscopic instrument section of this chapter.

### Magnetic Compass

Having an instrument on board an aircraft that indicates direction can be invaluable to the pilot. 14 CFR part 91, section 91.205 requires that aircraft with standard category airworthiness certificates have a magnetic direction indicator for VFR flight during the day. The magnetic compass is a direction finding instrument that has been used for navigation for hundreds of years. It is a simple instrument that takes advantage of the earth’s magnetic field.

Figure 10-78 shows the earth and the magnetic field that surrounds it. The magnetic north pole is very close to the geographic North Pole of the globe, but they are not the same. An ordinary permanent magnet that is free to do so, aligns itself with the direction of the earth’s magnetic field. Upon this principle, an instrument is constructed that the pilot can reference for directional orientation. Permanent magnets are attached under a float that is mounted on a pivot so it is free to rotate in the horizontal plane. As such, the magnets align with the earth’s magnetic field. A numerical compass card, usually graduated in 5° increments, is constructed around the perimeter of the float. It serves as the instrument dial. The entire assembly is enclosed in a sealed case that is filled with a liquid similar to kerosene. This dampens vibration and oscillation of the moving float assembly and decreases friction.

On the front of the case, a glass face allows the numerical compass card to be referenced against a vertical lubber line. The magnetic heading of the aircraft is read by noting the graduation on which the lubber line falls. Thus, direction in any of 360° can be read off the dial as the magnetic float compass card assembly holds its alignment with magnetic north, while the aircraft changes direction.

The liquid that fills the compass case expands and contracts as altitude changes and temperature fluctuates. A bellows diaphragm expands and contracts to adjust the volume of the space inside the case so it remains full.

There are accuracy issues associated with using a magnetic compass. The main magnets of a compass align not only with the earth’s magnetic field, they actually align with the composite field made up of all magnetic influences around them, meaning local electromagnetic influence from metallic structures near the compass and operation of the aircraft’s electrical system. This is called magnetic deviation. It causes a magnet’s alignment with the earth’s magnetic field to be altered. Compensating screws are turned, which move small permanent magnets in the compass case to correct for this magnetic deviation. The two set-screws are on the face of the instrument and are labeled N-S and E-W. They position the small magnets to counterbalance the local magnetic influences acting on the main compass magnets.

The process for knowing how to adjust for deviation is known as swinging the compass. It is described in the instrument maintenance pages near the end of this chapter. Magnetic deviation cannot be overlooked. It should never be more than 10°. Using nonferrous mounting screws and shielding or twisting the wire running to the compass illuminating lamp are additional steps taken to keep deviation to a minimum.

Another compass error is called magnetic variation. It is caused by the difference in location between the earth’s magnetic poles and the geographic poles. There are only a few places on the planet where a compass pointing to magnetic north is also pointing to geographic North. A line drawn through these locations is called the Agonic line. At all other points, there is some variation between that which a magnetic compass indicates is north and geographic (true) North. Isogonic lines drawn on aeronautical charts indicate points of equal variation. Depending on the location of the aircraft, airmen must add or subtract degrees from the magnetic indication to obtain true geographic location information.

The earth’s magnetic field exits the poles vertically and arches around to extend past the equator horizontally or parallel to the earth’s surface. Operating an aircraft near the magnetic poles causes what is known as dip error. The compass magnets pull downward toward the pole, rather than horizontally, as is the case near the equator. This downward motion causes inaccuracy in the indication. Although the compass float mechanism is weighted to compensate, the closer the aircraft is to the north or south magnetic poles, the more pronounced the errors.

Dip errors manifest themselves in two ways. The first is called acceleration error. If an aircraft is flying on an east-west path and simply accelerates, the inertia of the float mechanism causes the compass to swing to the north. Rapid deceleration causes it to swing southward. Second, if flying toward the North Pole and a banked turn is made, the downward pull of the magnetic field initially pulls the card away from the direction of the turn. The opposite is true if flying south from the North Pole and a banked turn is initiated. In this case, there is initially a pull of the compass indicator toward the direction of the turn. These kinds of movements are called turning errors.

Another peculiarity exists with the magnetic compass that is not dip error. Look again at the magnetic compass in Figure 10-79 . If flying north or toward any indicated heading, turning the aircraft to the left causes a steady decrease in the heading numbers. But, before the turn is made, the numbers to the left on the compass card are actually increasing. The numbers to the right of the lubber line rotate behind it on a left turn. So, the compass card rotates opposite to the direction of the intended turn. This is because, from the pilot’s seat, you are actually looking at the back of the compass card. While not a major problem, it is more intuitive to see the 360° of direction oriented as they are on an aeronautical chart or a hand-held compass.

### Vertical Magnetic Compass

Solutions to the shortcomings of the simple magnetic compass described above have been engineered. The vertical magnetic compass is a variation of the magnetic compass that eliminates the reverse rotation of the compass card just described. By mounting the main indicating magnets of the compass on a shaft rather than a float, through a series of gears, a compass card can be made to turn about a horizontal axis. This allows the numbers for a heading, towards which the pilot wants to turn, to be oriented correctly on the indicating card. In other words, when turning right, increasing numbers are to the right; when turning left, decreasing numbers rotate in from the left.

Many vertical magnetic compasses have also replaced the liquid-filled instrument housing with a dampening cup that uses eddy currents to dampen oscillations. Note that a vertical magnetic compass and a directional gyro look very similar and are often in the lower center position of the instrument panel basic T. Both use the nose of an aircraft as the lubber line against which a rotating compass card is read. Vertical magnetic compasses are characterized by the absence of the hand adjustment knob found on DGs, which is used to align the gyro with a magnetic indication.

### Remote Indicating Compass

Magnetic deviation is compensated for by swinging the compass and adjusting compensating magnets in the instrument housing. A better solution to deviation is to remotely locate the magnetic compass in a wing tip or vertical stabilizer where there is very little interference with the earth’s magnetic field. By using a synchro remote indicating system, the magnetic compass float assembly can act as the rotor of the synchro system. As the float mechanism rotates to align with magnetic north in the remotely located compass, a varied electric current can be produced in the transmitter. This alters the magnetic field produced by the coils of the indicator in the flight deck, and a magnetic indication relatively free from deviation is displayed. Many of these systems are of the magnesyn type.

### Remote Indicating Slaved Gyro Compass (Flux Gate Compass)

An elaborate and very accurate method of direction indication has been developed that combines the use of a gyro, a magnetic compass, and a remote indicating system. It is called the slaved gyro compass or flux gate compass system. A study of the gyroscopic instruments section of this chapter assists in understanding this device.

A gyroscopic direction indicator is augmented by magnetic direction information from a remotely located compass. The type of compass used is called a flux valve or flux gate compass. It consists of a very magnetically permeable circular segmented core frame called a spider. The earth’s magnetic field flows through this iron core and varies its distribution through segments of the core as the flux valve is rotated via the movement of the aircraft. Pickup coil windings are located on each of the core’s spider legs that are positioned 120° apart.

The distribution of earth’s magnetic field flowing through the legs is unique for every directional orientation of the aircraft. A coil is placed in the center of the core and is energized by AC current. As the AC flow passes through zero while changing direction, the earth’s magnetic field is allowed to flow through the core. Then, it is blocked or gated as the magnetic field of the core current flow builds to its peak again. The cycle is repeated at the frequency of the AC supplied to the excitation coil. The result is repeated flow and nonflow of the earth’s flux across the pickup coils. During each cycle, a unique voltage is induced in each of the pickup coils reflecting the orientation of the aircraft in the earth’s magnetic field.

The electricity that flows from each of the pickup coils is transmitted out of the flux valve via wires into a second unit. It contains an autosyn transmitter, directional gyro, an amplifier, and a triple wound stator that is similar to that found in the indicator of a synchro system. Unique voltage is induced in the center rotor of this stator which reflects the voltage received from the flux valve pickup coils sent through the stator coils. It is amplified and used to augment the position of the DG. The gyro is wired to be the rotor of an autosyn synchro system, which transmits the position of the gyro into an indicator unit located in the flight deck. In the indicator, a vertical compass card is rotated against a small airplane type lubber line like that in a vertical magnetic compass.

Further enhancements to direction finding systems of this type involving the integration of radio navigation aids are common. The radio magnetic indicator (RMI) is one such variation. In addition to the rotating direction indicator of the slaved gyro compass, it contains two pointers. Each pointer can show the bearing to either a very high frequency (VHF) omnidirectional range (VOR) station or to a non-directional automatic direction finder (ADF) beacon. These and other radio navigation aids are discussed further in the communications and navigation chapter of this handbook. It should also be noted that integration of slaved gyro direction indicating system information into auto-pilot systems is also possible.

### Solid State Magnetometers

Solid state magnetometers are used on many modern aircraft. They have no moving parts and are extremely accurate. Tiny layered structures react to magnetism on a molecular level resulting in variations in electron activity. These low power consuming devices can sense not only the direction to the earth’s magnetic poles, but also the angle of the flux field. They are free from oscillation that plagues a standard magnetic compass. They feature integrated processing algorithms and easy integration with digital systems.

## 2. 中文翻译

帮助飞行员导航飞机的技术与仪表数不胜数，方向指示就是这种导航的一部分。下一章讲述通信与导航，本节则讨论几种磁方向指示仪表。此外，本章陀螺仪表一节还会讨论一种常用而可靠的陀螺方向指示器。

### 磁罗盘

机上装有能指示方向的仪表，对飞行员而言价值无可估量。美国联邦法规 14 CFR 第 91 部第 91.205 节要求，持有标准类适航证的飞机在昼间目视飞行（VFR）时必须装有磁方向指示器。磁罗盘是一种用于导航已有数百年历史的测向仪表。它结构简单，利用地球磁场工作。

图 10-78 示出地球及其周围的磁场。磁北极与地球的地理北极非常接近，但二者并不相同。一块可以自由转动的普通永磁铁，会自行对准地球磁场的方向。仪表即依此原理制成，供飞行员参照确定方向。永磁铁固定在浮子下方，浮子装在枢轴上，因而可在水平面内自由转动。这样，磁铁便与地球磁场对齐。浮子周边制有通常以 5° 为刻度的数字罗盘刻度盘，用作仪表的表盘。整个组件封装在密封的壳体内，壳中充注类似煤油的液体。这可以减弱活动浮子组件的振动与摆动，并减小摩擦。

壳体正面装有玻璃面，透过它可将数字罗盘刻度盘与一条垂直的基准线（lubber line）对照读数。飞机磁航向即基准线所落的那条刻度。因此，随着飞机改变方向，磁浮子罗盘刻度盘组件始终保持与磁北对齐，表盘上便可读出 360° 中任一方向。

充入罗盘壳体的液体会随高度变化和温度波动而膨胀、收缩。波纹膜盒随之伸缩，调节壳内空间的容积，使其始终保持充满。

使用磁罗盘存在精度问题。罗盘的主磁铁不仅与地球磁场对齐，实际上是与周围所有磁影响叠加而成的合成磁场对齐——也就是罗盘附近金属结构以及飞机电气系统工作所产生的局部电磁影响。这称为罗差（magnetic deviation）。它会使磁铁偏离地球磁场的方向。转动补偿螺钉，带动罗盘壳内的小永磁铁移动，即可修正这种罗差。两颗调节螺钉位于仪表正面，标有 N-S（南北）和 E-W（东西）。它们调整小磁铁的位置，以抵消作用在主罗盘磁铁上的局部磁影响。

掌握如何修正罗差的过程称为罗盘校摆（swinging the compass）。本章末尾附近的仪表维修章节对此有介绍。罗差不可忽视，任何时候都不应超过 10°。使用非铁磁性安装螺钉，以及对通往罗盘照明灯的导线加以屏蔽或双绞，都是把罗差保持在最小程度的附加措施。

另一种罗盘误差称为磁差（magnetic variation）。它由地球磁极与地理极位置不同引起。地球上只有少数几个地方，罗盘指向磁北的同时也指向地理北。穿过这些地点画出的线称为零磁差线（Agonic line）。在其他所有地点，磁罗盘所指的北与地理（真）北之间都存在一定磁差。航空图上画出的等磁差线（Isogonic lines）标示磁差相等的地点。飞行员必须根据飞机所在位置，在磁指示上加减度数，才能得到真实的地理位置信息。

地球磁场从两极垂直穿出，然后拱形弯转，到赤道以外便水平延伸，或与地球表面平行。在磁极附近操纵飞机会产生所谓的倾差（dip error）。罗盘磁铁会向下朝磁极方向拉，而不是像赤道附近那样水平。这种向下的运动造成指示不准。虽然罗盘浮子机构经过配重补偿，但飞机越靠近南北磁极，误差就越明显。

倾差以两种方式表现出来。第一种称为加速度误差。如果飞机沿东西向航路飞行而单纯加速，浮子机构的惯性会使罗盘摆向北方；急剧减速则使它摆向南方。第二种，如果朝北极飞行并做带坡度转弯，磁场的向下拉力最初会把刻度盘拉离转弯方向。如果从北极朝南飞并开始带坡度转弯，情况则相反：此时罗盘指示器最初会被拉向转弯方向。这类运动称为转弯误差。

磁罗盘还有一种不属于倾差的奇特现象。再看图 10-79 中的磁罗盘。如果朝北或沿任一指示航向飞行，向左转弯会使航向数字持续减小。但在开始转弯之前，刻度盘左侧的数字实际上是在增大。左转时，基准线右侧的数字会转到它后面。也就是说，刻度盘的转动方向与预定转弯方向相反。这是因为从驾驶座上看，你实际看到的是罗盘刻度盘的背面。这虽不算大问题，但若像航空图或手持罗盘那样把 360° 方向按原样排布，看起来会更直观。

### 垂直磁罗盘

针对上述简单磁罗盘的缺点，已有工程解决办法。垂直磁罗盘是磁罗盘的一种变型，可消除刚才所说的刻度盘反向转动。把罗盘的主指示磁铁装在轴上而不是浮子上，再通过一系列齿轮，可使刻度盘绕水平轴转动。这样，飞行员想要转往的航向数字就能在指示盘上以正确方位排布。换言之，右转时增大的数字在右侧；左转时减小的数字从左侧转入。

许多垂直磁罗盘还用阻尼杯取代充液式仪表壳体，利用涡流阻尼振荡。要注意，垂直磁罗盘与方向陀螺仪外观非常相似，通常都装在仪表板基本 T 形布局的下中部。二者都以机头作为基准线，对照转动的刻度盘读数。垂直磁罗盘的特征是没有方向陀螺仪上那种手调旋钮——那个旋钮用来把陀螺仪对准磁指示。

### 远读式罗盘

罗差可通过校摆罗盘、调整仪表壳内的补偿磁铁来补偿。更好的解决办法是把磁罗盘远程安装在翼尖或垂直安定面内，那里对地球磁场的干扰极小。采用自同步远读系统时，磁罗盘浮子组件可充当同步系统的转子。当远处罗盘中的浮子机构转动到与磁北对齐时，发送器内便产生变化的电流。这改变驾驶舱内指示器线圈产生的磁场，从而显示基本不受罗差影响的磁方向。这类系统许多属于磁同步（magnesyn）式。

### 远读式随动陀螺罗盘（磁通门罗盘）

人们已研制出一种精细而非常精确的方向指示方法，把陀螺仪、磁罗盘和远读系统结合使用。它称为随动陀螺罗盘或磁通门罗盘系统。学习本章陀螺仪表一节有助于理解这一装置。

陀螺方向指示器由来自远处罗盘的磁方向信息加以修正。所用罗盘称为磁通阀或磁通门罗盘。其核心是一个磁导率很高的圆形分段铁芯框架，称为蜘蛛架（spider）。地球磁场流过这个铁芯，并随磁通阀因飞机运动而转动，改变磁场在各铁芯段中的分布。拾取线圈绕在铁芯各条蜘蛛腿上，各腿相隔 120° 布置。

流经各腿的地球磁场分布，对飞机的每一个方向姿态都是独特的。铁芯中心放有一个线圈，由交流电励磁。当交流电流改变方向、经过零值时，地球磁场得以流过铁芯；随后，当铁芯电流的磁场再次增大到峰值时，地球磁场被阻断（门控）。该循环按供给励磁线圈的交流电频率重复。结果是地球磁通在拾取线圈上反复流过与不流过。每个循环中，各拾取线圈都感应出反映飞机在地球磁场中姿态的独特电压。

从各拾取线圈流出的电流经导线送出磁通阀，进入第二个组件。该组件包含一台自同步（autosyn）发送器、方向陀螺仪、一个放大器，以及一个与同步系统指示器中类似的三绕组定子。这个定子的中心转子中感应出独特电压，它反映从磁通阀拾取线圈经定子线圈送来的电压。该电压经放大后用于修正方向陀螺仪（DG）的位置。陀螺仪被接成自同步系统的转子，把陀螺仪的位置传输到驾驶舱内的指示器组件。在指示器中，垂直刻度盘相对一个小飞机形基准线转动，如同垂直磁罗盘那样。

这类测向系统进一步的改进通常涉及与无线电导航设备的整合。无线电磁指示器（RMI）就是其中一种变型。除了随动陀螺罗盘的转动方向指示外，它还有两根指针。每根指针可显示到甚高频（VHF）全向信标（VOR）台或到无方向性自动定向仪（ADF）信标的方位。这些及其他无线电导航设备将在本手册通信与导航一章中进一步讨论。还应指出，把随动陀螺方向指示系统的信息整合进自动驾驶仪系统也是可行的。

### 固态磁强计

许多现代飞机使用固态磁强计。它们没有运动部件，精度极高。微小的层状结构在分子层面响应磁性，引起电子活动的变化。这些低功耗器件不仅能感知地球磁极的方向，还能感知磁通场的角度。它们不受标准磁罗盘那种振荡的困扰，并具有集成处理算法、易于与数字系统集成的特点。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 定语从句套目的状语从句
**原句**：Permanent magnets are attached under a float that is mounted on a pivot so it is free to rotate in the horizontal plane.
**拆解**：主句为被动语态 Permanent magnets are attached under a float；that 引导定语从句修饰 float，从句谓语 is mounted（被动），on a pivot 为地点状语；so (that) 引导目的/结果状语从句 it is free to rotate...，其中 it 指 the float。
**要点**：that 从句里又套了 so 从句，两层从属关系要分清：that 只管到 float 的性质，so 从句说明装枢轴的目的。so it is free 中的 so 是 so that 的省略形式，不译成“所以”，而译“以便/因而”。

### ② not only ... 后不接 but also 的并列句
**原句**：The main magnets of a compass align not only with the earth’s magnetic field, they actually align with the composite field made up of all magnetic influences around them, meaning local electromagnetic influence from metallic structures near the compass and operation of the aircraft’s electrical system.
**拆解**：前一分句主语 The main magnets of a compass，谓语 align，not only with... 为介词短语状语；逗号后为并列分句，主语 they，谓语 align，宾语 the composite field，后接过去分词短语 made up of... 作后置定语；句末 meaning... 为现在分词短语作补充说明。
**要点**：not only 之后本应配 but (also)，这里作者改用逗号加分句来加强语气，属于破格写法，读时不要把后一分句当成 not only 的宾语。made up of 的逻辑主语是 the composite field，meaning 的逻辑主语则是整个前述事实。

### ③ 让步状语从句 + the more ... the more 比较结构
**原句**：Although the compass float mechanism is weighted to compensate, the closer the aircraft is to the north or south magnetic poles, the more pronounced the errors.
**拆解**：Although 引导让步状语从句，其中 is weighted 为被动、to compensate 为目的状语；主句为 the + 比较级 ..., the + 比较级 ... 结构：前段 the closer the aircraft is to... 是从句（表语提前），后段 the more pronounced the errors 是主句（表语提前）。
**要点**：the more...the more 结构中两个分句都用部分倒装式的语序——表语提到句首，系动词留在后面。第二段 the more pronounced the errors 省略了系动词 are，还原为 the errors are more pronounced。

### ④ by + 动名词作方式状语 + 被动谓语
**原句**：By mounting the main indicating magnets of the compass on a shaft rather than a float, through a series of gears, a compass card can be made to turn about a horizontal axis.
**拆解**：By + 动名词短语 mounting ... on a shaft rather than a float 作方式状语；through a series of gears 为插入的方式状语；主句主语 a compass card，谓语 can be made（被动），补足语 to turn about a horizontal axis 为不定式。
**要点**：make sth. do 变被动后，原宾语补足语的不定式 to 必须补出（be made to turn），这是被动语态常见考点。rather than a float 与 on a shaft 并列，比较的是安装基座而非动作。

### ⑤ 省略式让步状语 + it 作形式主语 + see sth. done
**原句**：While not a major problem, it is more intuitive to see the 360° of direction oriented as they are on an aeronautical chart or a hand-held compass.
**拆解**：While not a major problem 为省略了主语和系动词的让步状语（= While it is not a major problem）；主句 it 为形式主语，真正主语是 to see the 360° of direction oriented...；oriented 为过去分词作宾语补足语；as they are... 为方式状语从句。
**要点**：While 后直接跟名词短语，说明主从句主语一致且含 be 动词，故可省略。see sth. done 表示“看到某物处于某种状态”，这里刻度盘是“被排布”，所以用过去分词 oriented 而非 orienting。as they are 中的 they 指 the 360° of direction。
