# Common Gyroscopic Instruments

> 机体 Ch 10 · Common Gyroscopic Instruments · 原文 PDF 第 573 页

## 1. 原文

### Vacuum-Driven Attitude Gyros

The attitude indicator, or artificial horizon, is one of the most essential flight instruments. It gives the pilot pitch and roll information that is especially important when flying without outside visual references. The attitude indicator operates with a gyroscope rotating in the horizontal plane. Thus, it mimics the actual horizon through its rigidity in space. As the aircraft pitches and rolls in relation to the actual horizon, the gyro gimbals allow the aircraft and instrument housing to pitch and roll around the gyro rotor that remains parallel to the ground. A horizontal representation of the airplane in miniature is fixed to the instrument housing. A painted semisphere simulating the horizon, the sky, and the ground is attached to the gyro gimbals. The sky and ground meet at what is called the horizon bar. The relationship between the horizon bar and the miniature airplane are the same as those of the aircraft and the actual horizon. Graduated scales reference the degrees of pitch and roll. Often, an adjustment knob allows pilots of varying heights to place the horizon bar at an appropriate level.

In a typical vacuum-driven attitude gyro system, air is pulled through a filter and then through the attitude indicator in a manner that spins the gyro rotor inside. An erecting mechanism is built into the instrument to assist in keeping the gyro rotor rotating in the intended plane. Precession caused by bearing friction makes this necessary. After air engages the scalloped drive on the rotor, it flows from the instrument to the vacuum pump through four ports. These ports all exhaust the same amount of air when the gyro is rotating in plane. When the gyro rotates out of plane, air tends to port out of one side more than another. Vanes close to prevent this, causing more air to flow out of the opposite side. The force from this unequal venting of the air re-erects the gyro rotor.

Early vacuum-driven attitude indicators were limited in how far the aircraft could pitch or roll before the gyro gimbals contacted stops, causing abrupt precession and tumbling of the gyro. Many of these gyros include a caging device. It is used to erect the rotor to its normal operating position prior to flight or after tumbling. A flag indicates that the gyro must be uncaged before use. More modern gyroscopic instruments are built so they do not tumble, regardless of the angular movement of the aircraft about its axes.

In addition to the contamination potential introduced by the air-drive system, other shortcomings exist in the performance of vacuum-driven attitude indicators. Some are induced by the erection mechanism. The pendulous vanes that move to direct airflow out of the gyro respond not only to forces caused by a deviation from the intended plane of rotation, but centrifugal force experienced during turns also causes the vanes to allow asymmetric porting of the gyro vacuum air. The result is inaccurate display of the aircraft’s attitude, especially in skids and steep banked turns. Also, abrupt acceleration and deceleration imposes forces on the gyro rotor. Suspended in its gimbals, it acts similar to an accelerometer, resulting in a false nose-up or nose-down indication. Pilots must learn to recognize these errors and adjust accordingly.

### Electric Attitude Indicators

Electric attitude indicators are very similar to vacuum-driven gyro indicators. The main difference is in the drive mechanism. Inside the gimbals of an electric gyro, a small squirrel cage electric motor is the rotor. It is typically driven by 115-volt, 400-cycle AC. It turns at approximately 21,000 rpm.

Other characteristics of the vacuum-driven gyro are shared by the electric gyro. The rotor is still oriented in the horizontal plane. The free gyro gimbals allow the aircraft and instrument case to rotate around the gyro rotor that remains rigid in space. A miniature airplane fixed to the instrument case indicates the aircraft’s attitude against the moving horizon bar behind it.

Electric attitude indicators address some of the shortcomings of vacuum-driven attitude indicators. Since there is no air flowing through an electric attitude indicator, air filters, regulators, plumbing lines and vacuum pump(s) are not needed. Contamination from dirt in the air is not an issue, resulting in the potential for longer bearing life and less precession. Erection mechanism ports are not employed, so pendulous vanes responsive to centrifugal forces are eliminated.

It is still possible that the gyro may experience precession and need to be erected. This is done with magnets rather than vent ports. A magnet attached to the top of the gyro shaft spins at approximately 21,000 rpm. Around this magnet, but not attached to it, is a sleeve that is rotated by magnetic attraction at approximately 44 to 48 rpm. Steel balls are free to move around the sleeve. If the pull of gravity is not aligned with the axis of the gyro, the balls fall to the low side. The resulting precession re-aligns the axis of rotation vertically.

Typically, electric attitude indicator gyros can be caged manually by a lever and cam mechanism to provide rapid erection. When the instrument is not getting sufficient power for normal operation, an off flag appears in the upper right hand face of the instrument.

### Gyroscopic Direction Indicator or Directional Gyro (DG)

The gyroscopic direction indicator or directional gyro (DG) is often the primary instrument for direction. Because a magnetic compass fluctuates so much, a gyro aligned with the magnetic compass gives a much more stable heading indication. Gyroscopic direction indicators are located at the center base of the instrument panel basic T.

A vacuum-powered DG is common on many light aircraft. Its basis for operation is the gyro’s rigidity in space. The gyro rotor spins in the vertical plane and stays aligned with the direction to which it is set. The aircraft and instrument case moves around the rigid gyro. This causes a vertical compass card that is geared to the rotor gimbal to move. It is calibrated in degrees, usually with every 30 degrees labeled. The nose of a small, fixed airplane on the instrument glass indicates the aircraft’s heading.

Vacuum-driven direction indicators have many of the same basic gyroscopic instrument issues as attitude indicators. Built-in compensation for precession varies and a caging device is usually found. Periodic manual realignment with the magnetic compass by the pilot is required during flight.

### Turn Coordinators

Many aircraft make use of a turn coordinator. The rotor of the gyro in a turn coordinator is canted upwards 30°. As such, it responds not only to movement about the vertical axis, but also to roll movements about the longitudinal axis. This is useful because it is necessary to roll an aircraft to turn it about the vertical axis. Instrument indication of roll, therefore, is the earliest possible warning of a departure from straight-and-level flight.

Typically, the face of the turn coordinator has a small airplane symbol. The wing tips of the airplane provide the indication of level flight and the rate at which the aircraft is turning.

### Turn-and-Slip Indicator

The turn-and-slip indicator may also be referred to as the turn-and-bank indicator, or needle-and-ball indicator. Regardless, it shows the correct execution of a turn while banking the aircraft and indicates movement about the vertical axis of the aircraft (yaw). Most turn-and-slip indicators are located below the airspeed indicator of the instrument panel basic T, just to the left of the direction indicator.

The turn-and-slip indicator is actually two separate devices built into the same instrument housing: a turn indicator pointer and slip indicator ball. The turn pointer is operated by a gyro that can be driven by a vacuum, air pressure, or by electricity. The ball is a completely independent device. It is a round agate, or steel ball, in a glass tube filled with dampening fluid. It moves in response to gravity and centrifugal force experienced in a turn. The gyro turn-and-slip indicator may be driven electrically or pneumatically (by vacuum or air pressure).

Turn indicators vary. They all indicate the rate at which the aircraft is turning. Three degrees of turn per second cause an aircraft to turn 360° in 2 minutes. This is considered a standard turn. This rate can be indicated with marks right and left of the pointer, which normally rests in the vertical position. Sometimes, no marks are present, and the width of the pointer is used as the calibration device. In this case, one pointer width deflection from vertical is equal to the 3° per second standard 2-minute turn rate. Faster aircraft tend to turn more slowly and have graduations or labels that indicate 4-minute turns. In other words, a pointer’s width or alignment with a graduation mark on this instrument indicates that the aircraft is turning a 1 1 ⁄ 2° per second and completes a 360° turn in 4 minutes. It is customary to placard the instrument face with words indicating whether it is a 2-or 4-minute turn indicator.

The turn pointer indicates the rate at which an aircraft is turning about its vertical axis. It does so by using the precession of a gyro to tilt a pointer. The gyro spins in a vertical plane aligned with the longitudinal axis of the aircraft. When the aircraft rotates about its vertical axis during a turn, the force experienced by the spinning gyro is exerted about the vertical axis. Due to precession, the reaction of the gyro rotor is 90° further around the gyro in the direction of spin. This means the reaction to the force around the vertical axis is movement around the longitudinal axis of the aircraft. This causes the top of the rotor to tilt to the left or right. The pointer is attached with linkage that makes the pointer deflect in the opposite direction, which matches the direction of turn. So, the aircraft’s turn around the vertical axis is indicated around the longitudinal axis on the gauge. This is intuitive to the pilot when regarding the instrument, since the pointer indicates in the same direction as the turn.

The slip indicator (ball) part of the instrument is an inclinometer. The ball responds only to gravity during coordinated straight-and-level flight. Thus, it rests in the lowest part of the curved glass between the reference wires. When a turn is initiated, and the aircraft is banked, both gravity and the centrifugal force of the turn act upon the ball. If the turn is coordinated, the ball remains in place. Should a skidding turn exist, the centrifugal force exceeds the force of gravity on the ball and it moves in the direction of the outside of the turn. During a slipping turn, there is more bank than needed, and gravity is greater than the centrifugal force acting on the ball. The ball moves in the curved glass toward the inside of the turn.

As mentioned previously, often power for the turn-and-slip indicator gyro is electrical if the attitude and direction indicators are vacuum powered. This allows limited operation off battery power should the vacuum system and the electric generator fail. The directional and attitude information from the turn-and-slip indicator, combined with information from the pitot static instruments, allow continued safe emergency operation of the aircraft.

Electrically powered turn-and-slip indicators are usually DC powered. Vacuum-powered turn-and-slip indicators are usually run on less vacuum (approximately 2 "Hg) than fully gimbaled attitude and direction indicators. Regardless, proper vacuum must be maintained for accurate turn rate information to be displayed.

## 2. 中文翻译

### 真空驱动的姿态陀螺仪

姿态指示器（attitude indicator），又称人工地平仪（artificial horizon），是最重要的飞行仪表之一。它向飞行员提供俯仰和滚转信息，在无外部目视参考飞行时尤为重要。姿态指示器依靠在水平面内旋转的陀螺工作，因此通过其在空间中的定轴性来模拟真实地平线。当飞机相对真实地平线俯仰和滚转时，陀螺的常平架允许飞机和仪表壳体绕着保持与地面平行的陀螺转子俯仰、滚转。一个微缩的飞机水平示意图固定在仪表壳体上。一个模拟地平线、天空和地面的半球形涂装面连接在陀螺常平架上。天空与地面相交处称为地平线杆（horizon bar）。地平线杆与微缩飞机之间的关系，与飞机和真实地平线之间的关系相同。刻度盘标出俯仰与滚转的角度。通常还有一个调节旋钮，让身材高矮不同的飞行员把地平线杆调到合适的高度。

在典型的真空驱动姿态陀螺系统中，空气被抽过一个滤器，然后以能使内部陀螺转子旋转的方式流过姿态指示器。仪表内装有修正机构（erecting mechanism），帮助陀螺转子保持在预定的旋转平面内。轴承摩擦引起的进动使这一机构成为必要。空气吹动转子上的齿形驱动面之后，经四个端口从仪表流向真空泵。当陀螺在平面内旋转时，这些端口排出的空气量相同。当陀螺偏离平面旋转时，空气会从某一侧排出得比另一侧多。风门随即关闭以阻止这种情况，使更多空气从相反一侧流出。这种不均匀排气产生的力把陀螺转子重新扶正。

早期的真空驱动姿态指示器受限于飞机俯仰或滚转的角度：超过一定角度，陀螺常平架就会碰到限位，引起突发的进动和陀螺翻滚（tumbling）。许多这类陀螺装有锁定装置（caging device）。它用于在飞行前或陀螺翻滚之后，把转子扶正到正常工作位置。一个旗标会提示陀螺在使用前必须解锁。更现代的陀螺仪表在制造上保证无论飞机绕其各轴如何角运动，都不会翻滚。

除了空气驱动系统带来的污染隐患外，真空驱动姿态指示器的性能还存在其他缺陷。有些是由修正机构引起的。那些移动以引导气流排出陀螺的摆式风门，不仅响应偏离预定旋转平面所产生的力，转弯时受到的离心力也会使风门让陀螺真空空气产生不对称排放。其结果是飞机姿态显示不准确，在侧滑和陡坡转弯时尤其明显。此外，突然加速和减速会对陀螺转子施加作用力。转子悬置在常平架中，其作用类似加速度计，会造成虚假的抬头或低头指示。飞行员必须学会识别这些误差并相应修正。

### 电动姿态指示器

电动姿态指示器与真空驱动陀螺指示器非常相似，主要差别在驱动机构。在电动陀螺的常平架内，一个小型鼠笼式电动机就是转子。它通常由115伏、400周的交流电驱动，转速约为每分钟21,000转。

真空驱动陀螺的其他特性电动陀螺也同样具备。转子仍定向于水平面内。自由陀螺常平架允许飞机和仪表壳体绕着在空间中保持定轴的陀螺转子转动。固定在仪表壳体上的微缩飞机，以背后移动的地平线杆为参照指示飞机姿态。

电动姿态指示器解决了真空驱动姿态指示器的部分缺陷。由于电动姿态指示器内没有空气流过，因此不需要空气滤器、调节器、管路和真空泵。空气中的污物污染不再是问题，轴承寿命因而可能更长，进动也更小。不再采用修正机构的排气端口，因此对离心力敏感的摆式风门被取消。

陀螺仍有可能发生进动而需要扶正。这时用磁铁而不是排气端口来完成。陀螺轴顶端装有一块磁铁，以约每分钟21,000转旋转。磁铁周围有一个不与其相连的套筒，靠磁吸力以约每分钟44至48转旋转。钢球可在套筒周围自由移动。如果重力方向与陀螺轴线不一致，钢球就落向低的一侧。由此产生的进动使旋转轴线重新垂直对正。

通常，电动姿态指示器陀螺可通过杠杆与凸轮机构手动锁定，以实现快速扶正。当仪表得不到正常工作所需的足够电力时，仪表表面右上方会出现一个断电旗标（off flag）。

### 陀螺方向指示器或方向陀螺（DG）

陀螺方向指示器或方向陀螺（directional gyro，DG）常是主要的航向仪表。由于磁罗盘摆动太大，与磁罗盘对准的陀螺能给出稳定得多的航向指示。陀螺方向指示器位于仪表板基本T形布局的中心底部。

真空驱动的DG在许多轻型飞机上很常见。其工作原理是陀螺在空间中的定轴性。陀螺转子在垂直面内旋转，并保持与其设定方向一致。飞机和仪表壳体绕着这个定轴陀螺运动，使与转子常平架联动的垂直罗盘刻度卡移动。刻度卡以度为单位标定，通常每隔30度标一个数字。仪表玻璃上一架固定的小飞机机头指示飞机的航向。

真空驱动方向指示器具有许多与姿态指示器相同的基本陀螺仪表问题。针对进动的内置补偿各不相同，通常还装有锁定装置。飞行中需要飞行员定期参照磁罗盘手动重新对准。

### 转弯协调器

许多飞机使用转弯协调器。转弯协调器中陀螺的转子向上倾斜30°。因此它不仅响应绕垂直轴的运动，也响应绕纵轴的滚转运动。这很有用，因为飞机必须滚转才能绕垂直轴转弯。因此，滚转的仪表指示是偏离直线平飞的最早预警。

转弯协调器的表盘上通常有一个小飞机符号。飞机的翼尖提供平飞状态和飞机转弯速率的指示。

### 转弯侧滑仪

转弯侧滑仪也可称为转弯倾斜仪或针球仪。无论如何，它显示飞机倾斜转弯时的正确操纵，并指示飞机绕垂直轴的运动（偏航）。大多数转弯侧滑仪位于仪表板基本T形布局中空速表的下方、方向指示器的左侧。

转弯侧滑仪实际上是装在同一仪表壳体内的两个独立装置：转弯指示指针和侧滑指示球。转弯指针由陀螺驱动，可用真空、空气压力或电力驱动。小球则完全是独立装置。它是一个圆形玛瑙球（或钢球），装在一根充有阻尼液的玻璃管内。它响应转弯中受到的重力和离心力而移动。陀螺式转弯侧滑仪可用电动或气动（真空或空气压力）驱动。

转弯指示器有多种形式，它们都指示飞机的转弯速率。每秒3度的转弯使飞机2分钟转360°。这被视为标准转弯。该速率可用指针左右两侧的标记表示，指针通常停在垂直位置。有时没有标记，则用指针的宽度作为标定依据。此时，指针偏离垂直方向一个指针宽度，就等于每秒3°的标准2分钟转弯速率。速度较快的飞机转弯往往更慢，其刻度或标注指示4分钟转弯。换言之，指针宽度或与刻度标记的对正表示飞机正以每秒1 1 ⁄ 2°转弯，4分钟完成360°转弯。通常会在仪表盘面上标注该仪表是2分钟还是4分钟转弯指示器。

转弯指针指示飞机绕其垂直轴转弯的速率。它利用陀螺的进动使指针倾斜来实现这一点。陀螺在与飞机纵轴对齐的垂直面内旋转。转弯中飞机绕其垂直轴转动时，旋转陀螺受到的力作用在垂直轴方向上。由于进动，陀螺转子的反作用在陀螺上沿自转方向再向前90°。这意味着绕垂直轴的作用力的反作用，是绕飞机纵轴的运动。这使转子顶部向左或向右倾斜。指针通过连杆连接，使指针向相反方向偏转，从而与转弯方向一致。于是，飞机绕垂直轴的转弯，在仪表上以绕纵轴的方式显示出来。飞行员看仪表时会觉得直观，因为指针指示的方向与转弯方向相同。

仪表的侧滑指示（小球）部分是一个倾角计。在协调的直线平飞中，小球只响应重力，因此停在参考线之间弧形玻璃管的最低处。开始转弯、飞机倾斜时，重力和转弯的离心力同时作用于小球。如果转弯是协调的，小球保持原位。若存在外侧滑转弯，离心力超过作用在小球上的重力，小球向转弯外侧移动。在内侧滑转弯中，坡度超过所需，重力大于作用在小球上的离心力，小球在弧形玻璃管内向转弯内侧移动。

如前所述，如果姿态指示器和方向指示器是真空驱动的，转弯侧滑仪陀螺的电源常常是电动的。这样在真空系统和发电机都失效时，还能靠电瓶供电维持有限工作。来自转弯侧滑仪的方向与姿态信息，加上空速管静压仪表的信息，使飞机能够继续安全应急飞行。

电动转弯侧滑仪通常为直流供电。真空驱动的转弯侧滑仪通常比全常平架的姿态和方向指示器使用更低的真空度（约2 "Hg）。无论如何，必须保持适当的真空度，才能显示准确的转弯速率信息。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 定语从句 + 省略主语的状语从句
**原句**：It gives the pilot pitch and roll information that is especially important when flying without outside visual references.
**拆解**：主句 It gives the pilot pitch and roll information 为双宾语结构（间接宾语 the pilot，直接宾语 information）；that is especially important 是定语从句修饰 information；when flying without outside visual references 为时间状语，从句省略了主语与 be 动词，还原为 when it is flying。
**要点**：when + 现在分词相当于 when it is flying，状语从句主语与主句一致时可这样省略；that 从句紧跟 information，不要误读成同时修饰 pitch and roll。without outside visual references 指没有外部目视参考（云中、夜间），这正是姿态信息最关键的场合。

### ② 分词短语作后置定语 + 被动语态
**原句**：A painted semisphere simulating the horizon, the sky, and the ground is attached to the gyro gimbals.
**拆解**：主语中心词 A painted semisphere，painted 为过去分词作前置定语；simulating the horizon, the sky, and the ground 为现在分词短语作后置定语，说明该半球所模拟的内容；谓语 is attached to 为被动语态，to the gyro gimbals 引出附着对象。
**要点**：主谓之间插入带三个并列宾语的现在分词短语，读到 is attached 才出现谓语，长句要先抓住 semisphere ... is attached 这条主干。simulating 的逻辑主语是 semisphere，故用主动的现在分词。

### ③ 时间状语从句 + 介词短语链
**原句**：After air engages the scalloped drive on the rotor, it flows from the instrument to the vacuum pump through four ports.
**拆解**：After 引导时间状语从句（air engages the scalloped drive on the rotor）；主句主语 it 指空气，谓语 flows，from the instrument to the vacuum pump 表路径，through four ports 表经由的四个端口。
**要点**：三个介词短语按“起点—终点—途径”排列，中文宜按动作顺序译成“从仪表经四个端口流向真空泵”。it 指代前面出现的 air，不是 the rotor。

### ④ 比较结构 more than
**原句**：When the gyro rotates out of plane, air tends to port out of one side more than another.
**拆解**：When 引导时间状语从句；主句 air tends to port out of one side，tend to do 表示倾向；more than another 为比较结构，比较两侧排气量的多少，another 后省略了 side。
**要点**：more than another 意为“比另一侧更多”，不是“多于另一个东西”，其中省略了 side。port out 在此为动词“排出”，与前文 ports（端口）同源，注意词性转换。

### ⑤ 形式主语 it + 主语从句
**原句**：It is still possible that the gyro may experience precession and need to be erected.
**拆解**：It 为形式主语，真正的主语是 that 引导的主语从句；从句中谓语为并列的 may experience 与 (may) need to be erected，第二个分句承前省略情态动词。
**要点**：It is possible that... 是典型的形式主语句型，that 从句不能直接提到句首，须由 it 占位。need to be erected 用不定式被动式，因为转子是“被扶正”的对象；并列谓语第二个分句省略 may，理解时要补回。

### ⑥ 条件状语从句 + 主系表
**原句**：If the pull of gravity is not aligned with the axis of the gyro, the balls fall to the low side.
**拆解**：If 引导真实条件状语从句，主语 the pull of gravity，系表结构 is not aligned with 表示“与……不对正”；主句 the balls fall to the low side，to the low side 为方向状语。
**要点**：be aligned with 是“与……对正、对齐”的固定搭配，在陀螺仪表中指尖轴与重力方向重合。条件句提出重力方向与陀螺轴线不一致的假设，主句用一般现在时，说明这是反复发生的物理过程而非一次性事件。
