# Fixed-Wing Aircraft

## 1. 原文

### Fuselage

The fuselage is the main structure or body of the fixed-wing aircraft. It provides space for cargo, controls, accessories, passengers, and other equipment. In single-engine aircraft, the fuselage houses the powerplant. In multiengine aircraft, the engines may be either in the fuselage, attached to the fuselage, or suspended from the wing structure. There are two general types of fuselage construction: truss and monocoque.

#### Truss-Type

A truss is a rigid framework made up of members, such as beams, struts, and bars to resist deformation by applied loads. The truss-framed fuselage is generally covered with fabric. The truss-type fuselage frame is usually constructed of steel tubing welded together in such a manner that all members of the truss can carry both tension and compression loads. In some aircraft, principally the light, single-engine models, truss fuselage frames may be constructed of aluminum alloy and may be riveted or bolted into one piece, with cross-bracing achieved by using solid rods or tubes.

#### Monocoque Type

The monocoque (single shell) fuselage relies largely on the strength of the skin or covering to carry the primary loads. The design may be divided into three classes:

Different portions of the same fuselage may belong to either of the three classes, but most modern aircraft are considered to be of semimonocoque type construction.

The true monocoque construction uses formers, frame assemblies, and bulkheads to give shape to the fuselage. The heaviest of these structural members are located at intervals to carry concentrated loads and at points where fittings are used to attach other units such as wings, powerplants, and stabilizers. Since no other bracing members are present, the skin must carry the primary stresses and keep the fuselage rigid. Thus, the biggest problem involved in monocoque construction is maintaining enough strength while keeping the weight within allowable limits.

#### Semimonocoque Type

To overcome the strength/weight problem of monocoque construction, a modification called semimonocoque construction was developed. It also consists of frame assemblies, bulkheads, and formers as used in the monocoque design but, additionally, the skin is reinforced by longitudinal members called longerons. Longerons usually extend across several frame members and help the skin support primary bending loads. They are typically made of aluminum alloy either of a single piece or a built-up construction. The longerons are supplemented by other longitudinal members called stringers.

Stringers are typically more numerous and lighter in weight than the longerons. They come in a variety of shapes and are usually made from single piece aluminum alloy extrusions or formed aluminum. Stringers have some rigidity but are chiefly used for giving shape and for attachment of the skin. Stringers and longerons together prevent tension and compression from bending the fuselage.

Other bracing between the longerons and stringers can also be used. Often referred to as web members, these additional support pieces may be installed vertically or diagonally. It must be noted that manufacturers use different nomenclature to describe structural members. For example, there is often little difference between some rings, frames, and formers. One manufacturer may call the same type of brace a ring or a frame. Manufacturer instructions and specifications for a specific aircraft are the best guides.

The semimonocoque fuselage is constructed primarily of alloys of aluminum and magnesium, although steel and titanium are sometimes found in areas of high temperatures. Individually, not one of the aforementioned components is strong enough to carry the loads imposed during flight and landing. But, when combined, those components form a strong, rigid framework. This is accomplished with gussets, rivets, nuts and bolts, screws, and even friction stir welding. A gusset is a type of connection bracket that adds strength.

To summarize, in semimonocoque fuselages, the strong, heavy longerons hold the bulkheads and formers, and these, in turn, hold the stringers, braces, web members, etc. All are designed to be attached together and to the skin to achieve the full-strength benefits of semimonocoque design. It is important to recognize that the metal skin or covering carries part of the load. The fuselage skin thickness can vary with the load carried and the stresses sustained at a particular location.

The advantages of the semimonocoque fuselage are many. The bulkheads, frames, stringers, and longerons facilitate the design and construction of a streamlined fuselage that is both rigid and strong. Spreading loads among these structures and the skin means no single piece is failure critical. This means that a semimonocoque fuselage, because of its stressed-skin construction, may withstand considerable damage and still be strong enough to hold together.

Fuselages are generally constructed in two or more sections. On small aircraft, they are generally made in two or three sections, while larger aircraft may be made up of as many as six sections or more before being assembled.

#### Reinforced Shell Type

The reinforced shell has the skin reinforced by a complete framework of structural members.

### Pressurization

Many aircraft are pressurized. This means that air is pumped into the cabin after takeoff and a difference in pressure between the air inside the cabin and the air outside the cabin is established. This differential is regulated and maintained. In this manner, enough oxygen is made available for passengers to breathe normally and move around the cabin without special equipment at high altitudes.

Pressurization causes significant stress on the fuselage structure and adds to the complexity of design. In addition to withstanding the difference in pressure between the air inside and outside the cabin, cycling from unpressurized to pressurized and back again on each flight causes metal fatigue. To deal with these impacts and the other stresses of flight, nearly all pressurized aircraft are semimonocoque in design. Pressurized fuselage structures undergo extensive periodic inspections to ensure that any damage is discovered and repaired. Repeated weakness or failure in an area of structure may require that section of the fuselage be modified or redesigned.

## 2. 中文翻译

### 机身

机身是固定翼飞机的主体结构或机体。它为货物、操纵装置、附件、乘客及其他设备提供空间。在单发飞机上，机身容纳动力装置。在多发飞机上，发动机可以位于机身内、装在机身上，或悬挂在机翼结构上。机身构造一般有两种类型：桁架式和硬壳式。

#### 桁架式

桁架是由梁、撑杆和杆件等构件组成的刚性框架，用以抵抗所加载荷引起的变形。桁架式机身通常覆以蒙布。桁架式机身框架通常用钢管焊接而成，焊接方式使桁架的所有构件都能承受拉伸和压缩载荷。在某些飞机上，主要是轻型单发机型，桁架机身框架可用铝合金制造，并可用铆接或螺栓连接成整体，其交叉支撑通过实心杆或管件实现。

#### 硬壳式

硬壳式（单壳式）机身主要依靠蒙皮或覆盖层的强度来承受主要载荷。该设计可分为三类：

同一机身的不同部位可能分属这三类中的任一类，但大多数现代飞机被认为采用的是半硬壳式构造。

真正的硬壳式构造用成形件、框架组件和隔框来赋予机身外形。这些结构件中最重的部件按一定间隔布置，以承受集中载荷，并布置在使用接头连接其他部件（如机翼、动力装置和安定面）的部位。由于没有其他支撑构件，蒙皮必须承受主要应力并使机身保持刚性。因此，硬壳式构造的最大问题在于：既要保持足够的强度，又要使重量在允许范围内。

#### 半硬壳式

为克服硬壳式构造的强度/重量问题，人们研发出一种称为半硬壳式的改良构造。它同样由硬壳式设计中所用的框架组件、隔框和成形件组成，但此外蒙皮还由称为纵梁的纵向构件加强。纵梁通常跨过若干框架构件，帮助蒙皮承受主要弯曲载荷。纵梁通常用铝合金制成，或为单件，或为组合结构。纵梁还由另一种称为桁条的纵向构件加以补充。

桁条通常比纵梁数量更多、重量更轻。它们有多种截面形状，通常由单件铝合金挤压型材或成形铝板制成。桁条具有一定刚性，但主要用于赋予外形和连接蒙皮。桁条与纵梁共同防止拉伸和压缩使机身弯曲。

纵梁与桁条之间还可采用其他支撑。这些附加支撑件常称为腹板构件，可垂直或斜向安装。必须注意，各制造商描述结构件所用的名称不尽相同。例如，某些环、框架和成形件之间往往差别很小。同一类支撑件，一家制造商可能称之为环，另一家则称之为框架。具体机型的制造商说明和规范才是最好的依据。

半硬壳机身主要由铝镁合金制成，在高温部位有时也用钢和钛。单独来看，上述构件中没有哪一个强得足以承受飞行和着陆期间所加的载荷。但组合起来，这些构件便构成坚固、刚性的框架。这是通过角片、铆钉、螺母和螺栓、螺钉乃至搅拌摩擦焊实现的。角片是一种增加强度的连接支架。

总之，在半硬壳机身中，坚固沉重的纵梁固定隔框和成形件，而隔框和成形件又依次固定桁条、支撑件、腹板构件等。所有这些都设计成彼此连接并连接到蒙皮上，以实现半硬壳设计的全部强度优势。重要的是要认识到，金属蒙皮或覆盖层承担一部分载荷。机身蒙皮厚度可随所承受的载荷和特定部位所承受的应力而变化。

半硬壳机身的优点很多。隔框、框架、桁条和纵梁使流线型机身的设计与制造既刚性强又强度高。把载荷分散到这些结构和蒙皮上，意味着没有哪一个零件是失效关键件。这意味着半硬壳机身因其应力蒙皮构造，可以承受相当大的损伤而仍足够坚固、保持完整。

机身一般由两个或多个段组成。在小型飞机上，机身一般分成两段或三段制造；而较大的飞机在装配前可能由多达六段甚至更多段组成。

#### 加强壳式

加强壳式的蒙皮由完整的结构件框架加强。

### 增压

许多飞机是增压的。这意味着起飞后向客舱泵入空气，在客舱内空气与客舱外空气之间形成压差。这一压差受到调节和维持。这样，在高空就有足够的氧气供乘客正常呼吸并在客舱内走动，而无需特殊设备。

增压会对机身结构造成显著应力，并增加设计的复杂性。除了要承受客舱内外空气的压差之外，每次飞行中从未增压到增压再恢复的循环还会引起金属疲劳。为应对这些影响以及飞行中的其他应力，几乎所有增压飞机都采用半硬壳式设计。增压机身结构要接受广泛的定期检查，以确保任何损伤都能被发现并修复。结构某部位反复出现薄弱或失效，可能需要对该段机身进行改装或重新设计。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 过去分词定语 + 目的不定式
**原句**：A truss is a rigid framework made up of members, such as beams, struts, and bars to resist deformation by applied loads.
**拆解**：主句 A truss is a rigid framework；made up of members 为过去分词短语作后置定语，说明框架由什么构成；such as... 列举 members 的具体种类；to resist deformation 为目的不定式，by applied loads 为方式状语，applied 为过去分词修饰 loads。
**要点**：be made up of 意为“由……组成”，这里省略 be 直接用过去分词作定语，相当于 which is made up of。to resist 的逻辑主语是 framework（或 truss），不是 members；applied loads 是“所施加的载荷”，不是“施加的载荷”这一动作。

### ② in such a manner that 引导结果状语从句
**原句**：The truss-type fuselage frame is usually constructed of steel tubing welded together in such a manner that all members of the truss can carry both tension and compression loads.
**拆解**：主句为被动 is constructed of steel tubing；welded together 为过去分词短语作后置定语修饰 tubing；in such a manner that... 为方式状语，that 引导结果状语从句，从句主语 all members of the truss，谓语 can carry，宾语 both tension and compression loads。
**要点**：such...that 在这里修饰 manner，整体表示“以这样的方式……以至于……”，that 从句说明焊接方式带来的结果，不是定语从句。welded 的逻辑主语是 tubing，所以用过去分词（管件是被焊接的）。

### ③ 并列地点状语 + where 定语从句
**原句**：The heaviest of these structural members are located at intervals to carry concentrated loads and at points where fittings are used to attach other units such as wings, powerplants, and stabilizers.
**拆解**：主语 The heaviest of these structural members，谓语 are located（被动）；and 连接两个并列地点状语 at intervals 与 at points...；to carry concentrated loads 为目的状语；where 引导定语从句修饰 points，从句谓语 are used（被动），to attach... 为目的状语。
**要点**：are located 后接两个 at 短语，不要漏看第二个 at points，它与 at intervals 并列而不是另起谓语。where 的先行词是 points，从句中 fittings 作主语，are used 是被动；such as 列举的是 other units。

### ④ 目的不定式前置 + 过去分词定语 + 被动
**原句**：To overcome the strength/weight problem of monocoque construction, a modification called semimonocoque construction was developed.
**拆解**：To overcome... 为目的状语置于句首；主句主语 a modification，called semimonocoque construction 为过去分词短语作后置定语；谓语 was developed 为被动语态。
**要点**：句首不定式作目的状语时，其逻辑主语应与主句主语一致，这里“克服问题”的其实是人/设计者，属于英语中常见的悬垂不定式，阅读时不必强行对应。called... 修饰 modification，说明这种改良的名称。

### ⑤ with 短语 + 并列过去分词后置定语
**原句**：The fuselage skin thickness can vary with the load carried and the stresses sustained at a particular location.
**拆解**：主语 The fuselage skin thickness，谓语 can vary；with 引导伴随/依据状语，宾语为并列的 the load 与 the stresses；carried 修饰 load，sustained at a particular location 修饰 stresses，均为过去分词后置定语。
**要点**：vary with 意为“随……而变化”，with 后是两个并列名词短语，各自带自己的分词定语：carried 只修饰 load，sustained 只修饰 stresses，不能把两个分词都看成修饰同一名词。

### ⑥ 介词短语 + 动名词主语
**原句**：In addition to withstanding the difference in pressure between the air inside and outside the cabin, cycling from unpressurized to pressurized and back again on each flight causes metal fatigue.
**拆解**：In addition to... 为介词短语作状语，to 是介词，后接动名词 withstanding；主句主语为动名词短语 cycling from... to... and back again，on each flight 为时间状语；谓语 causes，宾语 metal fatigue。
**要点**：in addition to 的 to 是介词，后面必须用动名词，不能接动词原形。主句主语是整段动名词短语而不是 pressure；cycling 在此是名词化的动名词，表示“循环”这一过程。
