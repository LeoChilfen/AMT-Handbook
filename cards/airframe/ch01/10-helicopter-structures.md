# Helicopter Structures

> 机体 Ch 01 · Helicopter Structures · 原文 PDF 第 65 页

## 1. 原文

The structures of the helicopter are designed to give the helicopter its unique flight characteristics. A simplified explanation of how a helicopter flies is that the rotors are rotating airfoils that provide lift similar to the way wings provide lift on a fixed-wing aircraft. Air flows faster over the curved upper surface of the rotors, causing a negative pressure and thus, lifting the aircraft. Changing the angle of attack of the rotating blades increases or decreases lift, respectively raising or lowering the helicopter. Tilting the rotor plane of rotation causes the aircraft to move horizontally. Figure 1-93 shows the major components of a typical helicopter.

### Airframe

The airframe, or fundamental structure, of a helicopter can be made of either metal or wood composite materials, or some combination of the two. Typically, a composite component consists of many layers of fiber-impregnated resins, bonded to form a smooth panel. Tubular and sheet metal substructures are usually made of aluminum, though stainless steel or titanium are sometimes used in areas subject to higher stress or heat. Airframe design encompasses engineering, aerodynamics, materials technology, and manufacturing methods to achieve favorable balances of performance, reliability, and cost.

### Fuselage

As with fixed-wing aircraft, helicopter fuselages and tail booms are often truss-type or semimonocoque structures of stress-skin design. Steel and aluminum tubing, formed aluminum, and aluminum skin are commonly used. Modern helicopter fuselage design includes an increasing utilization of advanced composites as well. Firewalls and engine decks are usually stainless steel. Helicopter fuselages vary widely from those with a truss frame, two seats, no doors, and a monocoque shell flight compartment to those with fully enclosed airplane-style cabins as found on larger twin-engine helicopters. The multidirectional nature of helicopter flight makes wide-range visibility from the flight deck essential. Large, formed polycarbonate, glass, or plexiglass windscreens are common.

### Landing Gear or Skids

As mentioned, a helicopter’s landing gear can be simply a set of tubular metal skids. Many helicopters do have landing gear with wheels, some retractable.

### Powerplant & Transmission

The two most common types of engine used in helicopters are the reciprocating engine and the turbine engine. Reciprocating engines, also called piston engines, are generally used in smaller helicopters. Most training helicopters use reciprocating engines because they are relatively simple and inexpensive to operate.

#### Turbine Engines

Turbine engines are more powerful and are used in a wide variety of helicopters. They produce a tremendous amount of power for their size but are generally more expensive to operate. The turbine engine used in helicopters operates differently than those used in airplane applications. In most applications, the exhaust outlets simply release expended gases and do not contribute to the forward motion of the helicopter. Because the airflow is not a straight line pass through as in jet engines and is not used for propulsion, the cooling effect of the air is limited. Approximately 75 percent of the incoming airflow is used to cool the engine.

The gas turbine engine mounted on most helicopters is made up of a compressor, combustion chamber, turbine, and accessory gearbox assembly. The compressor draws filtered air into the plenum chamber and compresses it. Common type filters are centrifugal swirl tubes where debris is ejected outward and blown overboard prior to entering the compressor, or engine barrier filters (EBF), a paper element type filter, encased in a frame with a screen/grill over the inlet, and usually coated with an oil. This design significantly reduces the ingestion of foreign object debris (FOD). The compressed air is directed to the combustion section through discharge tubes where atomized fuel is injected into it. The air-fuel mixture is ignited and allowed to expand. This combustion gas is then forced through a series of turbine wheels causing them to turn. These turbine wheels provide power to both the engine compressor and the accessory gearbox. Depending on model and manufacturer, the rpm range can vary from a range low of 20,000 to a range high of 51,600.

Power is provided to the main rotor and tail rotor systems through the freewheeling unit which is attached to the accessory gearbox power output gear shaft. The combustion gas is finally expelled through an exhaust outlet. The temperature of gas is measured at different locations and is referenced differently by each manufacturer. Some common terms are: inter-turbine temperature (ITT), exhaust gas temperature (EGT), or turbine outlet temperature (TOT). TOT is used throughout this discussion for simplicity purposes.

#### Transmission

The transmission system transfers power from the engine to the main rotor, tail rotor, and other accessories during normal flight conditions. The main components of the transmission system are the main rotor transmission, tail rotor drive system, clutch, and freewheeling unit. The freewheeling unit, or autorotative clutch, allows the main rotor transmission to drive the tail rotor drive shaft during autorotation. Helicopter transmissions are normally lubricated and cooled with their own oil supply. A sight gauge is provided to check the oil level. Some transmissions have chip detectors located in the sump. These detectors are wired to warning lights located on the pilot’s instrument panel that illuminate in the event of an internal problem. Some chip detectors on modern helicopters have a “burn off” capability and attempt to correct the situation without pilot action. If the problem cannot be corrected on its own, the pilot must refer to the emergency procedures for that particular helicopter.

### Main Rotor System

The rotor system is the rotating part of a helicopter which generates lift. The rotor consists of a mast, hub, and rotor blades. The mast is a cylindrical metal shaft that extends upwards from and is driven, and sometimes supported, by the transmission. At the top of the mast is the attachment point for the rotor blades called the hub. The rotor blades are then attached to the hub by any number of different methods. Main rotor systems are classified according to how the main rotor blades are attached and move relative to the main rotor hub. There are three basic classifications: rigid, semirigid, or fully articulated.

#### Rigid Rotor System

The simplest is the rigid rotor system. In this system, the rotor blades are rigidly attached to the main rotor hub and are not free to slide back and forth (drag) or move up and down (flap). The forces tending to make the rotor blades do so are absorbed by the flexible properties of the blade. The pitch of the blades, however, can be adjusted by rotation about the spanwise axis via the feathering hinges.

#### Semirigid Rotor System

The semirigid rotor system in Figure 1-96 makes use of a teetering hinge at the blade attach point. While held in check from sliding back and forth, the teetering hinge does allow the blades to flap up and down. With this hinge, when one blade flaps up, the other flaps down.

Flapping is caused by a phenomenon known as dissymmetry of lift. As the plane of rotation of the rotor blades is tilted and the helicopter begins to move forward, an advancing blade and a retreating blade become established (on two-bladed systems). The relative windspeed is greater on an advancing blade than it is on a retreating blade. This causes greater lift to be developed on the advancing blade, causing it to rise up or flap. When blade rotation reaches the point where the blade becomes the retreating blade, the extra lift is lost and the blade flaps downward.

### Fully Articulated Rotor System

Fully articulated rotor blade systems provide hinges that allow the rotors to move fore and aft, as well as up and down. This lead-lag, drag, or hunting movement as it is called is in response to the Coriolis effect during rotational speed changes. When first starting to spin, the blades lag until centrifugal force is fully developed. Once rotating, a reduction in speed causes the blades to lead the main rotor hub until forces come into balance. Constant fluctuations in rotor blade speeds cause the blades to “hunt.” They are free to do so in a fully articulating system due to being mounted on the vertical drag hinge.

One or more horizontal hinges provide for flapping on a fully articulated rotor system. Also, the feathering hinge allows blade pitch changes by permitting rotation about the spanwise axis. Various dampers and stops can be found on different designs to reduce shock and limit travel in certain directions. Figure 1-98 shows a fully articulated main rotor system with the features discussed.

Numerous designs and variations on the three types of main rotor systems exist. Engineers continually search for ways to reduce vibration and noise caused by the rotating parts of the helicopter. Toward that end, the use of elastomeric bearings in main rotor systems is increasing. These polymer bearings have the ability to deform and return to their original shape. As such, they can absorb vibration that would normally be transferred by steel bearings. They also do not require regular lubrication, which reduces maintenance.

Some modern helicopter main rotors have been designed with flextures. These are hubs and hub components that are made out of advanced composite materials. They are designed to take up the forces of blade hunting and dissymmetry of lift by flexing. As such, many hinges and bearings can be eliminated from the traditional main rotor system. The result is a simpler rotor mast with lower maintenance due to fewer moving parts. Often, designs using flextures incorporate elastomeric bearings.

### Antitorque System

Ordinarily, helicopters have between two and seven main rotor blades. These rotors are usually made of a composite structure. The large rotating mass of the main rotor blades of a helicopter produce torque. This torque increases with engine power and tries to spin the fuselage in the opposite direction. The tail boom and tail rotor, or antitorque rotor, counteract this torque effect. Controlled with foot pedals, the countertorque of the tail rotor must be modulated as engine power levels are changed. This is done by changing the pitch of the tail rotor blades. This, in turn, changes the amount of countertorque, and the aircraft can be rotated about its vertical axis, allowing the pilot to control the direction the helicopter is facing.

Similar to a vertical stabilizer on the empennage of an airplane, a fin or pylon is also a common feature on rotorcraft. Normally, it supports the tail rotor assembly, although some tail rotors are mounted on the tail cone of the boom. Additionally, a horizontal member called a stabilizer is often constructed at the tail cone or on the pylon.

A Fenestron® is a unique tail rotor design which is actually a multiblade ducted fan mounted in the vertical pylon. It works the same way as an ordinary tail rotor, providing sideways thrust to counter the torque produced by the main rotors. A NOTAR® antitorque system has no visible rotor mounted on the tail boom. Instead, an engine-driven adjustable fan is located inside the tail boom. NOTAR ® is an acronym that stands for “no tail rotor.” As the speed of the main rotor changes, the speed of the NOTAR ® fan changes. Air is vented out of two long slots on the right side of the tail boom, entraining main rotor wash to hug the right side of the tail boom, in turn causing laminar flow and a low pressure (Coanda Effect). This low pressure causes a force counter to the torque produced by the main rotor. Additionally, the remainder of the air from the fan is sent through the tail boom to a vent on the aft left side of the boom where it is expelled. This action to the left causes an opposite reaction to the right, which is the direction needed to counter the main rotor torque.

### Controls

The controls of a helicopter differ slightly from those found in an aircraft. The collective, operated by the pilot with the left hand, is pulled up or pushed down to increase or decrease the angle of attack on all of the rotor blades simultaneously. This increases or decreases lift and moves the aircraft up or down. The engine throttle control is located on the hand grip at the end of the collective. The cyclic is the control “stick” located between the pilot’s legs. It can be moved in any direction to tilt the plane of rotation of the rotor blades. This causes the helicopter to move in the direction that the cyclic is moved. As stated, the foot pedals control the pitch of the tail rotor blades thereby balancing main rotor torque. ## 2. 中文翻译

直升机的结构旨在赋予其独特的飞行特性。对直升机如何飞行的一种简化解释是：旋翼是旋转的翼面，其产生升力的方式类似于固定翼飞机上机翼产生升力的方式。气流较快地流过旋翼弯曲的上表面，形成负压，从而把飞机升起来。改变旋转桨叶的迎角，就能增大或减小升力，使直升机相应上升或下降。使旋翼的旋转平面倾斜，则使飞机水平移动。图 1-93 示出了一架典型直升机的主要部件。

### 机体

直升机的机体（airframe），即基本结构，可用金属或木质复合材料制成，也可由两者以某种方式组合而成。通常，复合材料部件由多层浸渍纤维的树脂粘合而成，形成光滑的板件。管材和钣金件构成的子结构通常用铝制造，不过在承受较高应力或较高热量的部位，有时也使用不锈钢或钛。机体设计涵盖工程、空气动力学、材料工艺和制造方法，以在性能、可靠性和成本之间取得良好平衡。

### 机身

与固定翼飞机一样，直升机的机身和尾梁通常是桁架式或半硬壳式的应力蒙皮结构。常用材料有钢管和铝管、成形铝材以及铝蒙皮。现代直升机机身设计还越来越多地采用先进复合材料。防火墙和发动机舱顶板通常用不锈钢。直升机机身的差异很大：有的采用桁架框架、两个座位、无舱门，驾驶舱为硬壳式壳体；有的则具有完全封闭的飞机式客舱，如较大的双发直升机。直升机飞行的多方向性，使驾驶舱必须具备大范围视野。大型的成形聚碳酸酯、玻璃或有机玻璃风挡十分常见。

### 起落架或滑橇

如前所述，直升机的起落架可以简单到只是一组管状金属滑橇。许多直升机确实装有带机轮的起落架，其中一些可收放。

### 动力装置与传动系统

直升机上最常见的两类发动机是往复式发动机和涡轮发动机。往复式发动机又称活塞发动机，一般用于较小的直升机。大多数训练直升机采用往复式发动机，因为其操作相对简单且成本较低。

#### 涡轮发动机

涡轮发动机功率更大，广泛用于各种直升机。就其尺寸而言，它能产生巨大的功率，但运行成本通常较高。直升机所用涡轮发动机的工作方式与飞机上的不同。在大多数应用中，排气口只是排出已膨胀做功的燃气，并不为直升机的前飞提供推力。由于气流不像喷气发动机那样呈直线穿过，也不用于推进，空气的冷却效果有限。进气流量的约 75% 用于冷却发动机。

大多数直升机上安装的燃气涡轮发动机由压气机、燃烧室、涡轮和附件齿轮箱组件构成。压气机把经过滤的空气吸入集气室并加以压缩。常见的滤型有离心旋流管式——碎屑在进入压气机之前被甩向外侧并排到机外；以及发动机阻隔式滤（EBF）——一种纸质滤芯，装在带滤网/格栅的进气口框架内，通常涂有油。这种设计显著减少了外来物碎屑（FOD）的吸入。压缩空气经排气支管被导入燃烧段，在那里向其中喷入雾化燃油。油气混合气被点燃并膨胀。这些燃气随后被强制通过一系列涡轮轮盘，使其转动。这些涡轮轮盘为发动机压气机和附件齿轮箱提供动力。视型号和制造商不同，转速范围可从低端 20,000 转/分到高端 51,600 转/分。

动力通过自由轮组件传递给主旋翼和尾桨系统，该组件连接在附件齿轮箱的动力输出齿轮轴上。燃气最终经排气口排出。燃气温度在不同位置测量，各制造商的称谓也不相同。常见的术语有：涡轮间温度（ITT）、排气温度（EGT）或涡轮出口温度（TOT）。为简便起见，本讨论中统一使用 TOT。

#### 传动系统

在正常飞行条件下，传动系统把发动机的功率传递给主旋翼、尾桨及其他附件。传动系统的主要部件有主旋翼传动装置、尾桨驱动系统、离合器和自由轮组件。自由轮组件又称自转离合器，在自转状态下允许主旋翼传动装置驱动尾桨传动轴。直升机传动装置通常有各自的滑油供应进行润滑和冷却。设有观察窗以检查油位。有些传动装置在油池中装有金属屑检测器。这些检测器连接到飞行员仪表板上的警告灯，一旦内部出现问题，警告灯即点亮。现代直升机上的一些金属屑检测器具有“烧除”能力，可在飞行员不介入的情况下尝试纠正该状况。如果问题无法自行纠正，飞行员必须查阅该型直升机的应急程序。

### 主旋翼系统

旋翼系统是直升机产生升力的旋转部分。旋翼由桅柱、桨毂和旋翼桨叶组成。桅柱是一根圆柱形金属轴，自传动装置向上伸出，由传动装置驱动，有时也由它支撑。桅柱顶部是旋翼桨叶的安装点，称为桨毂。旋翼桨叶再以多种不同方法之一连接到桨毂上。主旋翼系统按主旋翼桨叶相对主旋翼桨毂的连接方式和运动方式分类。基本分为三类：刚性、半刚性和全铰接式。

#### 刚性旋翼系统

最简单的是刚性旋翼系统。在该系统中，旋翼桨叶刚性连接到主旋翼桨毂上，不能前后滑动（摆振），也不能上下运动（挥舞）。力图使桨叶产生这些运动的力，由桨叶本身的弹性特性吸收。不过，桨叶的桨距可通过变距铰绕展向轴转动来调节。

#### 半刚性旋翼系统

图 1-96 中的半刚性旋翼系统在桨叶连接点采用跷跷板式铰。跷跷板铰虽被限制不能前后滑动，却允许桨叶上下挥舞。有了这个铰，一片桨叶向上挥舞时，另一片就向下挥舞。

挥舞是由称为升力不对称（dissymmetry of lift）的现象引起的。当旋翼桨叶的旋转平面倾斜、直升机开始前飞时（在双桨叶系统上），便形成前进桨叶和后行桨叶。前进桨叶上的相对风速大于后行桨叶。这使前进桨叶产生更大的升力，使其向上抬起，即挥舞。当桨叶转到成为后行桨叶的位置时，多余的升力消失，桨叶便向下挥舞。

### 全铰接式旋翼系统

全铰接式旋翼桨叶系统设有铰，允许旋翼前后运动，也允许上下运动。这种所谓的前后摆振、摆振或“追逐”运动，是旋转速度变化时对科里奥利效应的响应。刚开始旋转时，桨叶滞后，直到离心力充分建立。一旦进入旋转，转速降低会使桨叶超前于主旋翼桨毂，直到各力重新平衡。旋翼桨叶转速的不断波动使桨叶“追逐”。在全铰接系统中，由于桨叶安装在垂直的摆振铰上，它们可以自由地这样做。

在全铰接式旋翼系统上，一个或多个水平铰提供挥舞运动。此外，变距铰允许桨叶绕展向轴转动，从而改变桨距。不同设计上可见各种减摆器和限动块，用于减小冲击并限制某些方向上的行程。图 1-98 示出了具有上述特征的全铰接式主旋翼系统。

三类主旋翼系统存在众多设计和变型。工程师们不断寻求减少直升机旋转部件所引起的振动和噪声的方法。为此，主旋翼系统中弹性体轴承的使用日益增多。这类聚合物轴承能够变形并恢复原状。因此，它们可以吸收通常由钢制轴承传递的振动。它们也不需要定期润滑，从而减少了维修工作。

一些现代直升机主旋翼设计采用了柔性件（flextures）。这些是用先进复合材料制成的桨毂及桨毂部件。它们被设计成通过弯曲来承受桨叶追逐的力和升力不对称。因此，传统主旋翼系统中的许多铰和轴承都可以取消。其结果是旋翼桅柱更简单，运动部件更少，维修工作量更低。采用柔性件的设计往往同时使用弹性体轴承。

### 反扭矩系统

通常，直升机有两到七片主旋翼桨叶。这些旋翼一般由复合材料结构制成。直升机主旋翼桨叶巨大的旋转质量会产生扭矩。该扭矩随发动机功率增大而增大，并力图使机身向相反方向旋转。尾梁和尾桨（即反扭矩旋翼）抵消这一扭矩效应。尾桨的反扭矩由脚蹬控制，必须随发动机功率水平的变化而调节。这是通过改变尾桨桨叶的桨距实现的。这又改变了反扭矩的大小，飞机便可绕其垂直轴转动，使飞行员能够控制直升机的朝向。

与飞机尾翼上的垂直安定面类似，垂尾或尾梁支柱也是旋翼机上的常见特征。它通常支撑尾桨组件，不过有些尾桨安装在尾梁的尾锥上。此外，尾锥或支柱上常造有一个称为安定面的水平构件。

Fenestron® 是一种独特的尾桨设计，实际上是安装在垂直支柱内的多叶涵道风扇。它的工作原理与普通尾桨相同，提供侧向推力以抵消主旋翼产生的扭矩。NOTAR® 反扭矩系统在尾梁上没有可见的旋翼。取而代之的是，尾梁内部装有一个由发动机驱动的可调风扇。NOTAR® 是“无尾桨”（no tail rotor）的首字母缩略词。随着主旋翼转速变化，NOTAR® 风扇的转速也随之变化。空气从尾梁右侧两条长缝排出，卷吸主旋翼下洗气流贴附尾梁右侧，进而形成层流和低压（柯恩达效应）。这一低压产生一个与主旋翼扭矩方向相反的力。此外，风扇其余的空气经尾梁送至尾梁左后侧的一个排气口排出。向左排出的这一作用产生向右的反作用力，而这正是抵消主旋翼扭矩所需的方向。

### 操纵机构

直升机的操纵机构与飞机上的略有不同。总距杆由飞行员左手操纵，向上拉起或向下推入，可同时增大或减小所有旋翼桨叶的迎角。这增大或减小升力，使飞机上升或下降。发动机油门控制装在总距杆末端的手柄上。驾驶杆是位于飞行员两腿之间的操纵“杆”。它可以向任意方向移动，使旋翼桨叶的旋转平面倾斜。这使直升机朝驾驶杆移动的方向飞行。如前所述，脚蹬控制尾桨桨叶的桨距，从而平衡主旋翼扭矩。图 1-103 和 1-104 示出了典型直升机上的操纵机构。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 被动语态 + 不定式作目的状语
**原句**：The structures of the helicopter are designed to give the helicopter its unique flight characteristics.
**拆解**：主语 The structures of the helicopter，谓语 are designed 为被动语态；不定式短语 to give the helicopter its unique flight characteristics 作目的状语，其中 give 带双宾语（the helicopter 与 its unique flight characteristics）。
**要点**：be designed to do 意为“被设计用来做”，to do 说明设计目的而非结果。give sb. sth. 的双宾语结构在被动语态中若以直接宾语作主语，需补介词 to，这里未变被动，保持原双宾语即可。

### ② 表语从句 + 定语从句 + the way 引导方式
**原句**：A simplified explanation of how a helicopter flies is that the rotors are rotating airfoils that provide lift similar to the way wings provide lift on a fixed-wing aircraft.
**拆解**：主语 A simplified explanation + of how a helicopter flies（介词后接宾语从句）；系动词 is 后接 that 引导的表语从句，从句主语 the rotors，表语 rotating airfoils，其后再接 that 引导的定语从句；similar to the way... 为形容词短语作状语，the way 后直接跟从句，相当于 the way in which。
**要点**：句中出现两个 that：第一个 that 引导表语从句，不能省略；第二个 that 引导定语从句，修饰 airfoils。the way 引导方式状语从句时可省略 in which，不要误认为 the way 后面缺成分。

### ③ 现在分词短语作结果状语
**原句**：Air flows faster over the curved upper surface of the rotors, causing a negative pressure and thus, lifting the aircraft.
**拆解**：主句 Air flows faster over the curved upper surface of the rotors 为一般现在时；逗号后 causing... 与 lifting... 为并列的现在分词短语，作结果状语，thus 为连接副词，表示“因而”。
**要点**：分词作结果状语时，其逻辑主语就是主句主语 Air，且动作是主句动作自然引出的结果，不是同时发生的伴随动作。causing 与 lifting 共用逗号后的并列结构，thus 后又用逗号断开，朗读时注意停顿位置。

### ④ 让步状语从句的省略 + do 强调
**原句**：While held in check from sliding back and forth, the teetering hinge does allow the blades to flap up and down.
**拆解**：While held in check... 是让步状语从句 While it is held in check... 的省略形式，省略了主语 it 与 be 动词；主句主语 the teetering hinge，does 为强调助动词，谓语 allow，宾语 the blades，宾补 to flap up and down。
**要点**：状语从句主语与主句主语一致且含 be 动词时，可同时省略主语和 be。does allow 中的 does 不是实义动词，而是强调“确实允许”，与后面的 allow 构成“助动词 + 动词原形”。held in check from doing 意为“被约束住不能做”。

### ⑤ 插入语 as it is called
**原句**：This lead-lag, drag, or hunting movement as it is called is in response to the Coriolis effect during rotational speed changes.
**拆解**：主语中心词 movement 前有三个并列定语 lead-lag、drag、hunting；as it is called 为插入语，插在主语与谓语之间，其中 as 作补足语，it 指代前面的 movement；谓语 is in response to，宾语 the Coriolis effect，during... 为时间状语。
**要点**：插入语用两个逗号与句子其他成分隔开，去掉后句子仍完整：This movement is in response to...。it 指代 movement，不是形式主语。in response to 意为“作为对……的响应”，比 respond to 更书面。

### ⑥ 过去分词短语作状语 + as 引导时间从句
**原句**：Controlled with foot pedals, the countertorque of the tail rotor must be modulated as engine power levels are changed.
**拆解**：Controlled with foot pedals 为过去分词短语作方式/条件状语，逻辑主语是主句主语 the countertorque；主句谓语 must be modulated 为带情态动词的被动语态；as 引导时间状语从句，从句为被动语态 are changed。
**要点**：过去分词作状语时，主句主语是它的逻辑宾语（countertorque 被 control），所以用过去分词而非现在分词。as 在此表示“随着”，强调两种变化同步发生，不要译成“因为”。
