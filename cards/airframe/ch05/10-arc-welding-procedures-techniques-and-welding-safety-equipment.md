# Arc Welding Procedures, Techniques, & Welding Safety Equipment

## 1. 原文

Arc welding, also referred to as stick welding, has been performed successfully on almost all types of metals. This section addresses the procedures as they may apply to fusion welding of steel plate and provides the basic steps and procedures required to produce an acceptable arc weld. Additional instruction and information pertaining to arc welding of other metals can be obtained from training institutions and the various manufacturers of the welding equipment.

The first step in preparing to arc weld is to make certain that the necessary equipment is available and that the welding machine is properly connected and in good working order. Particular attention should be given to the ground connection, since a poor connection results in a fluctuating arc, that is difficult to control.

When using a shielded electrode, the bare end of the electrode should be clamped in its holder at a 90° angle to the jaws. (Some holders allow the electrode to be inserted at a 45° angle when needed for various welding positions.)

Before starting to weld, the following typical list of items should be checked:

• Is the proper personal safety equipment being used, including a welding helmet, welding gloves, protective clothing, and footwear; if not, in an adequately ventilated area, appropriate breathing equipment?

• Has the ground connection been properly made to the work piece and is it making a good connection?

• Has the proper type and size electrode been selected for the job?

• Is the electrode properly secured in the holder?

• Does the polarity of the machine coincide with that of the electrode?

• Is the machine in good working order and is it adjusted to provide the necessary current for the job?

The welding arc is established by touching the base metal plate with the electrode and immediately withdrawing it a short distance. At the instant the electrode touches the plate, a rush of current flows through the point of contact. As the electrode is withdrawn, an electric arc is formed, melting a spot on the plate and at the end of the electrode.

Correctly striking an arc takes practice. The main difficulty in confronting a beginner in striking the arc is sticking the electrode to the work. If the electrode is not withdrawn promptly upon contact with the metal, the high amperage flows through the electrode causing it to stick or freeze to the plate and practically short circuits the welding machine. A quick roll of the wrist, either right or left, usually breaks the electrode loose from the work piece. If that does not work, quickly unclamp the holder from the electrode, and turn off the machine. A small chisel and hammer frees the electrode from the metal so it can be regripped in the holder. The welding machine can then be turned back on.

There are two essentially similar methods of striking the arc. One is the touch or tapping method. When using this method, the electrode should be held in a vertical position and lowered until it is an inch or so above the point where the arc is to be struck. Then, the electrode is lightly tapped on the work piece and immediately lifted to form an arc approximately ¼-inch in length.

The second (and usually easier to master) is a scratch or sweeping method. To strike the arc by the scratch method, the electrode is held just above the plate at an angle of 20°–25°. The arc should be struck by sweeping the electrode with a wrist motion and lightly scratching the plate. The electrode is then lifted immediately to form an arc.

Either method takes some practice, but with time and experience, it becomes easy. The key is to raise the electrode quickly, but only about ¼-inch from the base or the arc is lost. If it is raised too slowly, the electrode sticks to the plate.

To form a uniform bead, the electrode must be moved along the plate at a constant speed in addition to the downward feed of the electrode. If the rate of advance is too slow, a wide overlapping bead forms with no fusion at the edges. If the rate is too fast, the bead is too narrow and has little or no fusion at the plate.

The proper length of the arc cannot be judged by looking at it. Instead, depend on the sound that the short arc makes. This is a sharp cracking sound, and it should be heard during the time the arc is being moved down to and along the surface of the plate.

A good weld bead on a flat plate should have the following characteristics:

• Little or no splatter on the surface of the plate.

• An arc crater in the bead of approximately ⁄ 16 -inch when the arc has been broken.

• The bead should be built up slightly, without metal overlap at the top surface.

• The bead should have a good penetration of approximately ⁄ 16 -inch into the base metal.

When advancing the electrode, it should be held at an angle of about 20° to 25° in the direction of travel moving away from the finished bead.

If the arc is broken during the welding of a bead and the electrode is removed quickly, a crater is formed at the point where the arc ends. This shows the depth of penetration or fusion that the weld is getting. The crater is formed by the pressure of the gases from the electrode tip forcing the weld metal toward the edges of the crater. If the electrode is removed slowly, the crater is filled.

If you need to restart an arc of an interrupted bead, start just ahead of the crater of the previous weld bead, as shown in position 1, Figure 5-33 . Then, the electrode should be returned to the back edge of the crater (step 2). From this point, the weld may be continued by welding right through the crater and down the line of weld as originally planned (step 3).

Once a bead has been formed, every particle of slag must be removed from the area of the crater before restarting the arc. This is accomplished with a pick hammer and wire brush and prevents the slag from becoming trapped in the weld.

### Multiple Pass Welding

Groove and fillet welds in heavy metals often require the deposit of a number of beads to complete a weld. It is important that the beads be deposited in a predetermined sequence to produce the soundest welds with the best proportions. The number of beads is determined by the thickness of the metal being welded.

Plates from ⁄ 8 -inch to ¼-inch can be welded in one pass, but they should be tacked at intervals to keep them aligned. Any weld on a plate thicker than ¼-inch should have the edges beveled and multiple passes.

The sequence of the bead deposits is determined by the kind of joint and the position of the metal. All slag must be removed from each bead before another bead is deposited. Typical multiple-pass groove welding of butt joints is shown in Figure 5-34 .

### Techniques of Position Welding

Each time the position of a welded joint or the type of joint is changed, it may be necessary to change any one or a combination of the following: • Current value • Electrode • Polarity • Arc length • Welding technique Current values are determined by the electrode size, as well as the welding position. Electrode size is governed by the thickness of the metal and the joint preparation. The electrode type is determined by the welding position. Manufacturers specify the polarity to be used with each electrode. Arc length is controlled by a combination of the electrode size, welding position, and welding current.

Since it is impractical to cite every possible variation occasioned by different welding conditions, only the information necessary for the commonly used positions and welds is discussed here.

### Flat Position Welding

There are four types of welds commonly used in flat position welding: bead, groove, fillet, and lap joint. Each type is discussed separately in the following paragraphs.

#### Bead Weld

The bead weld utilizes the same technique that is used when depositing a bead on a flat metal surface. The only difference is that the deposited bead is at the butt joint of two steel plates, fusing them together. Square butt joints may be welded in one or multiple passes. If the thickness of the metal is such that complete fusion cannot be obtained by welding from one side, the joint must be welded from both sides. Most joints should first be tack-welded to ensure alignment and reduce warping.

#### Groove Weld

Groove welding may be performed on a butt joint or an outside corner joint. Groove welds are made on butt joints where the metal to be welded is ¼-inch or more in thickness. The butt joint can be prepared using either a single or double groove depending on the thickness of the plate. The number of passes required to complete a weld is determined by the thickness of the metal being welded and the size of the electrode being used.

Any groove weld made in more than one pass must have the slag, spatter, and oxide carefully removed from all previous weld deposits before welding over them. Some of the common types of groove welds performed on butt joints in the flat position are shown in Figure 5-36.

#### Fillet Weld

Fillet welds are used to make tee and lap joints. The electrode should be held at an angle of 45° to the plate surface. The electrode should be tilted at an angle of about 15° in the direction of welding. Thin plates should be welded with little or no weaving motion of the electrode and the weld is made in one pass. Fillet welding of thicker plates may require two or more passes using a semicircular weaving motion of the electrode.

#### Lap Joint Weld

The procedure for making fillet weld in a lap joint is similar to that used in the tee joint. The electrode is held at about a 30° angle to the vertical and tilted to an angle of about 15° in the direction of welding when joining plates of the same thickness.

### Vertical Position Welding

Vertical positing welding includes any weld applied to a surface inclined more than 45° from the horizontal. Welding in the vertical position is more difficult than welding in the flat position because of the force of gravity. The molten metal has the tendency to run down. To control the flow of molten metal, the voltage and current adjustments of the welding machine must be correct.

The current setting, or amperage, is less for welding in the vertical position than for welding in the flat position for similar size electrodes. Additionally, the current used for welding upward should be set slightly higher than the current used for welding downward on the same work piece. When welding up, hold the electrode 90° to the vertical, and weld moving the bead upward. Focus on welding the sides of the joint and the middle takes care of itself. In welding downward, with the hand below the arc and the electrode tilted about 15° upward, the weld should move downward.

### Overhead Position Welding

Overhead position welding is one of the most difficult in welding since a very short arc must be constantly maintained to control the molten metal. The force of gravity tends to cause the molten metal to drop down or sag from the plate, so it is important that protective clothing and head gear be worn at all times when performing overhead welding.

For bead welds in an overhead position, the electrode should be held at an angle of 90° to the base metal. In some cases where it is desirable to observe the arc and the crater of the weld, the electrode may be held at an angle of 15° in the direction of welding.

When making fillet welds on overhead tee or lap joints, a short arc should be held, and there should be no weaving of the electrode. The arc motion should be controlled to secure good penetration to the root of the weld and good fusion to the plates. If the molten metal becomes too fluid and tends to sag, the electrode should be whipped away quickly from the center ahead of the weld to lengthen the arc and allow the metal to solidify. The electrode should then be returned immediately to the crater of the weld and the welding continued.

Anyone learning or engaged in arc welding should always have a good view of the weld puddle. Otherwise there is no way to ensure that the welding is in the joint and keeping the arc on the leading edge of the puddle. For the best view, the welder should keep their head off to the side and out of the fumes so they can see the puddle.

## 2. 中文翻译

电弧焊又称焊条电弧焊（stick welding），已成功用于几乎各类金属。本节讲述可适用于钢板熔焊的操作程序，并给出做出合格电弧焊缝所需的基本步骤与程序。有关其他金属电弧焊的补充说明与资料，可从培训机构及各焊接设备制造商处获得。

准备电弧焊的第一步，是确认所需设备齐备、焊机连接正确且处于良好工作状态。应特别注意接地连接，因为连接不良会导致电弧波动、难以控制。

使用药皮焊条时，应把焊条裸露的一端夹在焊钳中，并与钳口成90°角。（有些焊钳允许在需要适应各种焊接位置时，以45°角插入焊条。）

开始焊接前，应检查下列典型项目：

• 是否使用了合适的个人安全防护装备，包括焊接面罩、焊工手套、防护服和防护鞋；若没有，在通风良好的区域，是否配备了适当的呼吸防护装备？

• 接地线是否已正确接到工件上，且接触良好？

• 是否已针对该作业选好正确型号和规格的焊条？

• 焊条是否已牢固地装在焊钳中？

• 焊机的极性是否与焊条的要求一致？

• 焊机是否处于良好工作状态，并已调整到能提供作业所需电流？

电弧的引燃方法是：用焊条触碰基材板，随即迅速提起一小段距离。焊条接触板面的瞬间，一股电流涌过接触点。随着焊条提起，形成电弧，在板面和焊条端部各熔出一个小坑。

正确地引弧需要练习。初学者引弧时的主要困难是焊条粘在工件上。如果焊条接触金属后没有及时提起，大电流会流过焊条，使其粘住或“冻结”在板面上，实际上把焊机短路。手腕快速向左或向右一翻，通常能使焊条从工件上脱开。若仍不行，应迅速松开焊钳、取下焊条，并关闭焊机。用小凿子和锤子把焊条从金属上敲下来，便可重新夹入焊钳。然后可重新开机。

引弧有两种基本相似的方法。一种是接触法，或称敲击法。使用此法时，焊条应保持竖直，下降到距拟引弧点上方约一英寸处；然后轻敲工件，并立即提起，形成约1/4英寸长的电弧。

第二种（通常也更容易掌握）是摩擦法，或称划擦法。用划擦法引弧时，焊条以20°–25°的角度保持在板面正上方，靠手腕动作扫过焊条、轻轻划擦板面来引弧，然后立即提起焊条形成电弧。

两种方法都要练一练，但随着时间的推移和经验的积累，就会变得容易。关键在于迅速提起焊条，但只提离基材约1/4英寸，否则电弧就会熄灭。若提得太慢，焊条就会粘在板上。

要形成均匀的焊道，除了向下送进焊条之外，还必须以恒定速度沿板面移动焊条。若前进速度太慢，会形成宽而重叠的焊道，边缘没有熔合；若速度太快，焊道太窄，与板材几乎没有熔合。

电弧长度是否合适，不能靠眼睛判断，而要靠短弧发出的声音来判断。这是一种尖锐的噼啪声，在电弧沿板面向下、向前移动的整个过程中都应听到。

平板上的良好焊道应具备以下特征：

• 板面上飞溅极少或没有。

• 断弧后，焊道上留有约 1/16 英寸的弧坑。

• 焊道应略微隆起，顶面不得有金属重叠。

• 焊道应具有约 1/16 英寸的良好熔深，深入基材。

图5-31给出了操作者手法与焊机设置方面的示例。

向前推进焊条时，应使其朝行进方向、背离已完成焊道，保持约20°至25°的角度。

如果在焊一道焊缝的过程中断弧并迅速移开焊条，电弧终止处会形成一个弧坑。弧坑显示出焊缝所获得的熔深或熔合深度。弧坑是由焊条端部气体的压力把熔敷金属推向弧坑边缘而形成的。若缓慢移开焊条，弧坑则会被填满。

如果需要重新引弧以续焊中断的焊道，应从前一道焊道弧坑的前方起弧，如图5-33位置1所示；然后把焊条退回弧坑的后缘（步骤2）。从这一点起，可径直焊过弧坑、沿原定焊缝方向继续施焊（步骤3）。

焊道一旦形成，重新引弧之前必须把弧坑区域内每一粒熔渣清除干净。这要用尖锤和钢丝刷来完成，可防止熔渣夹入焊缝。

### 多道焊

厚金属的坡口焊和角焊，往往需要熔敷多道焊道才能完成一条焊缝。重要的是按预定顺序熔敷各焊道，以获得致密性最好、成形比例最佳的焊缝。焊道的数量取决于被焊金属的厚度。

厚度从 ⁄8 英寸到 1/4 英寸的板可单道焊成，但应间隔定位焊以保持对正。厚度超过 1/4 英寸的板，任何焊接都应开坡口并采用多道焊。

焊道熔敷顺序由接头种类和金属位置决定。每熔敷一道焊道之前，都必须清除前一道焊道上的全部熔渣。对接接头典型的多道坡口焊如图5-34所示。

### 空间位置焊接技术

每当焊接接头的位置或接头形式改变时，都可能需要改变下列各项中的一项或几项：• 电流值 • 焊条 • 极性 • 电弧长度 • 焊接手法。电流值由焊条规格以及焊接位置决定；焊条规格取决于金属厚度和接头准备；焊条型号由焊接位置决定；制造商会规定每种焊条应使用的极性；电弧长度则由焊条规格、焊接位置和焊接电流共同控制。

由于不可能把不同焊接条件引起的每一种变化都列举出来，这里只讨论常用位置和常用焊缝所必需的信息。

### 平焊位置焊接

平焊位置常用的焊缝有四种：焊道、坡口焊、角焊和搭接焊。以下各段分别讨论。

#### 焊道焊

焊道焊所用的手法与在平金属面上熔敷焊道相同，唯一区别是熔敷的焊道位于两块钢板的对接接头处，把它们熔合在一起。对接接头可单道或多道焊成。若金属厚度使得从一面焊接无法获得完全熔合，就必须从两面施焊。大多数接头应先定位焊，以保证对正并减少变形。

#### 坡口焊

坡口焊可在对接接头或外角接头上进行。坡口焊用于待焊金属厚度为1/4英寸或以上的对接接头。对接接头可根据板厚采用单面坡口或双面坡口。完成一条焊缝所需的道数，取决于被焊金属的厚度和所用焊条的规格。

任何多道完成的坡口焊，在覆盖焊接之前，都必须把此前各道焊缝上的熔渣、飞溅和氧化物仔细清除干净。平焊位置对接接头上常见的几种坡口焊如图5-36所示。

#### 角焊

角焊用于T形接头和搭接接头。焊条应与板面成45°角，并朝焊接方向倾斜约15°。薄板焊接时焊条几乎不作摆动，一道焊成。较厚板的角焊可能需要两道或更多道，焊条作半圆形摆动。

#### 搭接接头焊

搭接接头角焊的做法与T形接头类似。焊接同厚度的板时，焊条与垂直面约成30°角，并朝焊接方向倾斜约15°。

### 立焊位置焊接

立焊位置包括施加于与水平面倾角大于45°的表面上的任何焊缝。由于重力的作用，立焊比平焊更难。熔融金属有向下流淌的趋势。为控制熔池金属的流动，焊机的电压和电流必须调整正确。

在焊条规格相同的情况下，立焊时的电流设定（安培数）比平焊小。此外，同一工件上向上立焊所用电流应比向下立焊略高。向上焊时，焊条与垂直面成90°，焊道自下而上移动；注意力集中在接头两侧，中间会自然成形。向下焊时，手在电弧下方，焊条向上倾斜约15°，焊缝向下推进。

### 仰焊位置焊接

仰焊是焊接中最困难的工种之一，因为必须始终保持很短的弧长来控制熔融金属。重力会使熔融金属下坠或从板面垂流，因此进行仰焊时始终必须穿戴防护服和头具。

仰焊焊道时，焊条应与基材成90°角。在某些需要观察电弧和焊缝弧坑的场合，焊条可朝焊接方向倾斜15°。

在仰焊T形接头或搭接接头上进行角焊时，应保持短弧，焊条不得摆动。应控制电弧运动，以保证根部熔透良好、与板材熔合良好。若熔融金属变得过稀、有下垂趋势，应迅速把焊条从焊缝中心前方甩开，以拉长电弧、让金属凝固，然后把焊条立即送回焊缝弧坑继续施焊。

任何学习或从事电弧焊的人，都应始终清楚地看到熔池。否则就无法确保熔敷金属落在接头内、电弧始终走在熔池的前缘。为获得最佳视野，焊工应把头偏向一侧、避开烟尘，以便看清熔池。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 主语带长后置定语，表语为两个并列的 that 从句
**原句**：The first step in preparing to arc weld is to make certain that the necessary equipment is available and that the welding machine is properly connected and in good working order.
**拆解**：主语 The first step 后接介词短语 in preparing to arc weld 作后置定语；系动词 is 的表语是不定式 to make certain，其后跟两个由 that 引导的并列宾语从句，第一个从句为主系表结构（equipment is available），第二个从句含被动语态 is properly connected 与并列的表语 in good working order。
**要点**：make certain that... 意为“确认……”，that 在从句中不作成分，只起引导作用；两个 that 从句由 and 并列，共用 make certain 这个谓语，第二个 that 不能省，否则会被误读成 connected 的宾语。翻译时按“确认 A、确认 B”拆成两个分句更顺。

### ② 条件状语从句 + 现在分词短语作结果状语
**原句**：If the electrode is not withdrawn promptly upon contact with the metal, the high amperage flows through the electrode causing it to stick or freeze to the plate and practically short circuits the welding machine.
**拆解**：If 引导条件状语从句，从句为被动语态 is not withdrawn，upon contact with the metal 作时间状语；主句主语 the high amperage，并列谓语 flows through the electrode 与 short circuits the welding machine；中间的 causing it to stick or freeze to the plate 是现在分词短语作结果状语，逻辑主语是前面的整件事（电流过大）。
**要点**：causing 的逻辑主语不是 the electrode，而是主句所述情形，这类分词状语常译成“从而/致使……”。causing 与 short circuits 是两层并列：分词短语说明后果，short circuits 是主句的第二个谓语，不能把 and 后的 short circuits 当成 causing 的并列宾语。

### ③ It is + 形容词 + that 从句中的虚拟语气
**原句**：It is important that the beads be deposited in a predetermined sequence to produce the soundest welds with the best proportions.
**拆解**：It 为形式主语，真正主语是 that 引导的主语从句；从句谓语用原形 be deposited，构成虚拟语气被动式；in a predetermined sequence 作方式状语，to produce... 为目的状语，with the best proportions 修饰 welds。
**要点**：在 It is important/necessary/essential that... 结构中，从句要用“(should) + 动词原形”，即使是被动也只写 be deposited，不加 is。这里不能用 are deposited，否则语法错误；中文直接译“重要的是……应当按预定顺序熔敷”。

### ④ 并列条件从句 + where 引导的定语从句
**原句**：If the arc is broken during the welding of a bead and the electrode is removed quickly, a crater is formed at the point where the arc ends.
**拆解**：If 引导两个并列的条件从句（the arc is broken... 与 the electrode is removed quickly），主句为被动语态 a crater is formed，at the point 作地点状语，where the arc ends 是修饰 point 的定语从句。
**要点**：where 定语从句的先行词是 point 这类地点名词，where 在从句中作地点状语而不是主语。主句用被动 is formed，是因为要强调“弧坑被形成”这一结果而不必说出施事者；两个条件从句共用一个 If，第二个分句前不再重复 if。

### ⑤ Since 原因状语从句 + 过去分词后置定语 + 主句被动
**原句**：Since it is impractical to cite every possible variation occasioned by different welding conditions, only the information necessary for the commonly used positions and welds is discussed here.
**拆解**：Since 引导原因状语从句，从句中 it 为形式主语，真正主语是不定式 to cite...；variation 后接过去分词短语 occasioned by different welding conditions 作后置定语。主句主语 the information，后接形容词短语 necessary for... 作后置定语，谓语为被动语态 is discussed，here 作地点状语。
**要点**：occasion 作动词意为“引起、造成”，过去分词 occasioned by 相当于 which are occasioned by，是“被……引起的”而非“引起……的”，方向不能译反。主句主语 information 与谓语 is discussed 之间隔了 9 个词的后置定语，读句时先跳过定语锁定“information is discussed”这条主干。
