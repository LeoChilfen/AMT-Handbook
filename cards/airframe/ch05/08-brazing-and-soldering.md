# Brazing & Soldering

## 1. 原文

### Torch Brazing of Steel

The definition of joining two pieces of metal by brazing typically meant using brass or bronze as the filler metal. However, that definition has been expanded to include any metal joining process in which the bonding material is a nonferrous metal or alloy with a melting point higher than 800 °F, but lower than that of the metals being joined.

Brazing requires less heat than welding and can be used to join metals that may be damaged by high heat. However, because the strength of a brazed joint is not as great as that of a welded joint, brazing is not used for critical structural repairs on aircraft. Also, any metal part that is subjected to a sustained high temperature should not be brazed.

Brazing is applicable for joining a variety of metals, including brass, copper, bronze and nickel alloys, cast iron, malleable iron, wrought iron, galvanized iron and steel, carbon steel, and alloy steels. Brazing can also be used to join dissimilar metals, such as copper to steel or steel to cast iron.

When metals are joined by brazing, the base metal parts are not melted. The brazing metal adheres to the base metal by molecular attraction and intergranular penetration; it does not fuse and amalgamate with them.

In brazing, the edges of the pieces to be joined are usually beveled as in welding steel. The surrounding surfaces must be cleaned of dirt and rust. Parts to be brazed must be securely fastened together to prevent any relative movement. The strongest brazed joint is one in which the molten filler metal is drawn in by capillary action, requiring a close fit.

A brazing flux is necessary to obtain a good union between the base metal and the filler metal. It destroys the oxides and floats them to the surface, leaving a clean metal surface free from oxidation. A brazing rod can be purchased with a flux coating already applied, or any one of the numerous fluxes available on the market for specific application may be used. Most fluxes contain a mixture of borax and boric acid.

The base metal should be preheated slowly with a neutral soft flame until it reaches the flowing temperature of the filler metal. If a filler rod that is not precoated with flux is used, heat about 2 inches of the rod end with the torch to a dark purple color and dip it into the flux. Enough flux adheres to the rod that it is unnecessary to spread it over the surface of the metal. Apply the flux-coated rod to the red-hot metal with a brushing motion, using the side of the rod; the brass flows freely into the steel. Keep the torch heat on the base metal to melt the filler rod. Do not melt the rod with the torch. Continue to add the rod as the brazing progresses, with a rhythmic dipping action so that the bead is built to a uniform width and height. The job should be completed rapidly and with the fewest possible passes of the rod and torch.

Notice that some metals are good conductors of heat and dissipate the heat more rapidly away from the joint. Other metals are poor conductors that tend to retain the heat and overheat readily. Controlling the temperature of the base metal is extremely important. The base metal must be hot enough for the brazing filler to flow, but never overheated to the filler boiling point. This causes the joint to be porous and brittle.

The key to even heating of the joint area is to watch the appearance of the flux. The flux should change appearance uniformly when even heat is being applied. This is especially important when joining two metals of different mass or conductivity.

The brazing rod melts when applied to the red-hot base metal and runs into the joint by capillary attraction. (Note that molten brazing filler metal tends to flow toward the area of higher temperature.) In a torch heated assembly, the outer metal surfaces are slightly hotter than the interior joint surfaces. The filler metal should be deposited directly adjacent to the joint. Where possible, the heat should be applied to the assembly on the side opposite to where the filler is applied because the filler metal tends to flow toward the source of greater heat.

After the brazing is complete, the assembly or component must be cleaned. Since most brazing fluxes are water soluble, a hot water rinse (120 °F or hotter) and a wire brush remove the flux. If the flux was overheated during the brazing process, it usually turns green or black. In this case, the flux needs to be removed with a mild acid solution recommended by the manufacturer of the flux in use.

### Torch Brazing of Aluminum

Torch brazing of aluminum is done using similar methods as brazing of other materials. The brazing material itself is an aluminum/silicon alloy having a slightly lower melting temperature than the base material. Aluminum brazing occurs at temperatures over 875 °F, but below the melting point of the parent metal. This is performed with a specific aluminum brazing flux. Brazing is best suited to joint configurations that have large surface areas in contact, such as the lap, or for fitting fuel tank bungs and fittings. Either acetylene or hydrogen may be used as fuel gas, both being used for production work for many years. Using eye protection that reduces the sodium flare, such as the TM2000 lens, is recommended.

When using acetylene, the tip size is usually the same, or one size smaller than that used for welding of aluminum. A 1–2X reducing flame is used to form a slightly cooler flame, and the torch is held back at a greater distance using the outer envelope as the heat source rather than the inner cone. Prepare the flux and apply in the same manner as the aluminum welding flux, fluxing both the base metal and filler material. Heat the parts with the outer envelope of the flame, watching for the flux to begin to liquefy; the filler may be applied at that point. The filler should flow easily. If the part gets overheated, the flux turns brown or grey. If this happens, reclean and re-flux the part before continuing. Brazing is more easily accomplished on 1100, 3003, and 6061 aluminum alloys. 5052 alloy is more difficult; proper cleaning and practice are vital. There are brazing products sold that have the flux contained in hollow spaces in the filler metal itself, which typically work only on 1100, 3003, and 6061 alloys as the flux is not strong enough for use on 5052. Cleaning after brazing is accomplished the same as with oxy-fuel welding of aluminum, using hot water and a clean stainless brush. The flux is corrosive, so every effort should be made to remove it thoroughly and quickly after the brazing is completed.

### Soldering

Soldering is a method of thermally joining metal parts with a molten nonferrous alloy that melts at a temperature below 800 °F. The molten alloy is pulled up between close-fitting parts by capillary action. When the alloy cools and hardens, it forms a strong, leak-proof connection.

Soft solder is chiefly used to join copper and brass where a leak proof joint is desired, and sometimes for fitting joints to promote rigidity and prevent corrosion. Soft soldering is generally performed only in minor repair jobs. Soft solder is also used to join electrical connections. It forms a strong union with low electrical resistance.

Soft soldering does not require the heat of an oxy-fuel gas torch and can be performed using a small propane or MAPP® torch, an electrical soldering iron, or in some cases, a soldering copper, that is heated by an outside source, such as an oven or torch. The soft solders are chiefly alloys of tin and lead. The percentages of tin and lead vary considerably in the various solders with a corresponding change in their melting points ranging from 293 °F to 592 °F. Half-and-half (50/50) is the most common general-purpose solder. It contains equal portions of tin and lead and melts at approximately 360 °F.

To get the best results for heat transfer when using an electrical soldering iron or a soldering copper, the tip must be clean and have a layer of solder on it. This is usually referred to as being tinned. The hot iron or copper should be fluxed and the solder wiped across the tip to form a bright, thin layer of solder.

Flux is used with soft solder for the same reasons as with brazing. It cleans the surface area to be joined and promotes the flow by capillary action into the joint. Most fluxes should be cleaned away after the job is completed because they cause corrosion. Electrical connections should be soldered only with soft solder containing rosin. Rosin does not corrode the electrical connection.

#### Aluminum Soldering

The soldering of aluminum is much like the soldering of other metals. The use of special aluminum solders is required, along with the necessary flux. Aluminum soldering occurs at temperatures below 875 °F. Soldering can be accomplished using the oxy-acetylene, oxy-hydrogen, or even an air-propane torch setup. A neutral flame is used in the case of either oxy-acetylene or oxy-hydrogen. Depending on the solder and flux type, most common aluminum alloys can be soldered. Being of lower melting temperature, a tip one or two sizes smaller than required for welding is used, along with a soft flame setting.

Joint configurations for aluminum soldering follow the same guidelines as any other base material. Lap joints are preferred to tee or butt joints due to the larger surface contact area. However parts, such as heat exchanger tubes, are a common exception to this.

Normally, the parts are cleaned as for welding or brazing, and the flux is applied according to manufacturer’s instructions. The parts are evenly heated with the outer envelope of the flame to avoid overheating the flux, and the solder is applied in a fashion similar to that for other base metals. Cleaning after soldering may not be required to prevent oxidation because some fluxes are not corrosive. However, it is always advisable to remove all flux residues after soldering.

Aluminum soldering is commonly used in such applications as the repair of heat exchanger or radiator cores originally using a soldered joint. It is not, however, to be used as a direct replacement repair for brazing or welding.

#### Silver Soldering

The principle use of silver solder in aircraft work is in the fabrication of high-pressure oxygen lines and other parts that must withstand vibration and high temperatures.

Silver solder is used extensively to join copper and its alloys, nickel and silver, as well as various combinations of these metals and thin steel parts. Silver soldering produces joints of higher strength than those produced by other brazing processes.

Flux must be used in all silver soldering operations to ensure the base metal is chemically clean. The flux removes the film of oxide from the base metal and allows the silver solder to adhere to it.

All silver solder joints must be physically, as well as chemically, clean. The joint must be free of dirt, grease, oil, and/or paint. After removing the dirt, grease, etc., any oxide (rust and/or corrosion) should be removed by grinding or filing the piece until bright metal can be seen. During the soldering operation, the flux continues to keep the oxide away from the metal and aid in the flow of the solder.

The three recommended types of joint for silver soldering are lap, flanged, and edge. With these, the metal is formed to furnish a seam wider than the base metal thickness and provide the type of joint that holds up under all types of loads.

The oxy-acetylene flame for silver soldering should be a soft neutral or slightly reducing flame. That is, a flame with a slight excess of acetylene. During both preheating and application of the solder, the tip of the inner cone of the flame should be held about ⁄ 2 -inch from the work. The flame should be kept moving so that the metal does not overheat.

When both parts of the base metal are at the correct temperature, the flux flows and solder can be applied directly adjacent to the edge of the seam. It is necessary to simultaneously direct the flame over the seam and keep it moving so that the base metal remains at an even temperature.

## 2. 中文翻译

### 钢的火焰钎焊

把两块金属连接起来的钎焊（brazing），其定义历来是指用黄铜或青铜作填充金属。不过，这一定义已被扩展为：凡是以熔点高于 800 °F、但低于被连接金属熔点的非铁金属或合金作为结合材料的金属连接工艺，都属于钎焊。

钎焊所需热量比焊接少，可用于连接那些可能被高温损伤的金属。但由于钎焊接头的强度不如焊接接头，钎焊不用于飞机的关键结构修理。此外，任何长期承受高温的金属件都不应进行钎焊。

钎焊适用于连接多种金属，包括黄铜、铜、青铜和镍合金、铸铁、可锻铸铁、熟铁、镀锌铁和钢、碳钢以及合金钢。钎焊还可用于连接异种金属，例如铜与钢、钢与铸铁。

用钎焊连接金属时，母材并不熔化。钎料依靠分子吸引力和晶间渗透附着在母材上；它并不与母材熔合、混为一体。

钎焊时，待连接件的边缘通常像钢焊接那样开坡口。周围表面必须清除污物和锈蚀。待钎焊的零件必须牢固固定在一起，以防任何相对移动。最强的钎焊接头，是熔化的钎料被毛细作用吸入的那种接头，这要求配合紧密。

要获得母材与钎料之间的良好结合，必须使用钎剂。钎剂破坏氧化物并将其浮到表面，留下洁净、未被氧化的金属表面。可以购买已预涂钎剂的钎焊棒，也可使用市面销售的众多专用钎剂中的任何一种。大多数钎剂含有硼砂和硼酸的混合物。

母材应当用中性软火焰缓慢预热，直到达到钎料的流动温度。如果使用未预涂钎剂的填充棒，用焊炬把棒端约 2 英寸加热到暗紫色，然后浸入钎剂中。棒上附着的钎剂量已足够，无需再把它涂抹到金属表面。用棒的侧面以刷抹的动作把涂有钎剂的棒施加到赤热的金属上，黄铜便自由地流入钢中。焊炬热量要保持在母材上以熔化填充棒，不要用焊炬直接熔化填充棒。钎焊过程中要持续添加填充棒，以有节奏的蘸送动作使焊道宽度和高度均匀。作业应迅速完成，并使填充棒与焊炬的往返次数尽可能少。

注意，有些金属导热性好，会较快地把热量从接头处散走；另一些金属导热性差，容易蓄热而过热。控制母材温度极为重要。母材必须热到能让钎料流动，但绝不可过热到钎料沸点，否则会使接头多孔且脆。

接头区域加热均匀的关键，在于观察钎剂的外观。加热均匀时，钎剂应均匀地改变外观。在连接质量或导热性不同的两种金属时，这一点尤为重要。

钎焊棒施加到赤热的母材上即熔化，并靠毛细引力流入接头。（注意：熔化的钎料倾向于流向温度较高的区域。）在用焊炬加热的组件中，外侧金属表面比内部接头表面略热。钎料应直接施加在接头附近。条件允许时，应从与施加钎料相反的一侧对组件加热，因为钎料倾向于流向热源。

钎焊完成后，组件或部件必须清洗。由于大多数钎剂可溶于水，用热水（120 °F 或更热）冲洗并用钢丝刷即可去除钎剂。如果钎焊过程中钎剂被过热，它通常会变成绿色或黑色。这种情况下，需用钎剂制造商推荐的弱酸溶液去除。

### 铝的火焰钎焊

铝的火焰钎焊所用方法与其他材料钎焊类似。钎料本身是一种铝硅合金，熔点略低于母材。铝钎焊在 875 °F 以上的温度进行，但低于母材熔点。作业使用专用的铝钎剂。钎焊最适合接触面较大的接头形式，如搭接接头，或用于装配油箱口盖和接头件。乙炔或氢均可作燃气，两者多年来都用于生产作业。建议使用可减弱钠焰的护目镜，例如 TM2000 镜片。

使用乙炔时，焊嘴尺寸通常与铝焊接相同或小一号。采用 1–2X 的还原焰以获得稍冷的火焰，焊炬拉开较大距离，用外焰而非内锥作为热源。按铝焊接钎剂的方式配制和施加钎剂，母材和钎料都要涂覆。用火焰外焰加热零件，观察钎剂开始液化，此时即可施加钎料。钎料应能顺畅流动。若零件过热，钎剂会变成褐色或灰色。一旦如此，应重新清理并重新涂覆钎剂后再继续。1100、3003 和 6061 铝合金较易钎焊；5052 合金较难，彻底清洁和练习至关重要。有些市售钎焊产品的钎剂就封在钎料内部的空腔中，这类产品通常只适用于 1100、3003 和 6061 合金，因为其钎剂强度不足以用于 5052。钎焊后的清洗与铝的氧燃气焊接相同，用热水和洁净的不锈钢刷。钎剂有腐蚀性，因此钎焊完成后应尽一切努力彻底、迅速地将其清除。

### 软钎焊

软钎焊（soldering）是用熔点低于 800 °F 的熔融非铁合金把金属零件热连接起来的方法。熔融合金靠毛细作用被吸升到紧密配合的零件之间。合金冷却硬化后，形成坚固、不漏的连接。

软钎料主要用于连接需要不漏接头的铜和黄铜，有时也用于配合接头以增加刚性、防止腐蚀。软钎焊一般只在小的修理作业中进行。软钎料也用于连接电气接头，形成电阻低的牢固连接。

软钎焊无需氧燃气焊炬的热量，可用小型丙烷或 MAPP® 焊炬、电烙铁，或在某些情况下用由外部热源（如烘箱或焊炬）加热的烙铁铜头来完成。软钎料主要是锡和铅的合金。各种钎料中锡、铅的百分比差别很大，熔点也相应从 293 °F 变到 592 °F。对半（50/50）钎料是最常见的通用钎料，含等量的锡和铅，熔点约为 360 °F。

使用电烙铁或烙铁铜头时，为获得最佳传热效果，烙铁头必须清洁并覆有一层钎料，这通常称为“挂锡”。热烙铁或铜头应先涂钎剂，再把钎料擦过烙铁头，形成一层明亮、薄的钎料层。

软钎焊使用钎剂的原因与钎焊相同：清洁待连接的表面，并促使钎料靠毛细作用流入接头。多数钎剂在作业完成后应清除，因为它们会引起腐蚀。电气接头只应使用含松香的软钎料。松香不会腐蚀电气接头。

#### 铝的软钎焊

铝的软钎焊与其他金属的软钎焊非常相似。必须使用专用的铝钎料以及相应的钎剂。铝软钎焊在 875 °F 以下的温度进行。可用氧乙炔、氧氢，甚至空气-丙烷焊炬装置完成。使用氧乙炔或氧氢时均采用中性焰。视钎料和钎剂类型而定，多数常用铝合金都可软钎焊。由于熔点较低，采用比焊接所需小一到两号的焊嘴，并用软焰设定。

铝软钎焊的接头形式遵循与其他母材相同的准则。由于接触面积较大，搭接接头优于 T 形或对接接头。但换热器管等零件是常见的例外。

通常，零件按焊接或钎焊的方法清理，钎剂按制造商说明施加。用火焰外焰均匀加热零件，以免钎剂过热，钎料则按与其他母材相似的方式施加。软钎焊后的清洗可能并非为防止氧化所必需，因为有些钎剂无腐蚀性；不过，钎焊后清除全部钎剂残留总是可取的。

铝软钎焊常用于修理原本采用钎焊接头的换热器或散热器芯等场合。但它不得用作钎焊或焊接的直接替代修理。

#### 银钎焊

银钎料在飞机作业中的主要用途，是制造高压氧气导管以及其他必须承受振动和高温的零件。

银钎料被广泛用于连接铜及其合金、镍和银，以及这些金属的各种组合和薄钢件。银钎焊产生的接头强度高于其他钎焊工艺。

所有银钎焊作业都必须使用钎剂，以确保母材化学洁净。钎剂去除母材上的氧化膜，使银钎料能够附着。

所有银钎焊接头都必须既化学洁净又物理洁净。接头不得有污物、油脂、油和/或漆。去除污物、油脂等之后，还应通过打磨或锉削去除任何氧化物（锈和/或腐蚀），直到露出光亮的金属。钎焊过程中，钎剂持续使氧化物远离金属并助钎料流动。

银钎焊推荐三种接头形式：搭接、翻边和边缘接头。用这些形式把金属成形为比母材厚度更宽的接缝，并提供能承受各种载荷的接头形式。

银钎焊的氧乙炔焰应是软中性焰或稍还原焰，即乙炔略有剩余的火焰。预热和施加钎料时，火焰内锥尖端应保持在距工件约 1/2 英寸处。火焰应不断移动，以免金属过热。

当母材两部分都达到正确温度时，钎剂流动，即可把钎料直接施加在接缝边缘附近。必须同时把火焰对准接缝并保持移动，使母材温度保持均匀。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 定义句里的“介词 + which”定语从句与比较结构
**原句**：However, that definition has been expanded to include any metal joining process in which the bonding material is a nonferrous metal or alloy with a melting point higher than 800 °F, but lower than that of the metals being joined.
**拆解**：主句为现在完成时被动语态 has been expanded；to include any metal joining process 作结果状语；in which 引导定语从句修饰 process，从句主语 the bonding material，表语 a nonferrous metal or alloy；with a melting point... 为介词短语作后置定语，其中 higher than... but lower than... 是并列的比较成分，than 后的 that 指代 melting point，being joined 为现在分词的被动式作后置定语。
**要点**：in which 相当于 in the process，因为先行词是“工艺”，从句却用“材料是什么”来说明，译成中文要把这种“工艺—材料”关系理顺。两个 than 后面都无法直接跟名词：第一个跟数字，第二个跟代词 that，that of the metals being joined 即“被连接金属的熔点”，不要误读成“被连接的金属”。

### ② because 原因状语从句置于主句之前
**原句**：However, because the strength of a brazed joint is not as great as that of a welded joint, brazing is not used for critical structural repairs on aircraft.
**拆解**：because 引导原因状语从句，从句为“主语 + be 动词 + not as...as 比较结构”，that of a welded joint 中 that 指代 strength；主句 brazing is not used for... 为一般现在时被动语态，for 引出用途。
**要点**：not as great as 是“不如……大”的否定同级比较，不等于“不比……大”；因为主句谓语是被动，中文宜译成“不用于……”。全句两个否定叠加，理解时要先分清哪个否定属于比较结构。

### ③ in which 定语从句 + 现在分词作结果状语
**原句**：The strongest brazed joint is one in which the molten filler metal is drawn in by capillary action, requiring a close fit.
**拆解**：主干 The strongest brazed joint is one；in which 引导定语从句修饰 one，从句为被动语态 is drawn in，by capillary action 表方式；逗号后的 requiring a close fit 是现在分词短语作结果状语，逻辑主语是整个从句所述情形。
**要点**：one 是代词，指代 joint，不能理解为数词“一个”。requiring 前有逗号，说明它不是限制性定语，而是补充结果——“因而要求配合紧密”，中文可用“这要求……”承接。

### ④ 现在分词短语作结果状语
**原句**：It destroys the oxides and floats them to the surface, leaving a clean metal surface free from oxidation.
**拆解**：主语 It 指钎剂；并列谓语 destroys the oxides 与 floats them to the surface；leaving... 为现在分词短语作结果状语，a clean metal surface 是 leaving 的宾语，free from oxidation 为形容词短语作后置定语。
**要点**：leaving 的逻辑主语是前面的整个动作（钎剂破坏并浮除氧化物），不是 it 单独发出的第二个动作，所以不能译成并列谓语“并且留下”。free from 是“不含、免于”，作后置定语修饰 surface。

### ⑤ when + 过去分词，状语从句的省略
**原句**：The brazing rod melts when applied to the red-hot base metal and runs into the joint by capillary attraction.
**拆解**：主语 The brazing rod，并列谓语 melts 与 runs；when applied to... 是时间状语从句的省略形式，完整为 when it is applied to...，省略了与主句相同的主语和 be 动词；by capillary attraction 表方式。
**要点**：分词作状语时若逻辑主语与主句主语一致且含 be 动词，常连同主语一起省略，只剩过去分词。这里“被施加到母材上”的是钎焊棒，所以用过去分词 applied，不能用 applying。
