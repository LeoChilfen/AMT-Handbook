## 1. 原文

The FAA has had a formal involvement in this issue since 1988. That was the year the first Human Factors Issues in Aviation Maintenance and Inspection National Conference was conducted, and that effort reflects a working relationship between government research and industry activity. This yearly event includes airlines, suppliers, manufacturers, schools, and government agencies. There is also an FAA website for human factors at hf.faa.gov which is a tremendous resource.

### Importance of Human Factors

The greatest impact in aircraft safety in the future will not come from improving the technology. Rather it will be from educating the employee to recognize and prevent human error. A review of accident related data indicates that approximately 75–80 percent of all aviation accidents are the result of human error. Of those accidents, about 12 percent are maintenance related. Although pilot/co-pilot errors tend to have immediate and highly visible effects, maintenance errors tend to be more latent and less obvious. However, they can be just as lethal.

### Definitions of Human Factors

Human factors are concerned with optimizing performance … including reducing errors so that the highest level of safety is achieved and maintained.

—Ron LoFaro, PhD FAA Human factors is the study of how people interact with their environments.

—FAA-H-8083-25, Pilot’ s Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge Human factors are those elements that affect our behavior and performance, especially those that may cause us to make errors.

—Canadian Department of Defense (video)

Our focus is on human factors as it relates to improper actions. Note, however, that human factors exist in both proper and improper actions. Since improper actions usually result in human error, we should also define that term.

Human error is the unintentional act of performing a task incorrectly that can potentially degrade the system. There are three types of human error:

1. Omission: not performing an act or task.

2. Commission: accomplishing a task incorrectly.

3. Extraneous: performing a task not authorized.

There are also four consequences of human error:

1. Little or no effect.

2. Damage to equipment/hardware.

Why are human conditions, such as fatigue, complacency, and stress, so important in aviation maintenance? These conditions, along with many others, are called human factors. Human factors directly cause or contribute to many aviation accidents. It is universally agreed that 80 percent of maintenance errors involve human factors. If they are not detected, they can cause events, worker injuries, wasted time, and even accidents.

Aviation safety relies heavily on maintenance. When it is not done correctly, it contributes to a significant proportion of aviation accidents and incidents. Some examples of maintenance errors are parts installed incorrectly, missing parts, and necessary checks not being performed. In comparison with many other threats to aviation safety, the mistakes of an aviation maintenance technician (AMT) can be more difficult to detect. Often, these mistakes are present but not visible and have the potential to remain latent, affecting the safe operation of aircraft for extended periods of time.

AMTs are confronted with a set of human factors unique within aviation. They can be working in the evening or early morning hours, in confined spaces, on high platforms, and in a variety of adverse temperature/humidity conditions. The work can be physically strenuous, yet it also requires attention to detail. Because of the nature of maintenance tasks, AMTs commonly spend more time preparing for a task than actually carrying it out. Proper documentation of all maintenance work is a key element, and AMTs typically spend as much time updating maintenance logs as they do performing the work.

Human factors awareness can lead to improved quality, an environment that ensures continuing worker and aircraft safety, and a more involved and responsible work force. The reduction of even minor errors can provide measurable benefits including cost reductions, fewer missed deadlines, reduction in work related injuries, reduction of warranty claims, and reduction in more significant events that can be traced back to maintenance error. Within this chapter, the many aspects of human factors are discussed in relation to aviation maintenance. The most common human factors are introduced along with ways to mitigate the risk to stop them from developing into a problem. Several Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) human factor resources are provided, including a direct link to aviation maintenance human factors are at hf.faa.gov.

## 2. 中文翻译

FAA 自 1988 年起就正式介入这一问题。正是在那一年，首次举办了「航空维修与检查中的人为因素问题」全国会议，这一努力体现了政府研究与产业活动之间的合作关系。这项年度活动涵盖航空公司、供应商、制造商、学校以及政府机构。FAA 还设有人为因素网站 hf.faa.gov，这是一个极好的资源。

### 人为因素的重要性

未来航空安全的最大改善不会来自技术改进，而会来自教育员工认识并预防人为差错。对事故相关数据的复查表明，全部航空事故中约 75%–80% 是人为差错造成的。在这些事故中，约 12% 与维修有关。尽管飞行员/副驾驶的差错往往具有即时且高度可见的后果，维修差错却往往更潜伏、更不明显。然而，它们同样可能致命。

### 人为因素的定义

人为因素关注的是优化绩效……包括减少差错，从而达到并保持最高安全水平。

——Ron LoFaro 博士，FAA。人为因素是研究人与环境如何相互作用的学科。

——FAA-H-8083-25，《航空知识飞行员手册》。人为因素是影响我们行为与绩效的那些要素，尤其是可能使我们犯错的要素。

——加拿大国防部（视频）

我们关注的是与不当操作有关的人为因素。但要注意，人为因素在正当操作和不当操作中都存在。由于不当操作通常会导致人为差错，我们也应当定义这一术语。

人为差错是指无意中以不正确的方式执行任务、并可能使系统性能下降的行为。人为差错有三种类型：

1. 遗漏：未执行某一动作或任务。

2. 做错：以不正确的方式完成任务。

3. 多余：执行了未经授权的任务。

人为差错还有四种后果：

1. 影响很小或没有影响。

2. 设备/硬件损坏。

为什么疲劳、自满和应激等人员状况在航空维修中如此重要？这些状况以及许多其他状况被称为人为因素。人为因素直接导致或促成了许多航空事故。人们普遍认为，80% 的维修差错涉及人为因素。如果这些因素未被发现，就可能引发事件、人员受伤、时间浪费，甚至事故。

航空安全在很大程度上依赖维修。若维修做得不正确，就会在相当大比例的航空事故和事件中起作用。维修差错的例子包括零件安装错误、零件缺失以及未执行必要的检查。与航空安全的许多其他威胁相比，航空维修技术员（AMT）的失误可能更难被发现。这些失误常常存在却不可见，并有可能长期潜伏，在很长一段时间内影响飞机的安全运行。

AMT 面临着一组航空领域特有的人员因素。他们可能在傍晚或清晨工作，在狭小空间内、在高台上、在各种不利的温度/湿度条件下工作。这项工作可能很耗费体力，同时又要求关注细节。由于维修任务的性质，AMT 用于准备任务的时间通常多于实际执行任务的时间。妥善记录所有维修工作是关键环节，而 AMT 更新维修记录所花的时间通常与执行维修工作本身一样多。

对人为因素的认识可以带来质量提升、确保工作人员与飞机持续安全的环境，以及更加投入、更负责任的工作队伍。即使减少轻微差错也能带来可衡量的收益，包括降低成本、减少逾期、减少工伤、减少保修索赔，以及减少可追溯至维修差错的更严重事件。本章将结合航空维修讨论人为因素的诸多方面。文中介绍最常见的人为因素，以及降低风险、防止其发展成问题的办法。还提供了若干美国联邦航空管理局（FAA）的人为因素资源，包括航空维修人为因素的直接链接 hf.faa.gov。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① not…rather… 的对比否定
**原句**：The greatest impact in aircraft safety in the future will not come from improving the technology.
**拆解**：主语为 The greatest impact in aircraft safety in the future，谓语为 will not come，from improving the technology 是介词短语作状语，improving 为动名词。
**要点**：not…rather… 先否定前者、再肯定后者，rather 后接与前面平行的成分；come from doing 表示「来自做某事」，介词 from 后必须用动名词。

### ② indicate that 宾语从句与百分数主谓一致
**原句**：A review of accident related data indicates that approximately 75–80 percent of all aviation accidents are the result of human error.
**拆解**：主语 A review of accident related data，谓语 indicates，that 引导宾语从句；从句主语是 approximately 75–80 percent of all aviation accidents，谓语用 are。
**要点**：percent of + 名词作主语时，谓语与 of 后的名词一致，此处 aviation accidents 为复数，故用 are；be the result of 是「是……的结果」的固定搭配。

### ③ although 引导的让步状语从句
**原句**：Although pilot/co-pilot errors tend to have immediate and highly visible effects, maintenance errors tend to be more latent and less obvious.
**拆解**：Although 引导让步状语从句，主句为 maintenance errors tend to be more latent and less obvious；tend to do 表示「往往、倾向于」。
**要点**：although 不能与 but 连用；more latent and less obvious 是并列比较级，翻译时按「更……、更……」处理。

### ④ be difficult to detect 的主动形式表被动
**原句**：In comparison with many other threats to aviation safety, the mistakes of an aviation maintenance technician (AMT) can be more difficult to detect.
**拆解**：In comparison with… 为状语；主语 the mistakes of an AMT；can be more difficult 为系表结构，to detect 为不定式。
**要点**：形容词后的不定式用主动形式表示被动含义，detect 的逻辑宾语就是主语 mistakes；in comparison with 意为「与……相比」。

### ⑤ spend time doing 与 more…than 平行结构
**原句**：Because of the nature of maintenance tasks, AMTs commonly spend more time preparing for a task than actually carrying it out.
**拆解**：Because of… 为原因状语；主句为 AMTs spend more time preparing for a task than actually carrying it out，spend time doing 为固定结构。
**要点**：more…than 比较的是 preparing for a task 与 actually carrying it out 两个动名词短语，than 后省略了 spending time；carry out 的宾语 it 指 a task。
