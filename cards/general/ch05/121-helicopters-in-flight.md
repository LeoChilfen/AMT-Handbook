## 1. 原文

Hovering For a helicopter, hovering means that it is in flight at a constant altitude, with no forward, aft, or sideways movement. In order to hover, a helicopter must be producing enough lift in its main rotor blades to equal the weight of the aircraft. The engine of the helicopter must be producing enough power to drive the main rotor, and also to drive whatever type of anti-torque system is being used. The ability of a helicopter to hover is affected by many things, including whether or not it is in ground effect, the density altitude of the air, the available power from the engine, and how heavily loaded it is.

For a helicopter to experience ground effect, it typically needs to be no higher off the ground than one half of its main rotor system diameter. If a helicopter has a main rotor diameter of 40 ft., it will be in ground effect up to an altitude of approximately 20 ft. Being close to the ground affects the velocity of the air through the rotor blades, causing the effective angle of attack of the blades to increase and the lift to increase. So, if a helicopter is in ground effect, it can hover at a higher gross weight than it can when out of ground effect. On a windy day, the positive influence of ground effect is lessened, and at a forward speed of 5 to 10 mph the positive influence becomes less. In Figure 5-93, an Air Force CH-53 is seen in a hover, with all the rotor blades flapping up as a result of creating equal lift.

Forward Flight In the early days of helicopter development, the ability to hover was mastered before there was success in attaining forward flight. The early attempts at forward flight resulted in the helicopter rolling over when it tried to depart from the hover and move in any direction. The cause of the rollover is what we now refer to as dissymmetry of lift.

When a helicopter is in a hover, all the rotor blades are experiencing the same velocity of airflow and the velocity of the airflow seen by the rotor blades changes when the helicopter starts to move. For helicopters built in the United States, the main rotor blades turn in a counterclockwise direction when viewed from the top. Viewed from the top, as the blades move around the right side of the helicopter, they are moving toward the nose; as they move around the left side of the helicopter, they are moving toward the tail. When the helicopter starts moving forward, the blade on the right side is moving toward the relative wind, and the blade on the left

The increased lift created by the blade on the right side will try to roll the helicopter to the left. If this condition is allowed to exist, it will ultimately lead to the helicopter crashing.

Blade Flapping To solve the problem of dissymmetry of lift, helicopter designers came up with a hinged design that allows the rotor blade to flap up when it experiences increased lift, and to flap down when it experiences decreased lift. When a rotor blade advances toward the front of the helicopter and experiences an increased velocity of airflow, the increase in lift causes the blade to flap up. This upward motion of the blade changes the direction of the relative wind in relation to the chord line of the blade, and causes the angle of attack to decrease.

The decrease in the angle of attack decreases the lift on the blade. The retreating blade experiences a reduced velocity of airflow and reduced lift, and flaps down. By flapping down, the retreating blade ends up with an increased angle of attack and an increase in lift. The end result is the lift on the blades is equalized, and the tendency for the helicopter to roll never materializes.

Advancing Blade and Retreating Blade Problems The blade advancing toward the relative wind sees the airflow at an ever increasing velocity as a helicopter flies forward at higher and higher speeds. Eventually, the velocity of the air over the rotor blade will reach sonic velocity, much like the critical Mach number for the wing of an airplane. When this happens, a shock wave will form and the air will separate from the rotor blade, resulting in a high-speed stall.

As the helicopters forward speed increases, the relative wind over the retreating blade decreases, resulting in a loss of lift.

The loss of lift causes the blade to flap down and the effective angle of attack to increase. At a high enough forward speed, the angle of attack will increase to a point that the rotor blade stalls. The tip of the blade stalls first, and then progresses in toward the blade root.

When approximately 25 percent of the rotor system is stalled, due to the problems with the advancing and retreating blades, control of the helicopter will be lost. Conditions that will lead to the rotor blades stalling include high forward speed, heavy gross weight, turbulent air, high-density altitude, and steep or abrupt turns.

Autorotation The engine on a helicopter drives the main rotor system by way of a clutch and a transmission. The clutch allows the engine to be running and the rotor system not to be turning, while the helicopter is on the ground, and it also allows the rotor system to disconnect from the engine while in flight, if the engine fails. Having the rotor system disconnect from the engine in the event of an engine failure is necessary if the helicopter is to be capable of a flight condition called autorotation.

Autorotation is a flight condition where the main rotor blades are driven by the force of the relative wind passing through the blades, rather than by the engine. This flight condition is similar to an airplane gliding if its engine fails while in flight. As long as the helicopter maintains forward airspeed, while decreasing altitude and the pilot lowers the blade angle on the blades with the collective pitch, the rotor blades will continue to rotate. The altitude of the helicopter, which equals potential energy, is given up in order to have enough energy, which will then be kinetic energy, to keep the rotor blades turning. As the helicopter nears the ground, the cyclic pitch control is used to slow the forward speed and to flare the helicopter for landing. With the airspeed bled off, and the helicopter now close to the ground, the final step is to use the collective pitch control to cushion the landing. ## 2. 中文翻译

悬停　对直升机而言，悬停是指以恒定高度飞行，没有前、后或侧向移动。为了悬停，直升机主旋翼桨叶产生的升力必须等于航空器的重量。直升机的发动机必须产生足够的功率来驱动主旋翼，并驱动所使用的任何一种反扭矩系统。直升机悬停的能力受许多因素影响，包括是否处于地面效应之中、空气的密度高度、发动机可用功率以及载重的大小。

直升机要获得地面效应，通常离地高度不得超过主旋翼系统直径的一半。如果直升机主旋翼直径为 40 ft，那么在约 20 ft 高度以内都处于地面效应中。靠近地面会影响流经旋翼桨叶的气流速度，使桨叶的有效迎角增大、升力增大。因此，处于地面效应中的直升机，其可悬停的总重比脱离地面效应时更大。在有风的日子，地面效应的有利影响会减弱；前飞速度达到 5 至 10 mph 时，这种有利影响变得更小。图 5-93 中，一架空军 CH-53 正在悬停，由于产生相同的升力，所有旋翼桨叶都向上挥舞。

前飞　在直升机发展的早期，人们先掌握了悬停能力，之后才成功实现前飞。早期尝试前飞时，直升机在试图脱离悬停并向任一方向移动时会发生侧翻。侧翻的原因是今天我们所说的升力不对称。

桨叶挥舞　为解决升力不对称问题，直升机设计者提出了一种铰接式设计，使旋翼桨叶在升力增大时向上挥舞、升力减小时向下挥舞。当一片旋翼桨叶向直升机前方运动并获得更大的气流速度时，升力的增大会使该桨叶向上挥舞。桨叶的这种向上运动改变了相对气流相对于桨叶弦线的方向，使迎角减小。

迎角减小使桨叶上的升力减小。后退桨叶经历较低的气流速度和较小的升力，因而向下挥舞。通过向下挥舞，后退桨叶最终获得较大的迎角和较大的升力。最终结果是桨叶上的升力被均衡，直升机滚转的趋势不再出现。

前进桨叶与后退桨叶的问题　随着直升机前飞速度越来越高，朝相对气流方向运动的前进桨叶所遇到的气流速度不断增大。最终，流过旋翼桨叶的空气速度会达到声速，很像飞机机翼的临界马赫数。一旦发生这种情况，就会形成激波，气流从旋翼桨叶上分离，导致高速失速。

随着直升机前飞速度增大，后退桨叶上的相对气流减弱，导致升力损失。

升力损失使桨叶向下挥舞，有效迎角增大。当前飞速度足够高时，迎角会增大到使旋翼桨叶失速的程度。桨尖先失速，然后向桨根方向发展。

当前进桨叶和后退桨叶的问题导致旋翼系统约有 25% 失速时，直升机将失去操纵。会导致旋翼桨叶失速的情况包括：前飞速度高、总重大、大气紊流、高密度高度，以及急陡或突然的转弯。

自转　直升机的发动机通过离合器和传动装置驱动主旋翼系统。离合器使直升机在地面时发动机可以运转而旋翼系统不转，也使飞行中发动机失效时旋翼系统能与发动机脱开。要使直升机能够进入一种称为自转的飞行状态，发动机失效时让旋翼系统与发动机脱开是必需的。

自转是这样一种飞行状态：主旋翼桨叶由流经桨叶的相对气流的力量驱动，而不是由发动机驱动。这种飞行状态类似于飞机在飞行中发动机失效后的滑翔。只要直升机保持前飞空速，同时降低高度，并且飞行员用总距杆减小桨叶角，旋翼桨叶就会继续旋转。直升机的高度相当于势能，牺牲高度以换取足够的能量（此时为动能），来维持旋翼桨叶转动。接近地面时，用周期变距杆减小前飞速度并拉平直升机以便着陆。空速减小、直升机已很接近地面时，最后一步是用总距杆缓冲着陆。正常前飞和自转飞行状态下流经旋翼桨叶的气流如图 5-95 所示。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① for + 名词 + 不定式（逻辑主语）+ no higher than

**原句**：For a helicopter to experience ground effect, it typically needs to be no higher off the ground than one half of its main rotor system diameter.

**拆解**：For a helicopter to experience ground effect 是 for + 名词 + 不定式结构，作状语表示「对直升机而言要获得地面效应」，不定式的逻辑主语由 for 引出；主句 it typically needs to be no higher off the ground than one half of its main rotor system diameter，no higher than 是比较结构。

**要点**：for sb/sth to do 是不定式的复合结构，for 不能省。no higher than 意为「不高于」，是否定词 + 比较级构成的绝对比较；off the ground 是「离地」，off 在此为介词，不是短语动词的一部分。

### ② 被动 + including + 名词性从句并列

**原句**：The ability of a helicopter to hover is affected by many things, including whether or not it is in ground effect, the density altitude of the air, the available power from the engine, and how heavily loaded it is.

**拆解**：主语 The ability of a helicopter to hover（ability 后接不定式作定语）；谓语 is affected by many things 为被动；including 后并列四项：whether or not it is in ground effect（whether 从句）、the density altitude of the air、the available power from the engine、how heavily loaded it is（how 从句）。

**要点**：including 是介词，后面可以直接接从句（whether / how 引导），此时不是分词短语作状语。whether or not 意为「是否」，从句用陈述语序 it is，不能倒装；ability to do 是固定搭配，不用 ability of doing。

### ③ 两个非限制性定语从句 + in order to + keep + 宾语 + -ing

**原句**：The altitude of the helicopter, which equals potential energy, is given up in order to have enough energy, which will then be kinetic energy, to keep the rotor blades turning.

**拆解**：主语 The altitude of the helicopter 后接 which equals potential energy（非限制性定语从句）；谓语 is given up 为被动；in order to have enough energy 是目的状语，energy 后又有 which will then be kinetic energy 非限制性定语从句；句末 to keep the rotor blades turning 仍属目的，说明取得能量用来做什么。

**要点**：两个 which 从句都用逗号隔开，属补充说明，删掉不影响主干 The altitude is given up。keep + 宾语 + 现在分词表示「使……持续（转动）」，与 keep + 宾语 + 形容词不同。翻译时 in order to… 与句末 to keep… 是同一目的链，不可割裂。
