## 1. 原文

By definition, a fluid is any substance that is able to flow if it is not in some way confined or restricted. Liquids and gases are both classified as fluids, and often act in a very similar way. One significant difference comes into play when a force is applied to these fluids. In this case, liquids tend to be incompressible and gases are highly compressible. Many of the principles that aviation is based on, such as the theory of lift on a wing and the force generated by a hydraulic system, can be explained and quantified by using the laws of fluid mechanics.

A solid body submerged in a liquid or a gas weighs less than when weighed in free space. This is because of the upward force, called buoyant force, which any fluid exerts on a body submerged in it. An object will float if this upward force of the fluid is greater than the weight of the object. Objects denser than the fluid, even though they sink readily, appear to lose a part of their weight when submerged. A person can lift a larger weight under water than they can possibly lift in the air.

Consider the following experiment. The overflow can is filled to the spout with water. The heavy metal cube is first weighed in still air and weighs 10 lb. It is then weighed while completely submerged in the water and it weighs 3 lb. The difference between the two weights is the buoyant force of the water. As the cube is lowered into the overflow can, the water is caught in the catch bucket. The volume of water which overflows equals the volume of the cube. The volume of irregular shaped objects can also be measured by using this method. If this experiment is performed carefully, the weight of the water displaced by the metal cube exactly equals the buoyant force of the water, which the scale shows to be 7 lb.

Archimedes (287–212 B.C.) performed similar experiments. As a result, he discovered that the buoyant force which a fluid exerts upon a submerged body is equal to the weight of the fluid the body displaces. This statement is referred to as Archimedes’ principle. This principle applies to all fluids, gases as well as liquids. Just as water exerts a buoyant force on submerged objects, air exerts a buoyant force on objects submerged in it.

The amount of buoyant force available to an object can be calculated by using the following formula:

Buoyant Force = V olume of Object × Density of Fluid Displaced If the buoyant force is more than the object weighs, the object will float. If the buoyant force is less than the object weighs, the object will sink. For the object that sinks, its measurable weight will be less by the weight of the displaced fluid.

Example: A 10-ft³ object weighing 700 lb is placed in pure water. Will the object float? If the object sinks, what is its measurable weight in the submerged condition? If the object floats, how many cubic feet of its volume is below the water line?

Buoyant Force = V olume of Object × Density of Fluid Displaced = 10 (62.4)

= 624 lb The object will sink because the buoyant force is less than the object weighs. The difference between the buoyant force and the object’s weight will be its measurable weight, or 76 lb.

Two good examples of buoyancy are a helium filled airship and a seaplane on floats. An airship is able to float in the atmosphere and a seaplane is able to float on water. That means both have more buoyant force than weight. Take the DeHavilland Twin Otter seaplane, with a gross takeoff weight of 12,500 lb. At a minimum, the floats on this airplane must be large enough to displace a weight in water equal to the airplane’s weight. According to Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations (14 CFR) part 23, the floats must be 80 percent larger than the minimum needed to support the airplane. The pressure exerted on the bottom of a container by a liquid is determined by the height of the liquid and not by the shape of the container. Three different shapes and sizes of containers are full of colored water. Even though they are different shapes and have different volumes of liquid, each one has a height of 231 inches. Each one would exert a pressure on the bottom of 8.34 psi because of this height. The container on the left, with a surface area of 1 in², contains a volume of 231 in³ (one gallon). One gallon of water weighs 8.34 lb, which is why the pressure on the bottom is 8.34 psi.

Still thinking about these containers, if the pressure was measured half way down, it would be half of 8.34, or 4.17 psi. In other words, the pressure is adjustable by varying the height of the column. Pressure based on the column height of a fluid is known as static pressure. With liquids, such as gasoline, it is sometimes referred to as a head of pressure. For example, if a carburetor needs to have 2 psi supplied to its inlet, or head of pressure, this could be accomplished by having the fuel tank positioned the appropriate number of inches higher than the carburetor.

As identified in the previous paragraph, pressure due to the height of a fluid column is known as static pressure. When a fluid is in motion, and its velocity is converted to pressure, that pressure is known as ram. When ram pressure and static pressure are added together, the result is known as total pressure. In the inlet of a gas turbine engine, for example, total pressure is often measured to provide a signal to the fuel metering device or to provide a signal to a gauge on the flight deck.

The foundations of modern hydraulics and pneumatics were established in 1653 when Pascal discovered that pressure set up in a fluid acts equally in all directions. This pressure acts at right angles to containing surfaces. When the pressure in the fluid is caused solely by the fluid’s height, the pressure against the walls of the container is equal at any given level, but it is not equal if the pressure at the bottom is compared to the pressure half way down. The concept of the pressure set up in a fluid, and how it relates to the force acting on the fluid and the surface area through which it acts, is Pascal’s law.

If a piston is placed at the top of the cylinder and an external force pushes down on the piston, additional pressure will be created in the liquid. If the additional pressure is 100 psi, this 100 psi will act equally and undiminished from the top of the cylinder all the way to the bottom.

The gauge at the bottom will now read 108.34 psi, and if a gauge were positioned half way down the cylinder, it would read 104.17 psi, which is found by adding 100 plus half of 8.34.

Pascal’s law, when dealing with the variables of force, pressure, and area, is dealt with by way of the following formula.

Force = Pressure × Area In this formula, the force is in units of pounds, the pressure is in pounds per square inch (psi), and the area is in square inches. By transposing the original formula, we have two additional formulas, as follows:

Pressure = Force ÷ Area and Area = Force ÷ Pressure An easy and convenient way to remember the formulas for Pascal’s law, and the relationship between the variables, is with a triangle. If the variable we want to solve for is covered up, the position of the remaining two variables shows the proper math relationship. For example, if the “A,” or area, is covered up, what remains is the “F” on the top and the “P” on the bottom, meaning force divided by pressure.

A simple hydraulic system has 5 lb force acting on a piston with a 1⁄2-in² surface area. Based on Pascal’s law, the pressure in the system would be equal to the force applied divided by the area of the piston, or 10 psi. The pressure of 10 psi is present everywhere in the fluid.

This hydraulic system is a little more complex than the previous one. The input force of 5 lb is acting on a 1⁄2-in² piston, creating a pressure of 10 psi. The input cylinder and piston is connected to a second cylinder, which contains a 5-in² piston. The pressure of 10 psi created by the input piston pushes on the piston in the second cylinder, creating an output force of 50 pounds.

Often, the purpose of a hydraulic system is to generate a large output force, with the input force being much less. The input force is 5 lb and the output force is 50 lb, or 10 times greater. The relationship between the output force and the input force, as discussed earlier in this chapter, is known as mechanical advantage. The mechanical advantage would be 50 divided by 5, or 10. The following formulas can be used to calculate mechanical advantage.

Mechanical Advantage = Force Out ÷ Force In or Mechanical Advantage = Distance Out ÷ Distance In Earlier in this chapter when simple machines, such as levers and gears were discussed, it was identified that no machine allows us to gain work. The same statement holds true for a hydraulic system, that we get no more work out of a hydraulic system than we put in. Since work is equal to force times distance, if we gain force with a hydraulic system, we must lose distance. We only get the same work out, if the system is 100 percent efficient.

In order to think about the distance that the output piston will move in response to the movement of the input piston, the volume of fluid displaced must be considered. In the study of geometry, one learns that the volume of a cylinder is equal to the cylinder’s surface area multiplied by its height. So, when a piston of 2 in² moves down in a cylinder a distance of 10 in, it displaces a volume of fluid equal to 20 in³ (2 in² × 10 in). The 20 in³ displaced by the first piston is what moves over to the second cylinder and causes its piston to move. In a simple two-piston hydraulic system, the relationship between the piston area and the distance moved is shown by the following formula. Input Piston Area (Distance Moved) = Output Piston Area (Distance Moved)

This formula shows that the volume in is equal to the volume out. A small input piston moves a distance of 20 inches, and the larger output piston only moves a distance of 1 inch.

Example: A two-piston hydraulic system, has an input piston with an area of 1⁄4 in² and an output piston with an area of 15 in². An input force of 50 lb is applied, and the input piston moves 30 inches. What is the pressure in the system, how much force is generated by the output piston, how far would the output piston move, and what is the mechanical advantage?

Pressure = Force ÷ Area = 50 ÷ 1⁄4 = 200 psi Force = Pressure × Area = 200 × 15 = 3,000 lb Mechanical Advantage = Force Out ÷ Force In = 3,000 ÷ 50 = 60 Input Piston Area (Distance Moved) = Output Piston Area (Distance Moved)

1⁄4 (30) = 15 (Distance Moved)

1⁄4 (30) ÷ 15 = Distance Moved Distance Moved = 1⁄2 in Part of understanding Pascal’s law and hydraulics involves utilizing formulas, and recognizing the relationship between the individual variables. Before the numbers are plugged into the formulas, it is often possible to analyze the variables in the system and come to a realization about what is happening. For example, look at the variables in the system and notice that the output piston is 20 times larger than the input piston, 5 in² compared to 1⁄4 in². That comparison tells us that the output force will be 20 times greater than the input force, and also that the output piston will only move 1⁄20 as far. Without doing any formula based calculations, we can conclude that the hydraulic system in question has a mechanical advantage of 20.

### Bernoulli’s Principle

Bernoulli’s principle was originally stated to explain the action of a liquid flowing through the varying cross-sectional areas of tubes.

¼ in² (20 in) = 5 in (distance)

Consider a tube in which the cross-sectional area gradually decreases to a minimum diameter in its center section. A tube constructed in this manner is called a “venturi,” or “venturi tube.” Where the cross-sectional area is decreasing, the passageway is referred to as a converging duct. As the passageway starts to spread out, it is referred to as a diverging duct.

As a liquid, or fluid, flows through the venturi tube, the gauges at points “A,” “B,” and “C” are positioned to register the velocity and the static pressure of the liquid. The venturi can be used to illustrate Bernoulli’s principle, which states that: The static pressure of a fluid, liquid or gas, decreases at points where the velocity of the fluid increases, provided no energy is added to nor taken away from the fluid. The velocity of the air is kinetic energy and the static pressure of the air is potential energy.

In the wide section of the venturi (points A and C), the liquid moves at low velocity, producing a high static pressure, as indicated by the pressure gauge. As the tube narrows in the center, it must contain the same volume of fluid as the two end areas. As indicated by the velocity gauge reading high and the pressure gauge reading low, in this narrow section, the liquid moves at a higher velocity, producing a lower pressure than that at points A and C. A good application for the use of the venturi principle is in a float-type carburetor. As the air flows through the carburetor on its way to the engine, it goes through a venturi, where the static pressure is reduced. The fuel in the carburetor, which is under a higher pressure, flows into the lower pressure venturi area and mixes with the air.

Bernoulli’s principle is extremely important in understanding how some of the systems used in aviation work, including how the wing of an airplane generates lift or why the inlet duct of a turbine engine on a subsonic airplane is diverging in shape. The wing on a slow-moving airplane has a curved top surface and a relatively flat bottom surface. The curved top surface acts like half of the converging shaped middle of a venturi. As the air flow over the top of the wing, the air speeds up, and its static pressure decreases. The static pressure on the bottom of the wing is now greater than the pressure on the top, and this pressure difference creates the lift on the wing. Bernoulli’s principle and the concept of lift on a wing are covered in greater depth in “Aircraft Theory of Flight” located in this chapter.

## 2. 中文翻译

按定义，流体是任何在没有受到限制或约束时能够流动的物质。液体和气体都归入流体一类，而且常常表现出非常相似的行为。当对这些流体施加力时，就出现了一个重要差别：液体倾向于不可压缩，而气体则高度可压缩。航空所依据的许多原理，例如机翼产生升力的理论和液压系统所产生的力，都可以借助流体力学定律加以解释和量化。

浸没在液体或气体中的固体，其重量比在自由空间中称量时要小。这是因为任何流体都会对浸没其中的物体施加一个向上的力，称为浮力。如果流体这一向上的力大于物体的重量，物体就会浮起来。密度大于流体的物体虽然很容易下沉，但浸没之后似乎失去了一部分重量。人在水下能举起的重量，比在空气中能举起的要大。

看下面这个实验。溢水桶一直注水到出水口。先称那块重金属立方体在静止空气中的重量，为 10 lb；然后把它完全浸没在水中称量，重量为 3 lb。这两个重量之差就是水的浮力。当立方体被放入溢水桶时，溢出的水被接水桶接住。溢出的水的体积等于立方体的体积。形状不规则的物体的体积也可以用这种方法测量。如果这个实验做得仔细，立方体所排开的水的重量恰好等于水的浮力，天平显示为 7 lb。

阿基米德（公元前 287—212 年）做过类似的实验。由此他发现：流体对浸没物体所施加的浮力，等于该物体所排开的流体的重量。这一表述称为阿基米德原理。这条原理适用于所有流体，气体和液体都一样。正如水会对浸没的物体产生浮力一样，空气也会对其中的物体产生浮力。

某物体所能获得的浮力大小，可用下列公式计算：

浮力 = 物体体积 × 所排开流体的密度。如果浮力大于物体的重量，物体就会浮起来；如果浮力小于物体的重量，物体就会下沉。对于下沉的物体，其可测得的重量会减少，减少的量等于所排开流体的重量。

例：把一个体积 10 ft³、重 700 lb 的物体放入纯水。该物体会浮起来吗？如果下沉，它在浸没状态下的可测重量是多少？如果浮起，它有多少立方英尺的体积在水面以下？

浮力 = 物体体积 × 所排开流体的密度 = 10 (62.4)

= 624 lb。由于浮力小于物体的重量，该物体会下沉。浮力与物体重量之差就是它的可测重量，即 76 lb。

浮力的两个很好的例子是充氦气的飞艇和装浮筒的水上飞机。飞艇能够浮在大气中，水上飞机能够浮在水面上，这说明两者的浮力都大于重量。以德哈维兰“双水獭”水上飞机为例，其最大起飞重量为 12,500 lb。这架飞机的浮筒至少必须大到能在水中排开与该机重量相等的重量。按照《联邦法规》第 14 编（14 CFR）第 23 部，浮筒必须比支撑该机所需的最小体积大 80%。对这架飞机来说，浮筒的必要尺寸可计算如下：

用飞机重量除以水的密度。12,500 ÷ 62.4 = 200.3 ft³。把这个体积乘以 80%。200.3 × 80% = 160.2 ft³。把这两个体积相加，就得到浮筒的总体积。

200.3 + 160.2 = 360.5 ft³。对“双水獭”来说，很明显浮筒的大部分体积都在水面以上。这一点是通过确保浮筒体积至少比所需的最小体积大 80% 来实现的。一些大型固特异飞艇的体积达 230,000 ft³。由于它们所浸没其中的流体是空气，……（原文此处数字被截断，无正文）

要计算飞艇所受的浮力，就把飞艇的体积乘以空气的密度（.07651 lb⁄ft³）。对于这艘固特异飞艇，浮力为 17,597 lb。

前气囊和后气囊是飞艇内部的空气腔。通过进气口，可以把空气泵入气囊，或从气囊中抽走，以控制飞艇的重量。控制飞艇的重量，也就控制了它具有多大的正升力或负升力。尽管飞艇被归类为轻于空气的航空器，实际上它是在略重于空气的状态下飞行的。

液体对容器底部产生的压力，取决于液体的高度，而与容器的形状无关。三个形状和大小不同的容器里都装满了染色水。尽管形状不同、液体体积也不同，但每一个的液柱高度都是 231 英寸。由于这一高度，每一个容器对底部产生的压力都是 8.34 psi。左边那个容器的表面积为 1 in²，所盛的体积为 231 in³（1 加仑）。1 加仑水重 8.34 lb，这就是底部压力为 8.34 psi 的原因。

还是回到这些容器：如果在半深处测量压力，读数将是 8.34 的一半，即 4.17 psi。换句话说，压力可以通过改变液柱高度来调节。以流体液柱高度为基础的压力称为静压力。对于汽油之类的液体，有时把它称为压头。例如，如果一台汽化器需要在其进口处获得 2 psi 的供油压力，即压头，可以把油箱放到比汽化器高出相应英寸数的位置来实现。

如上一段所述，由流体液柱高度产生的压力称为静压力。当流体处于运动状态、其速度转化为压力时，这种压力称为冲压。把冲压与静压力相加，所得结果称为全压。例如在燃气涡轮发动机的进气口中，常常测量全压，以便向燃油计量装置提供信号，或向驾驶舱内的某个仪表提供信号。

现代液压技术与气动技术的基础，是在 1653 年帕斯卡发现流体中所建立的压力向各个方向等值传递时奠定的。这个压力垂直于容器的表面作用。当流体中的压力仅由流体的高度造成时，在同一水平面上作用于容器壁的压力相等；但若把底部压力与半深处的压力相比，两者则不相等。关于流体中所建立的压力，以及它与作用在流体上的力、该力所作用的表面积之间的关系，这一概念就是帕斯卡定律。

如果在一个圆筒顶部装一只活塞，并用外力向下压活塞，液体中就会产生附加压力。如果附加压力为 100 psi，这 100 psi 就会从圆筒顶部一直到底部，均匀而毫不减弱地传递。

底部的压力表此时应读 108.34 psi；如果在圆筒半深处再装一只压力表，它的读数应为 104.17 psi，即用 100 加上 8.34 的一半求得。

帕斯卡定律在涉及力、压力和面积这三个变量时，是用下列公式来处理的。

力 = 压力 × 面积。在这个公式中，力的单位是磅，压力的单位是磅每平方英寸（psi），面积的单位是平方英寸。把原公式变形，还可以得到另外两个公式，如下：

压力 = 力 ÷ 面积，面积 = 力 ÷ 压力。记住帕斯卡定律的公式以及各变量之间的关系，有一种简单方便的办法，就是使用一个三角形。把要求解的变量盖住，剩下两个变量的位置就显示出正确的数学关系。例如，若把“A”（面积）盖住，剩下的就是上面的“F”和下面的“P”，即力除以压力。

这套液压系统比前一套略复杂一些。5 lb 的输入力作用在 1⁄2 in² 的活塞上，产生 10 psi 的压力。输入圆筒与活塞同第二个圆筒相连，该圆筒内装有 5 in² 的活塞。输入活塞产生的 10 psi 压力推动第二个圆筒中的活塞，产生 50 磅的输出力。

液压系统的目的，常常就是要用小得多的输入力产生很大的输出力。输入力为 5 lb，输出力为 50 lb，即大 10 倍。输出力与输入力之间的关系，如本章前面所述，称为机械效益。机械效益为 50 除以 5，即 10。下列公式可用来计算机械效益。

机械效益 = 输出力 ÷ 输入力，或 机械效益 = 输出距离 ÷ 输入距离。本章前面讨论杠杆和齿轮等简单机械时曾指出，没有任何机械能让我们获得功。同样的说法对液压系统也成立：从液压系统中得到的功，不会多于我们输入的功。由于功等于力乘距离，所以如果液压系统使我们获得了力，我们就必然失去距离。只有在系统效率为 100% 时，输出的功才与输入相同。

压力 = 力 ÷ 面积 = 50 ÷ 1⁄4 = 200 psi　力 = 压力 × 面积 = 200 × 15 = 3,000 lb　机械效益 = 输出力 ÷ 输入力 = 3,000 ÷ 50 = 60　输入活塞面积（移动距离）= 输出活塞面积（移动距离）

1⁄4 (30) = 15（移动距离）

1⁄4 (30) ÷ 15 = 移动距离　移动距离 = 1⁄2 in。理解帕斯卡定律和液压技术的一部分工作，就在于运用公式并认清各个变量之间的关系。在把数字代入公式之前，往往可以先分析系统中的变量，从而认识到正在发生什么。例如，看系统中的变量会发现，输出活塞比输入活塞大 20 倍，即 5 in² 对 1⁄4 in²。这一比较告诉我们，输出力将是输入力的 20 倍，同时输出活塞的移动距离只有输入活塞的 1/20。完全不做基于公式的计算，我们也可以断定，所讨论的这套液压系统的机械效益为 20。

### 伯努利原理

伯努利原理最初是为了解释液体流经截面积变化的管道时的行为而提出的。

¼ in² (20 in) = 5 in（距离）

有一段管道，其截面积逐渐减小，在中段达到最小直径。这样构造的管道称为“文丘里管”，或称“文丘里”。截面积逐渐减小的那一段通道称为收敛管；通道开始扩大的那一段，则称为扩张管。

当液体或流体流经文丘里管时，A、B、C 三点处的仪表被安置用于显示液体的速度和静压力。文丘里管可用来说明伯努利原理，该原理表述为：流体（液体或气体）的静压力在其速度增大处减小，条件是流体既没有获得能量，也没有失去能量。空气的速度即动能，空气的静压力即势能。

在文丘里管的宽段（A 点和 C 点），液体低速流动，产生较高的静压力，压力表即显示这一点。当管道在中段收窄时，其中必须仍容纳与两端相同体积的流体。速度表读数高、压力表读数低，表明在这一狭窄段液体流速更高，产生的压力低于 A 点和 C 点处的压力。文丘里原理的一个很好的应用实例是浮子式汽化器。空气在通往发动机的途中流经汽化器时，要穿过一段文丘里管，那里的静压力降低。汽化器中处于较高压力下的燃油便流入压力较低的文丘里区，与空气混合。

伯努利原理对于理解航空中某些系统的工作方式极为重要，其中包括机翼如何产生升力，以及亚音速飞机上燃气涡轮发动机的进气道为什么做成扩张形。速度较慢的飞机，机翼上表面是弯曲的，下表面则相对平坦。弯曲的上表面如同文丘里管收缩中段的上半部分。气流经过机翼上表面时加速，其静压力下降。此时机翼下表面的静压力高于上表面，这一压力差就使机翼产生升力。伯努利原理以及机翼升力的概念，在本章“航空器飞行理论”一节中有更深入的讲解。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 过去分词短语作后置定语 + 比较从句的省略

**原句**：A solid body submerged in a liquid or a gas weighs less than when weighed in free space.

**拆解**：主干是 A solid body weighs less；submerged in a liquid or a gas 为过去分词短语，作后置定语修饰 body，相当于 which is submerged …；than 之后是 when weighed in free space，为比较状语从句的省略形式，完整说法是 than it does when it is weighed in free space。

**要点**：than 后的从句常只保留与前面不同的部分，省略重复的主语和谓语；翻译时要补足“比在自由空间中称量时”这层比较对象，否则句子读不通。

### ② called 引出的插入式定语 + which 定语从句

**原句**：This is because of the upward force, called buoyant force, which any fluid exerts on a body submerged in it.

**拆解**：This is because of … 中 because of 是介词短语，后接名词 the upward force，不接从句；called buoyant force 为过去分词短语，插在 force 与 which 之间作补充说明；which any fluid exerts on a body submerged in it 为定语从句，修饰 the upward force，其中 submerged in it 又是修饰 body 的后置定语，it 指代前面的 fluid。

**要点**：because of 与 because 分工不同，前者只能接名词或名词短语；called … 这种插入定语翻译时常处理成“称为……”的独立小句，避免定语层层嵌套。

### ③ 被动语态 + 并列介词短语的否定

**原句**：The pressure exerted on the bottom of a container by a liquid is determined by the height of the liquid and not by the shape of the container.

**拆解**：主语 The pressure 后接过去分词短语 exerted on the bottom of a container by a liquid 作后置定语；谓语 is determined by 引出方式；and not by the shape of the container 与前面的 by the height of the liquid 并列，构成“由……决定，而不是由……决定”的否定并列。

**要点**：and not 连接的是两个介词短语，instead of / rather than 也常用于这类对比；翻译宜重复“而是不……”以保持两个依据的对照关系。

### ④ just as 引导的方式比较状语从句

**原句**：Just as water exerts a buoyant force on submerged objects, air exerts a buoyant force on objects submerged in it.

**拆解**：Just as 引导方式状语从句，句意为“正如……一样”，位于句首并用逗号与主句隔开；主句 air exerts a buoyant force on objects submerged in it 与从句结构完全平行，submerged in it 为过去分词短语修饰 objects，it 指 air。

**要点**：just as 表类比而非原因，不可译成“因为”；前后两个分句的主语不同（water 与 air），这种平行结构要把后半句的重心译出来。

### ⑤ 省略关系代词的定语从句 + 介词后置

**原句**：If the variable we want to solve for is covered up, the position of the remaining two variables shows the proper math relationship.

**拆解**：if 从句的主语是 the variable，其后 we want to solve for 是省略了关系代词 that/which 的定语从句（solve for 为固定搭配，介词 for 留在句末）；is covered up 才是从句的谓语，故从句主语与谓语被定语从句隔开。主句主语 the position of the remaining two variables，谓语 shows。

**要点**：关系代词作宾语时可省略，此时容易把定语从句中的动词误认作主句谓语；遇到“名词 + 主谓 + 谓语”的结构，先找真正的谓语。

### ⑥ when 时间状语从句 + if 条件从句的被动语态

**原句**：When the pressure in the fluid is caused solely by the fluid’s height, the pressure against the walls of the container is equal at any given level, but it is not equal if the pressure at the bottom is compared to the pressure half way down.

**拆解**：When 引导时间状语从句，用被动 is caused by 表示来源；主句由 but 连接两个并列分句：the pressure … is equal at any given level 与 it is not equal if …；后一分句中 if 引导条件状语从句，含被动 is compared to。

**要点**：at any given level（同一水平面上）与 half way down（半深处）是理解本句的关键对照；be compared to 此处是“与……相比”，不是“被比作”。
