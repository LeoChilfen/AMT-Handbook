## 1. 原文

**Requirements for Fire to Occur**

Three things are required for a fire. Remove any one of these things and the fire extinguishes:

1. Fuel—combines with oxygen in the presence of heat, releasing more heat. As a result, it reduces itself to other chemical compounds.

2. Heat—accelerates the combining of oxygen with fuel, in turn releasing more heat.

3. Oxygen—the element that combines chemically with another substance through the process of oxidation. Rapid oxidation, accompanied by a noticeable release of heat and light, is called combustion or burning. [Figure 1-3]

**Classification of Fires**

For commercial purposes, the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) has classified fires into three basic types: Class A, Class B, and Class C.

1. Class A fires involve ordinary combustible materials, such as wood, cloth, paper, upholstery materials, and so forth.

2. Class B fires involve flammable petroleum products or other flammable or combustible liquids, greases, solvents, paints, and so forth.

3. Class C fires involve energized electrical wiring and equipment.

A fourth class of fire, the Class D fire, involves flammable metal. Class D fires are not commercially considered by the NFPA to be a basic type of fire since they are caused by a Class A, B, or C fire. Usually Class D fires involve magnesium in the shop, or in aircraft wheels and brakes, or are the result of improper welding operations.

Any one of these fires can occur during maintenance on or around, or operations involving aircraft. There is a particular type of extinguisher that is most effective for each type of fire.

**Types and Operation of Shop and Flight Line Fire Extinguishers**

Water extinguishers are the best type to use on Class A fires. Water has two effects on fire. It deprives fire of oxygen and cools the material being burned. Since most petroleum products float on water, water-type fire extinguishers are not recommended for Class B fires. Extreme caution must be used when fighting electrical fires (Class C) with water-type extinguishers. All electrical power must be removed or shut off to the burning area. Additionally, residual electricity in capacitors, coils, and so forth must be considered to prevent severe injury or possibly death from electrical shock. Never use water-type fire extinguishers on Class D fires. The cooling effect of water causes an explosive expansion of the metal, because metals burn at extremely high temperatures.

Water fire extinguishers are operated in a variety of ways. Some are hand pumped, while others are pressurized. The pressurized types of extinguishers may have a gas charge stored in the container with the water, or it may contain a “soda-acid” container where acid is spilled into a container of soda inside the extinguisher. The chemical reaction of the soda and the acid causes pressure to build inside the fire extinguisher, forcing the water out.

Carbon dioxide (CO2) extinguishers are used for Class A, B, and C fires, extinguishing the fire by depriving it of oxygen. [Figure 1-4] Additionally, like water-type extinguishers, CO2 cools the burning material. Never use CO2 on Class D fires. As with water extinguishers, the cooling effect of CO 2 on the hot metal can cause explosive expansion of the metal. When using CO 2 fire extinguishers, all parts of the extinguisher can become extremely cold, and remain so for a short time after operation. Wear protective equipment or take other precautions to prevent cold injury, such as frostbite. Extreme caution must be used when operating CO2 fire extinguishers in closed or confined areas. Not only can the fire be deprived of oxygen, but so too can the operator.

CO2 fire extinguishers generally use the self-expelling method of operation. This means that the CO2 has sufficient pressure at normal operating pressure to expel itself. Halogenated hydrocarbon extinguishers are most effective on Class B and C fires. They can be used on Class A and D fires, but they are less effective. Halogenated hydrocarbon, commonly called Freon™ by the industry, are numbered according to chemical formulas with Halon™ numbers.

Carbon tetrachloride (Halon 104), chemical formula CCl 4, has an Underwriters Laboratory (UL) toxicity rating of 3. As such, it is extremely toxic. [Figure 1-6] Hydrochloric acid vapor, chlorine, and phosgene gas are produced whenever carbon tetrachloride is used on ordinary fires. The amount of phosgene gas is increased whenever carbon tetrachloride is brought in direct contact with hot metal, certain chemicals, or continuing electrical arcs. It is not approved for any fire extinguishing use. Old containers of Halon 104 found in or around shops or hangars should be disposed of in accordance with Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) regulations and local laws and ordinances.

Methyl bromide (Halon 1001), chemical formula CH 3Br, is a liquefied gas with a UL toxicity rating of 2. It is very toxic and corrosive to aluminum alloys, magnesium, and zinc. Halon 1001 is not recommended for aircraft use. Chlorobromomethane (Halon 1011), chemical formula CH2ClBr, is a liquefied gas with a UL toxicity rating of 3. Like methyl bromide, Halon 1011 is not recommended for aircraft use. Dibromodifluoromethane (Halon 1202), chemical formula CBr2F2, has a UL toxicity rating of 4. Halon 1202 is not recommended for aircraft use.

Bromochlorodifluoromethane (Halon 1211), chemical formula CBrClF2, is a liquefied gas with a UL toxicity rating of 5. It is colorless, noncorrosive, and evaporates rapidly leaving no residue. It does not freeze or cause cold burns and does not harm fabrics, metals, or other materials it contacts. Halon 1211 acts rapidly on fires by producing a heavy blanketing mist that eliminates oxygen from the fire source. More importantly, it interferes chemically with the combustion process of the fire. Furthermore, it has outstanding properties in preventing re- flash after the fire has been extinguished.

Bromotrifluoromethane (Halon 1301), chemical formula CF3Br, is also a liquefied gas and has a UL toxicity rating of 6. It has all the characteristics of Halon 1211. The significant difference between the two is Halon 1211 forms a spray similar to CO2, while Halon 1301 has a vapor spray that is more difficult to direct.

Note: The EPA has restricted Halon to its 1986 production level due to its effect on the ozone layer.

Dry powder extinguishers, while effective on Class B and C fires, are best for use on Class D fires. The method of operation of dry powder fire extinguishers varies from gas cartridge charges, stored pressure within the container that forces the powder charge out of the container, to scooping pails or buckets of the powder from large containers or barrels to toss on the fire.

**Inspection of Fire Extinguishers**

Fire extinguishers need to be checked periodically utilizing a checklist. If a checklist is unavailable, check the following as a minimum:

• Proper location of appropriate extinguisher

• Safety seals unbroken

• All external dirt and rust removed

• Gauge or indicator in operable range

• No nozzle obstruction

• No obvious damage Airport or other local fire departments can usually help in preparing or providing extinguisher checklists. In addition, these fire departments can be helpful in answering questions and assisting in obtaining repairs to or replacement of fire extinguishers.

**Identifying Fire Extinguishers**

Fire extinguishers are marked to indicate suitability for a particular class of fire. The markings on Figure 1-7 must be placed on the fire extinguisher and in a conspicuous place in the vicinity of the fire extinguisher. When the location is marked, however, take extreme care to ensure that the fire extinguisher kept at that location is in fact the type depicted by the marking. In other words, if a location is marked for a Class B fire extinguisher, ensure that the fire extinguisher in that location is in fact suitable for Class B fires.

Markings must be applied by decalcomanias (decals), painting, or similar methods. They are to be legible and as durable as necessary for the location. For example, markings used outside need to be more durable than those in the hangar or office spaces.

When markings are applied to the extinguisher, they are placed on the front of the shell, if one is installed, above or below the extinguisher nameplate. Markings must be large enough and in a form that is easily seen and identifiable by the average person with average eyesight at a distance of at least 3 feet. When markings are applied to wall panels, and so forth, in the vicinity of extinguishers, they must be large enough and in a form that is easily seen and identifiable by the average person with average eyesight at a distance of at least 25 feet. [Figure 1-8]

**Using Fire Extinguishers**

When using a fire extinguisher, ensure the correct type is used for the fire. Most extinguishers have a pin to pull that allows the handle to activate the agent. Stand back 8 feet and aim at the base of the fire or flames. Squeeze the lever and sweep side to side until the fire is extinguished.

## 2. 中文翻译

**火灾发生的条件**

火灾需要三个要素。去掉其中任何一个，火就会熄灭：

1. 燃料——在有热量存在的条件下与氧气结合，释放出更多热量。其结果是，燃料自身转化为其他化合物。

2. 热量——加速氧气与燃料的结合，进而释放出更多热量。

3. 氧气——通过氧化过程与其他物质发生化学结合的元素。伴随明显放热和发光的快速氧化称为燃烧（combustion，亦即 burning）。[图 1-3]

**火灾的分类**

为便于商业应用，美国国家消防协会（NFPA）将火灾分为三种基本类型：A 类、B 类和 C 类。

1. A 类火涉及普通可燃材料，如木材、布料、纸张、内饰材料等。

2. B 类火涉及易燃石油制品或其他易燃或可燃液体、油脂、溶剂、油漆等。

3. C 类火涉及带电的电气线路和设备。

第四类火灾是 D 类火，涉及可燃金属。由于 D 类火是由 A、B、C 类火引起的，NFPA 在商业分类上不把 D 类火视为一种基本火灾类型。D 类火通常涉及车间中的镁，或飞机机轮和刹车中的镁，也可能是焊接操作不当造成的。

上述任何一种火灾，都可能在飞机上或飞机周围进行维修时、在涉及飞机的作业中发生。每一类火灾都有一种最有效的灭火器类型。

**车间与停机坪灭火器的类型和操作**

水型灭火器是扑救 A 类火的最佳选择。水对火有两种作用：它使火隔绝氧气，并冷却正在燃烧的材料。由于大多数石油制品会浮在水面上，因此不推荐用水型灭火器扑救 B 类火。用水型灭火器扑救电气火灾（C 类）时必须极其谨慎：必须先切断或关闭通往燃烧区域的全部电源。此外，还必须考虑电容器、线圈等设备中的残余电荷，以免因电击造成重伤甚至死亡。绝不要用水型灭火器扑救 D 类火。因为金属燃烧时温度极高，水的冷却作用会使金属发生爆炸性膨胀。

水型灭火器的操作方式多种多样。有些靠手动泵压，有些则是储压式。储压式灭火器可能把压缩气体与水一同封存在容器内，也可能装有一个“酸碱”式容器，其中的酸被倾入灭火器内部的碳酸氢钠（soda）容器。碳酸氢钠与酸发生化学反应后在灭火器内产生压力，把水压出。

二氧化碳（CO2）灭火器用于扑救 A、B、C 类火，通过使火隔绝氧气来灭火。[图 1-4] 此外，与水型灭火器一样，CO2 也能冷却燃烧的材料。绝不要用 CO2 扑救 D 类火。与水的道理相同，CO2 对灼热金属的冷却作用会使金属发生爆炸性膨胀。使用 CO2 灭火器时，灭火器的所有部件都会变得极冷，并在操作结束后短时间内保持低温。应穿戴防护装备或采取其他预防措施，防止冻伤等低温伤害。在封闭或狭窄区域操作 CO2 灭火器时必须极其谨慎：不仅火可能缺氧，操作者本人也可能缺氧。

CO2 灭火器一般采用自喷（self-expelling）式工作方式，即 CO2 在其正常工作压力下具有足以自行喷出的压力。（原文此处混入了一处图注残片：“This It takes three things to start a fire: OXYGEN, HEAT, FUEL Oxygen Friction (heat)Fuel”，系 PDF 抽取时图示文字错位进入正文，此处如实标注，不作增补。）容器内的压力由某种密封件或爆破片维持，该密封件或爆破片由击发机构（通常是一根销）打碎或刺破。这意味着，一旦密封件或爆破片被破坏，容器内的压力即被释放，灭火器随之报废，需要更换。[图 1-5]

卤代烃灭火器对 B 类和 C 类火最为有效。它们也可用于 A 类和 D 类火，但效果较差。卤代烃在工业界通常称为 Freon™（氟利昂），按化学式以 Halon™（哈龙）编号命名。

四氯化碳（Halon 104），化学式 CCl 4，其 Underwriters Laboratory（UL，保险商实验室）毒性等级为 3 级。正因如此，它毒性极强。[图 1-6] 只要用四氯化碳扑救普通火灾，就会产生氯化氢酸雾、氯气和光气。当四氯化碳直接接触灼热金属、某些化学品或持续电弧时，光气的生成量还会增加。该灭火剂未被批准用于任何灭火用途。在车间或机库内及其附近发现的老旧 Halon 104 容器，应按美国环境保护署（EPA）法规以及当地法律和条例处置。

溴甲烷（Halon 1001），化学式 CH 3Br，是一种液化气体，UL 毒性等级为 2 级。它毒性很强，并对铝合金、镁和锌有腐蚀性。Halon 1001 不建议用于飞机。溴氯甲烷（Chlorobromomethane，Halon 1011），化学式 CH2ClBr，是一种液化气体，UL 毒性等级为 3 级。与溴甲烷一样，Halon 1011 不建议用于飞机。二溴二氟甲烷（Halon 1202），化学式 CBr2F2，UL 毒性等级为 4 级。Halon 1202 不建议用于飞机。

溴氯二氟甲烷（Halon 1211），化学式 CBrClF2，是一种液化气体，UL 毒性等级为 5 级。它无色、无腐蚀性，且蒸发迅速、不留残渣。它不会冻结或造成冷灼伤，也不会损害其接触到的织物、金属或其他材料。Halon 1211 通过产生一层厚重的覆盖雾、使火源隔绝氧气而迅速灭火。更重要的是，它还能从化学上干扰火的燃烧过程。此外，它在火被扑灭后防止复燃方面性能卓越。

溴三氟甲烷（Halon 1301），化学式 CF3Br，同样是一种液化气体，UL 毒性等级为 6 级。它具备 Halon 1211 的全部特性。两者最显著的区别在于：Halon 1211 喷出的是类似 CO2 的雾状喷射流，而 Halon 1301 喷出的是更难定向的蒸气雾。

**灭火器的检查**

灭火器需要借助检查单定期检查。如果手头没有检查单，至少应检查以下各项：

• 合适类型的灭火器放置在规定位置

• 安全封条完好未破

• 外部污垢和锈迹已清除

• 压力表或指示器处于可用范围

• 喷嘴无堵塞

• 无明显损坏。机场消防部门或其他当地消防部门通常可以协助编制或提供灭火器检查单。此外，这些消防部门还能帮助解答问题，并协助安排灭火器的修理或更换。

**灭火器的标识**

灭火器上标有标识，用以表明其适用于特定类别的火灾。图 1-7 中的标识必须贴在灭火器上，并贴在灭火器附近醒目的位置。不过，在给某个位置贴标识时，务必格外小心，确保该位置实际配备的灭火器正是标识所示的那种类型。换言之，如果某个位置标的是 B 类灭火器，就要确保该位置配备的灭火器确实适用于 B 类火。

标识必须用贴花（decalcomania，即贴纸）、喷漆或类似方法施加。标识应清晰可辨，并具有与所处位置相适应的耐久性。例如，室外使用的标识需要比机库或办公区域内的标识更耐久。

标识贴在灭火器上时，若灭火器装有外壳，应贴在壳体正面，即灭火器铭牌的上方或下方。标识必须足够大，且形式应便于视力正常的普通人在至少 3 英尺的距离上轻松看清并辨认。标识贴在灭火器附近的墙板等位置时，必须足够大，且形式应便于视力正常的普通人在至少 25 英尺的距离上轻松看清并辨认。[图 1-8]

**灭火器的使用**

使用灭火器时，要确保所用类型与火灾类型相符。大多数灭火器都有一根需要拔出的保险销，拔销后手柄才能启动灭火剂。退到 8 英尺以外，对准火焰或火的根部。握紧压把，左右扫射，直到火被扑灭。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 祈使句 + and 表示条件与结果

**原句**：Remove any one of these things and the fire extinguishes:
**拆解**：Remove any one of these things 为肯定祈使句（省略主语 you）；and 连接后面的陈述句 the fire extinguishes；整体等于 “If you remove any one of these things, the fire will extinguish.”；句末冒号引出下面三条具体条件。
**要点**：祈使句 + and + 陈述句是表示“做某事，就会……”的固定句型，and 前相当于条件从句；否定条件用 or（Hurry up, or you will be late.）。科技说明文常用它把“操作—结果”压进一句，比 if 从句更简洁；注意后一分句用一般现在时表将来。

### ② since 引导原因状语从句 + consider...to be 的被动式

**原句**：Class D fires are not commercially considered by the NFPA to be a basic type of fire since they are caused by a Class A, B, or C fire.
**拆解**：主语 Class D fires + 谓语 are not considered（一般现在时被动）+ 插入副词 commercially + 施动者 by the NFPA + to be a basic type of fire（consider A (to be) B 变为被动后的主语补足语）+ since 引导原因状语从句（they are caused by…，亦为被动）。
**要点**：consider 的被动式后接 to be 或直接接名词，即 A is considered (to be) B；sb./sth. is considered to be… 是技术文献中的高频被动结构。since 在此表原因（“既然、由于”），语气比 because 略正式，且 since 从句与主句不能用 so 呼应。

### ③ 情态动词 must 的被动语态 + 后置定语

**原句**：All electrical power must be removed or shut off to the burning area.
**拆解**：主语 All electrical power + 后置定语 to the burning area（通往燃烧区域的电源）+ 谓语 must be removed or shut off（情态动词 must + be + 两个并列的过去分词，构成被动）。
**要点**：must be + 过去分词是手册、规程中最常见的强制性被动，表示“必须被……”，施动者 by… 常省略，因为执行者不言自明（维修人员）。易错点：情态动词后必须用 be，不能写成 must removed；翻译时宜补出“由人”或直接译成“必须切断……”。

### ④ once 引导的时间状语从句 + 现在分词作结果状语

**原句**：This means that once the seal or disk is broken, pressure in the container is released and the fire extinguisher is spent, requiring replacement.
**拆解**：This means + that 引导宾语从句；宾语从句中 once the seal or disk is broken 为时间状语从句（被动）前置，主句是 pressure in the container is released and the fire extinguisher is spent 两个被动分句并列；句末 requiring replacement 为现在分词短语作结果状语，相当于 and this requires replacement。
**要点**：once 作连词意为“一旦……”，从句用一般现在时表将来；现在分词短语表自然结果时，其逻辑主语应与主句主语一致，此处指前面整件事。spent 在此不是“花费”，而是“用尽、报废”，属熟词僻义。

### ⑤ enough 后置 + that 定语从句 + 多重后置定语

**原句**：When markings are applied to wall panels, and so forth, in the vicinity of extinguishers, they must be large enough and in a form that is easily seen and identifiable by the average person with average eyesight at a distance of at least 25 feet.
**拆解**：When 引导时间状语从句（markings are applied to wall panels…，被动，且被插入语 and so forth 打断）在前；主句为 they must be large enough and in a form（they 指 markings，谓语 must be + 并列表语 large enough 与介词短语 in a form；形容词后的 enough 属后置的限定词）+ that 引导定语从句修饰 form（that is easily seen and identifiable by the average person，被动）+ with average eyesight（介词短语后置修饰 person）+ at a distance of at least 25 feet（介词短语说明辨认距离，修饰 seen/identifiable）。
**要点**：形容词 + enough 中 enough 必须后置，不能说 enough large；“in a form that…”是描述“形式、规格”要求的常用搭配。多个介词短语连续后置时，汉语习惯把它们前置成“在至少 25 英尺的距离上、视力正常的普通人”；注意 identifiable 与 seen 共用同一个 by 引导的施动者。
