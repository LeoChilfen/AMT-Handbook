# Weight & Balance Terminology

## 1. 原文

The datum is an imaginary vertical plane from which all horizontal measurements are taken for balance purposes, with the aircraft in level flight attitude. If the datum is viewed on a drawing of an aircraft, it would appear as a vertical line that is perpendicular (90 degrees) to the aircraft’s longitudinal axis. For each aircraft make and model, the location of all items is identified in reference to the datum. For example, the fuel in a tank might be 60 inches (60") behind the datum, and a radio on the flight deck might be 90" forward of the datum.

The datum is determined by the manufacturer; it is often the leading edge of the wing or some specific distance from an easily identified location. Typical locations for the datum are the aircraft nose, the leading edge of the wing, the helicopter’s mast, or a specified distance from a known point. However, most modern helicopters, like airplanes, have the datum located at the nose of the aircraft or a specified distance ahead of it. The distance from this datum is measured in inches and can be either positive or negative depending upon where the equipment is located in relation to the datum.

The location of the datum is identified in the Aircraft Specifications or Type Certificate Data Sheet (TCDS). Aircraft certified prior to 1958 fell under the Civil Aeronautics Administration and had their weight and balance information contained in a document known as Aircraft Specifications. Aircraft certified since 1958 fall under the FAA and have their weight and balance information contained in a document known as a Type Certificate Data Sheet (TCDS). The Aircraft Specifications typically included the aircraft equipment list. For aircraft with a TCDS, the equipment list is a separate document.

The arm is the horizontal distance from the datum to any point within the aircraft. The arm’s distance is always measured in inches, and it is preceded by the algebraic sign for positive (+) or negative (−), except for a location which might be exactly on the datum. The positive sign indicates an item is located aft of the datum, and the negative sign indicates an item is located forward of the datum. If the manufacturer chooses a datum that is at the most forward location on an aircraft, all the arms will be positive numbers. Location of the datum at any other point on the aircraft results in some arms being positive numbers, or aft of the datum, and some arms being negative numbers, or forward of the datum. For this aircraft, any item (fuel, seat, radio, etc.) located forward of the wing leading edge has a negative arm, and any item located aft of the wing leading edge has a positive arm. If an item is located exactly at the wing leading edge, its arm would be zero, and mathematically it would not matter whether its arm was positive or negative.

The arm of each item is usually included in parentheses immediately after the item’s name or weight in the Aircraft Specifications, TCDS, or equipment list for the aircraft. For example, in a TCDS, the fuel quantity might be identified as 98 gallons (gal) (+93.6) and the forward baggage limit as 100 pounds (lb) (–22.5). These numbers indicate that the fuel is located 93.6" aft of the datum and the nose baggage is located 22.6" forward of the datum. If the arm for a piece of equipment is not known, its exact location must be accurately measured. When the arm for a piece of equipment is being determined, the measurement is taken from the datum to the piece of equipment’s own CG.

To understand balance, it is necessary to have a working knowledge of the principle of moments. For those unfamiliar with weight and balance terms, the word moment is the product of a force or weight times a distance. The distance used in calculating a moment is referred to as the arm or moment arm and is usually expressed in inches. To calculate a moment, a force (or weight) and a distance must be known. The weight is multiplied by the distance from the datum and the result is the moment, which is expressed in inch-pounds (in-lb), a point through which the force acts. The algebraic sign of the moment, based on the datum location and whether weight is being installed or removed, would be as follows:

• Weight being added aft of the datum produces a positive moment (+weight, +arm).

• Weight being added forward of the datum produces a negative moment (+weight, −arm).

• Weight being removed aft of the datum produces a negative moment (−weight, +arm).

• Weight being removed forward of the datum produces a positive moment (−weight, −arm).

When dealing with positive and negative numbers, remember that the product of like signs produces a positive answer, and the product of unlike signs produces a negative answer.

### Center of Gravity (CG)

The CG is the point at which all the weight of the aircraft is concentrated and balanced; therefore, the aircraft can be supported at that point (the CG). The magnitude of the nose-heavy and tail-heavy moments are exactly equal. It is the balance point for the aircraft and, if suspended from this point, there would be no tendency to rotate in a noseup or nosedown attitude.

Consider a lever with the pivot point (called a fulcrum) located at the CG for the lever. Even though the weights on either side of the fulcrum are not equal, and the distances from each weight to the fulcrum are not equal, the product of the weights and arms (moments) are equal, and that is what produces a balanced condition. Therefore, the lever would be balanced much like two persons sitting on a seesaw who are differing weights and located at different distances from the fulcrum.

The maximum weight is the maximum authorized weight of the aircraft and its contents, and is indicated in the Aircraft Specifications or TCDS. For many aircraft, there are variations to the maximum allowable weight depending on the purpose and conditions under which the aircraft is to be flown. For example, a certain aircraft may be allowed a maximum gross weight of 2,750 lb when flown in the normal category, but when flown in the utility category, which allows for limited aerobatics, the same aircraft’s maximum allowable gross weight might only be 2,175 lb. There are other variations when dealing with the concept of maximum weight, as follows:

• Maximum Ramp Weight—the heaviest weight to which an aircraft can be loaded while it is sitting on the ground. This is sometimes referred to as the maximum taxi weight.

• Maximum Takeoff Weight—the heaviest weight an aircraft can be when it starts the takeoff roll. The difference between this weight and the maximum ramp weight would equal the weight of the fuel that would be consumed prior to takeoff.

• Maximum Landing Weight—the heaviest weight an aircraft can be when it lands. For large, wide body commercial airplanes, it can be 100,000 lb less than maximum takeoff weight, or even more.

• Maximum Zero Fuel Weight—the heaviest weight an aircraft can be loaded to without having any usable fuel in the fuel tanks. Any weight loaded above this value must be in the form of fuel.

The empty weight of an aircraft includes all operating equipment that has a fixed location and is actually installed in the aircraft. It includes the weight of the airframe, powerplant, required equipment, optional or special equipment, fixed ballast, hydraulic fluid, and residual fuel and oil. Residual fuel and oil are the fluids that do not normally drain out because they are trapped in the fuel lines, oil lines, and tanks. They must be included in the aircraft’s empty weight. For most aircraft certified after 1978, the full capacity of the engine oil system is also included in the empty weight. Information regarding residual fluids in aircraft systems that must be included in the empty weight, and whether or not full oil is included, will be indicated in the Aircraft Specifications or TCDS.

Other terms that are used when describing empty weight include basic empty weight, licensed empty weight, and standard empty weight. The term “basic empty weight” applies when the full capacity of the engine oil system is included in the value. The term “licensed empty weight” applies when only the weight of residual oil is included in the value, so it generally involves only aircraft certified prior to 1978. Standard empty weight would be a value supplied by the aircraft manufacturer, and it would not include any optional equipment that might be installed in an aircraft. For most people working in the aviation maintenance field, the basic empty weight of the aircraft is the most important one.

### Empty Weight Center of Gravity (EWCG)

The EWCG for an aircraft is the point at which it balances when it is in an empty weight condition. The concepts of empty weight and CG were discussed earlier in this chapter, and now they are being combined into a single concept. One of the most important reasons for weighing an aircraft is to determine its EWCG. All other weight and balance calculations, including loading the aircraft for flight, performing an equipment change calculation, and performing an adverse condition check, begin with knowing the empty weight and EWCG. This crucial information is part of what is contained in the aircraft weight and balance report.

To determine the useful load of an aircraft, subtract the empty weight from the maximum allowable gross weight. For aircraft certificated in both normal and utility categories, there may be two useful loads listed in the aircraft weight and balance records. An aircraft with an empty weight of 3,100 lb may have a useful load of 850 lb, if the normal category maximum weight is listed as 3,950 lb. When the aircraft is operated in the utility category, the maximum gross weight may be reduced to 3,700 lb, with a corresponding decrease in the useful load to 600 lb. Some aircraft have the same useful load regardless of the category in which they are certificated.

The useful load consists of fuel, any other fluids that are not part of empty weight, passengers, baggage, pilot, copilot, and crewmembers. Whether the weight of engine oil is considered part of the useful load depends on when the aircraft was certificated and can be determined by looking at the Aircraft Specifications or TCDS. The payload of an aircraft is like the useful load, except it does not include fuel.

A reduction in the weight of an item, where possible, may be necessary to remain within the maximum weight allowed for the category in which an aircraft is operating. Determining the distribution of these weights is called a weight check.

Many modern aircraft have multiple rows of seats and often more than one baggage compartment. The weight and balance extreme conditions represent the maximum forward and rearward CG position for the aircraft. An aircraft has certain fixed points, fore and aft, beyond which the CG should not be permitted at any time during flight. A check should be made to ensure that the CG will not shift out of limits when crew, passengers, cargo, and expendable weights are added or removed. If the limits are exceeded and the aircraft is flown in this condition, it may lead to insufficient stability, with resulting difficulty in controlling the aircraft. After any repair or alteration that changes the weight and balance, the Airframe and Powerplant (A&P) mechanic or repairman must ensure that no legal condition of loading can move the CG outside of its allowable limits. To determine this, the mechanic will deliberately attempt to calculate the aircraft loading in such a manner as to place the CG outside the limits of the aircraft. This is called an adverse-loading check.

For example, in a forward adverse-loaded CG check, all useful load in front of the forward CG limit is loaded, and all useful load behind this limit is left empty. An exception to leaving it empty is the fuel tank. If the fuel tank is located behind the forward CG limit, it cannot be left empty because the aircraft cannot fly without fuel. In this case, an amount of fuel is accounted for, which is known as minimum fuel. Minimum fuel is the amount needed for 30 minutes of flight at cruise power. For weight and balance purposes, the minimum fuel is no more than the quantity needed for one half hour of operation at rated maximum continuous power. This is 1⁄12 gallon for each maximum except takeoff (METO) horsepower (hp). Because aviation gasoline (Avgas) weighs 6 pounds per gallon (lb/gal), determine the number of pounds of the minimum fuel by dividing the METO hp by 2. For instance, an aircraft having a METO hp of 200 hp will have a minimum fuel of 16.65 gallons or 99.99 pounds. An even simpler way is to take the METO hp divided by 2, which is 100 pounds. Both methods in determining minimum fuel are valued and result in essentially the same answer. In the latter computation, a piston engine in cruise flight burns 1 lb of fuel per hour for each hp, or 1⁄2 lb for 30 minutes, hence dividing the METO by 2. For example, if a forward adverse-loaded CG check was performed on a piston engine aircraft, with the engine having a METO hp of 200, the minimum fuel would be 100 lb (200 METO hp ÷ 2). For turbine engine-powered aircraft, minimum fuel is not based on engine hp. If an adverse-loaded CG check is being performed on a turbine engine-powered aircraft, the aircraft manufacturer would need to supply information on minimum fuel.

When aircraft are placed on scales and weighed, it is sometimes necessary to use support equipment to aid in the weighing process. For example, to weigh a tail dragger airplane, it is necessary to raise the tail to get the airplane level. To level the airplane, a jack might be placed on the scale and used to raise the tail. Unfortunately, the scale is now absorbing the weight of the jack in addition to the weight of the airplane. This extra weight is known as tare weight and must be subtracted from the scale reading. Other examples of tare weight are wheel chocks placed on the scales and ground locks left in place on retractable landing gear.

## 2. 中文翻译

基准是一个假想的垂直平面，在飞机处于平飞姿态时，所有水平测量都以它为基准。若在一幅飞机图纸上观察基准，它会显示为一条与飞机纵轴垂直（90 度）的竖线。对于每一种飞机型号与型别，各部件的位置都以基准为参照标定。例如，油箱中的燃油可能位于基准之后 60 英寸（60"），驾驶舱里的一台电台可能位于基准之前 90"。

基准由制造商确定；它常常是机翼前缘，或者距某个易于识别位置的一段特定距离。基准的典型位置有机头、机翼前缘、直升机的旋翼轴，或距某个已知点的一段指定距离。不过，大多数现代直升机与飞机一样，把基准设在机头处，或设在机头之前的一段指定距离处。以该基准为准的距离以英寸计量，依设备相对基准的位置不同，可以是正值也可以是负值。

基准的位置载于 Aircraft Specifications 或型号合格证数据表（TCDS）中。1958 年之前合格审定的航空器归民用航空管理局管辖，其重量与平衡资料载于一份称为 Aircraft Specifications 的文件。1958 年以后合格审定的航空器归 FAA 管辖，其重量与平衡资料载于一份称为型号合格证数据表（TCDS）的文件。Aircraft Specifications 通常包含航空器设备清单；而对于有 TCDS 的航空器，设备清单是单独的一份文件。

力臂是从基准到飞机内任意一点的水平距离。力臂的距离一律以英寸计量，除恰好位于基准上的位置外，其前面要带正（+）或负（−）的代数符号。正号表示该部件位于基准之后，负号表示该部件位于基准之前。如果制造商把基准选在飞机最靠前的位置，那么所有力臂都是正数。基准若位于飞机上的其他任何位置，就会使一部分力臂为正数（位于基准之后）、一部分力臂为负数（位于基准之前）。对于以机翼前缘为基准的这种飞机，任何位于机翼前缘之前的部件（燃油、座椅、电台等）力臂为负，任何位于机翼前缘之后的部件力臂为正。如果某个部件恰好位于机翼前缘上，其力臂为零，在计算上无论把它取正还是取负都没有影响。

在航空器的 Aircraft Specifications、TCDS 或设备清单中，每个部件的力臂通常紧跟在部件名称或重量之后、写在括号里。例如在 TCDS 中，燃油量可能标为 98 加仑（gal）（+93.6），前行李限制标为 100 磅（lb）（–22.5）。这些数字表示燃油位于基准之后 93.6"，机头行李位于基准之前 22.6"。如果不知道某件设备的力臂，就必须准确测量它的位置。确定设备力臂时，测量从基准量到该设备自身的重心。

平衡点两侧的力矩之和必须相等。因此，同一个重量若距基准的距离（以英寸计）不同，其力矩大小也就不同。

位于距基准 80" 处的一台 5 磅电台，其力矩为 400 in-lb（5 lb × 80"）。位于距基准 12" 处的一台 10 磅电台，其力矩为 120 in-lb。力矩前面取正（+）号还是负（−）号，取决于它相对基准的位置。由于重量和力臂都是正值，该力矩最终为正数。

力矩的代数符号取决于基准的位置，以及重量是被装上还是被拆除，情况如下：

• 在基准之后增加重量，产生正力矩（+weight，+arm）。

• 在基准之前增加重量，产生负力矩（+weight，−arm）。

• 从基准之后拆除重量，产生负力矩（−weight，+arm）。

• 从基准之前拆除重量，产生正力矩（−weight，−arm）。

处理正数与负数时，要记住：同号相乘得正，异号相乘得负。

### 重心（CG）

重心是飞机全部重量集中并取得平衡的那一点；因此，可以在这个点（重心）把飞机支撑起来。机头重与机尾重两侧的力矩大小完全相等。它是飞机的平衡点，若把飞机悬吊在该点上，它不会有抬头或低头的转动趋势。

可以这样设想一根杠杆：其支点（称为 fulcrum）位于该杠杆的重心处。尽管支点两侧的重量并不相等，各重量到支点的距离也不相等，但重量与力臂的乘积（力矩）相等，正是这一点造成了平衡状态。因此，这根杠杆会保持平衡，就像两个体重不同、到支点距离也不同的人坐在跷跷板上那样。

最大重量是飞机及其装载获准的最大重量，载于 Aircraft Specifications 或 TCDS 中。对许多飞机而言，允许的最大重量会随其用途和飞行条件而变化。例如，某型飞机按正常类运行时允许的最大全重为 2,750 lb，但按允许有限特技动作的实用类运行时，同一架飞机的最大允许全重可能只有 2,175 lb。涉及最大重量这一概念时还有其他变化，如下：

• 最大滑行重量——飞机停在地面时可装载到的最重重量。有时也称为最大滑行重量（taxi weight）。

• 最大起飞重量——飞机开始起飞滑跑时可具有的最重重量。它与最大滑行重量之差，等于起飞前将要消耗掉的燃油重量。

• 最大着陆重量——飞机着陆时可具有的最重重量。对于大型宽体商用飞机，它可能比最大起飞重量小 100,000 lb，甚至更多。

• 最大无燃油重量——油箱内没有任何可用燃油时飞机可装载到的最重重量。超过这一数值装载的任何重量都必须以燃油的形式存在。

飞机的空重包括所有位置固定、实际安装在飞机上的运行设备。它包括机体、动力装置、必备设备、选装或专用设备、固定配重、液压油，以及残余燃油和滑油。残余燃油和滑油是通常排不出来、滞留在燃油管路、滑油管路和油箱中的液体，必须计入飞机空重。对于 1978 年以后合格审定的大多数飞机，发动机滑油系统的满容量也计入空重。关于飞机各系统中必须计入空重的残余液体、以及是否计入满油量的信息，会在 Aircraft Specifications 或 TCDS 中标明。

描述空重时还会用到其他术语：基本空重、许可空重和标准空重。“基本空重”用于该数值已计入发动机滑油系统满容量的情况。“许可空重”用于该数值只计入残余滑油重量的情况，因此一般只涉及 1978 年之前合格审定的飞机。标准空重是飞机制造商提供的数值，其中不含可能装到飞机上的任何选装设备。对于在航空维修领域工作的多数人来说，飞机的基本空重是最重要的一个。

### 空重重心（EWCG）

飞机的空重重心是其在空重状态下取得平衡的那一点。空重与重心的概念已在本章前面讨论过，现在把它们合并为一个概念。对飞机称重最重要的原因之一，就是确定其空重重心。所有其他的重量与平衡计算，包括为飞行装载飞机、进行设备变更计算以及进行不利状态检查，都以已知空重和空重重心为起点。这项关键信息是飞机重量与平衡报告所包含内容的一部分。

要确定飞机的有效载重，用最大允许全重减去空重。对于同时按正常类和实用类合格审定的飞机，其重量与平衡记录中可能列出两个有效载重。若正常类的最大重量列为 3,950 lb，则空重为 3,100 lb 的飞机可以有 850 lb 的有效载重。当该机按实用类运行时，最大全重可能降至 3,700 lb，有效载重也相应减为 600 lb。有些飞机无论按哪个类别审定，有效载重都相同。

有效载重包括燃油、不属于空重的其他液体、乘客、行李、驾驶员、副驾驶和机组人员。发动机滑油的重量是否算作有效载重的一部分，取决于飞机的审定年代，可通过查阅 Aircraft Specifications 或 TCDS 判定。飞机的业载与有效载重类似，只是不含燃油。

在可能的情况下，为保持在飞机所运行类别的最大允许重量之内，可能需要减少某项物品的重量。确定这些重量的分配情况，称为重量检查（weight check）。

许多现代飞机有多排座椅，而且往往不止一个行李舱。重量与平衡极端状态代表飞机重心最靠前和最靠后的位置。飞机在前后方向上都有固定的界限点，飞行中的任何时刻都不允许重心越过这些点。应当进行检查，以确认在增减机组、乘客、货物和消耗性重量时，重心不会移出限制范围。如果超出限制而飞机仍在此状态下飞行，可能导致稳定性不足，进而难以控制飞机。在任何改变重量与平衡的修理或改装之后，机体与动力装置（A&P）维修人员或修理人员必须确保，没有任何合法的装载状态会使重心移出其允许范围。为确定这一点，维修人员会刻意按使重心落在飞机限制范围之外的方式去计算飞机的装载。这称为不利装载检查（adverse-loading check）。

例如，在前不利装载重心检查中，位于前重心限制之前的所有有效载重都装上，而该限制之后的所有有效载重都空着。空着这一做法有一个例外，就是油箱。如果油箱位于前重心限制之后，就不能让它空着，因为没有燃油飞机无法飞行。这种情况下要计入一定数量的燃油，称为最小燃油。最小燃油是在巡航功率下飞行 30 分钟所需的油量。就重量与平衡而言，最小燃油不超过在额定最大连续功率下运行半小时所需的油量，即每 1 最大除起飞（METO）马力（hp）对应 1/12 加仑。由于航空汽油（Avgas）每加仑重 6 磅（lb/gal），将 METO 马力除以 2 即可得出最小燃油的磅数。例如，METO 功率为 200 hp 的飞机，其最小燃油为 16.65 加仑或 99.99 磅。还有一种更简单的算法，直接把 METO 马力除以 2，即 100 磅。确定最小燃油的这两种方法都有价值，结果基本相同。后一种算法的依据是：活塞发动机在巡航飞行中每马力每小时消耗 1 磅燃油，半小时即 1/2 磅，故将 METO 除以 2。例如，若对一架活塞发动机飞机进行前不利装载重心检查，发动机 METO 功率为 200 hp，则最小燃油为 100 lb（200 METO hp ÷ 2）。对于涡轮发动机飞机，最小燃油不按发动机马力计算。若要对涡轮发动机飞机做不利装载重心检查，需要由飞机制造商提供有关最小燃油的资料。

当飞机被放上秤台称重时，有时需要使用辅助设备来帮助完成称重。例如，给一架后三点式飞机称重时，必须抬起尾部使飞机水平。为使飞机水平，可以把一台千斤顶放在秤台上，用来抬起尾部。不幸的是，此时秤台除承受飞机重量之外，还在承受千斤顶的重量。这部分额外的重量称为皮重，必须从秤台读数中减去。皮重的其他例子有放在秤台上的轮挡，以及留在可收放起落架上的地面锁。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 条件从句 + would + 定语从句
**原句**：If the datum is viewed on a drawing of an aircraft, it would appear as a vertical line that is perpendicular (90 degrees) to the aircraft’s longitudinal axis.
**拆解**：if 引导条件状语从句，用被动语态 is viewed；主句为 it would appear as a vertical line，其中 it 指 the datum，as a vertical line 为介词短语作方式状语；that is perpendicular… 为定语从句修饰 a vertical line，perpendicular to 是固定搭配。
**要点**：主句用 would 表示「（若去看）就会出现」的设想结果，而非过去将来时；perpendicular 后接介词 to，不接 with；条件从句用一般现在时被动表条件，属真实条件句的常见组合。

### ② 过去分词后置定语 + 并列谓语
**原句**：Aircraft certified prior to 1958 fell under the Civil Aeronautics Administration and had their weight and balance information contained in a document known as Aircraft Specifications.
**拆解**：主语 aircraft，过去分词短语 certified prior to 1958 作后置定语（相当于 which were certified）；and 连接两个并列谓语 fell under… 与 had their weight and balance information contained in…；contained 为过去分词作宾语补足语（have sth. done 结构）；known as Aircraft Specifications 为过去分词短语作后置定语修饰 a document。
**要点**：过去分词后置定语表被动、已完成；have sth. done 意为「使某事被做／处于某状态」，此处不表「让别人做」；fall under 表示「归……管辖」，与 fall into（归入某类）不同。

### ③ 非限制性定语从句 + 同位语
**原句**：The weight is multiplied by the distance from the datum and the result is the moment, which is expressed in inch-pounds (in-lb), a point through which the force acts.
**拆解**：第一分句为被动语态 the weight is multiplied by the distance；and 连接第二分句 the result is the moment；which is expressed in inch-pounds 为非限制性定语从句，修饰 the moment；a point through which the force acts 为名词短语作 the moment 的同位语，其中 through which 引导定语从句修饰 a point。
**要点**：非限制性定语从句用逗号隔开，which 不能用 that 替换；expressed in 表示「以……为单位表示」；同位语可以只是一个名词短语，不必是完整句子，翻译时常用「即……」引出。

### ④ even though 让步 + what 引导的表语从句
**原句**：Even though the weights on either side of the fulcrum are not equal, and the distances from each weight to the fulcrum are not equal, the product of the weights and arms (moments) are equal, and that is what produces a balanced condition.
**拆解**：even though 引导让步状语从句，从句内部由两个并列分句构成（weights are not equal；distances are not equal）；主句 the product of the weights and arms are equal 与 that is what produces a balanced condition 由 and 并列；what produces a balanced condition 为 what 引导的表语从句，what 兼作从句主语。
**要点**：even though 表「尽管」，所述内容为事实，区别于 even if（即使，假设）；what 引导表语从句时不能换成 that（that 只起连接作用，需有先行词）；product of A and B 表示「A 与 B 的乘积」。

### ⑤ there be + depending on + 介词 which 定语从句
**原句**：For many aircraft, there are variations to the maximum allowable weight depending on the purpose and conditions under which the aircraft is to be flown.
**拆解**：for many aircraft 为状语；there are variations to the maximum allowable weight 为 there be 句型，to 短语作后置定语；depending on the purpose and conditions 为现在分词短语作状语；under which the aircraft is to be flown 为「介词 + which」引导的定语从句修饰 the purpose and conditions。
**要点**：be to do 表示「按计划、规定要做」，此处说明飞机「按规定将如何飞行」；under which 中 which 指代前面的 conditions，介词的选择由先行词与从句的搭配决定（under conditions）；depending on 的逻辑主语是前面的 variations，不是句子的读者。

### ⑥ whether 引导主语从句 + 并列谓语
**原句**：Whether the weight of engine oil is considered part of the useful load depends on when the aircraft was certificated and can be determined by looking at the Aircraft Specifications or TCDS.
**拆解**：whether 引导主语从句 Whether the weight of engine oil is considered part of the useful load；主句有两个并列谓语 depends on… 与 can be determined by…；depends on 后接 when 引导的宾语从句；by looking at… 为介词 + 动名词作方式状语。
**要点**：whether 引导主语从句时不能用 if；主语从句较长时谓语保持单数（depends／can be determined），中间不要因为插入的宾语从句而误判句子结构；be considered part of 表示「被视为……的一部分」，part of 前不加冠词。
