## 1. 原文

Matter can be defined as anything that has mass and volume and is the substance of which physical objects are composed. Essentially, it is anything that can be touched. Matter is what all things are made of; whatever occupies space, has mass, and is perceptible to the senses in some way. Weight is an indirect method of determining mass, but it is not the same. Weight is a measure of the pull of gravity acting on the mass of an object. The more mass an object has, the more it weighs under the earth’s force of gravity. Mathematically, weight can be stated as follows:

Weight = Mass × Gravity Categories of matter are ordered by molecular activity. The four categories or states are: solids, liquids, gases, and plasma. For the purposes of the aircraft technician, only solids, liquids, and gases are considered.

An element is a substance that cannot be reduced to a simpler form by chemical means. Iron, gold, silver, copper, and oxygen are examples of elements. Beyond this point of reduction, the element ceases to be what it is.

A compound is a chemical combination of two or more elements. Water is one of the most common compounds and is made up of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.

The smallest particle of matter that can exist and still retain its identity, such as water (H 2O), is called a molecule. Substances composed of only one type of atom are called elements. But most substances occur in nature as compounds, that is, combinations of two or more types of atoms. It would no longer retain the characteristics of water if it were compounded of one atom of hydrogen and two atoms of oxygen. If a drop of water is divided and then divided again and again until it cannot be divided any longer, it is still water.

Atom The atom is considered to be the most basic building block of all matter. Atoms are composed of three subatomic particles: protons, neutrons, and electrons. These three particles determine the properties of the specific atoms. Elements are substances composed of the same atoms with specific properties. Oxygen is an example of this. The main property that defines each element is the number of neutrons, protons, and electrons. Hydrogen and helium are examples of elements. Both of these elements have neutrons, protons, and electrons but differ in the number of those items. This difference alone accounts for the variations in chemical and physical properties of these two different elements. There are over 100 known elements in the periodic table. They are categorized according to their properties on that table. The kinetic theory of matter also states that the particles that make up the matter are always moving. Thermal expansion is considered in the kinetic theory and explains why matter contracts when it is cool and expands when it is hot, with the exception of water/ice.

### Electrons, Protons, & Neutrons

At the center of the atom is the nucleus, which contains protons and neutrons. Protons are positively-charged particles, and neutrons are neutrally-charged particles. A neutron has approximately the same mass as the proton. The third particle of the atom is the electron that is a negatively charged particle with a very small mass compared to the proton. The proton’s mass is approximately 1,837 times greater than the electron. Due to the proton and the neutron location in the central portion of the atom (nucleus) and the electron’s position at the distant periphery of the atom, it is the electron that undergoes the change during chemical reactions. Since a proton weighs approximately 1,845 times as much as an electron, the number of protons and neutrons in its nucleus determines the overall weight of an atom. The weight of an electron is not considered in determining the weight of an atom. Indeed, the nature of electricity cannot be defined clearly because it is not certain whether the electron is a negative charge with no mass (weight) or a particle of matter with a negative charge.

Hydrogen represents the simplest form of an atom. At the nucleus of the hydrogen atom is one proton and at the outer shell is one orbiting electron. At a more complex level is the oxygen atoms, which has eight electrons in two shells orbiting the nucleus with eight protons and eight neutrons. When the total positive charge of the protons in the nucleus equals the total negative charge of the electrons in orbit around the nucleus, the atom is said to have a neutral charge.

### Electron Shells & Energy Levels

Electrons require a certain amount of energy to stay in an orbit. This particular quantity is called the electron’s energy level. By its motion alone, the electron possesses kinetic energy, while the electron’s position in orbit determines its potential energy. The total energy of an electron is the main factor that determines the radius of the electron’s orbit.

Electrons of an atom appear only at certain definite energy levels (shells). The spacing between energy levels is such that when the chemical properties of the various elements are cataloged, it is convenient to group several closely spaced permissible energy levels together into electron shells. The maximum number of electrons that can be contained in any shell or sub-shell is the same for all atoms and is defined as electron capacity = 2n 2. In this equation, n represents the energy level in question. The first shell can only contain two electrons; the second shell can only contain eight electrons; the third, 18, and so on until we reach the seventh shell for the heaviest atoms, which have six energy levels. Because the innermost shell is the lowest energy level, the shell begins to fill up from the shell closest to the nucleus and fill outward as the atomic number of the element increases. However, an energy level does not need to be completely filled before electrons begin to fill the next level. The Periodic Table of Elements should be checked to determine an element’s electron configuration.

#### Valence Electrons

Valence is the number of chemical bonds an atom can form. Valence electrons are electrons that can participate in chemical bonds with other atoms. The number of electrons in the outermost shell of the atom is the determining factor in its valence. Therefore, the electrons contained in this shell are called valence electrons.

Ionization is the process by which an atom loses or gains electrons. Dislodging an electron from an atom causes the atom to become positively charged. This net positively charged atom is called a positive ion or a cation. An atom that has gained an extra number of electrons is negatively charged and is called a negative ion or an anion. When atoms are neutral, the positively-charged proton and the negatively charged electron are equal.

Valence electrons are found drifting midway between two nuclei. Some electrons are more tightly bound to the nucleus of their atom than others and are positioned in a shell or sphere closer to the nucleus, while others are more loosely bound and orbit at a greater distance from the nucleus. These outermost electrons are called “free” electrons because they can be easily dislodged from the positive attraction of the protons in the nucleus. Once freed from the atom, the electron can then travel from atom to atom, becoming the flow of electrons commonly called current in a practical electrical circuit.

#### Conductors, Insulators, and Semiconductors

The valence of an atom determines its ability to gain or lose an electron, which ultimately determines the chemical and electrical properties of the atom. These properties can be categorized as being a conductor, semiconductor, or insulator, depending on the ability of the material to produce free electrons. When a material has a large number of free Electron Nucleus (1 Proton)

Elements such as gold, copper, and silver possess many free electrons and make good conductors. The atoms in these materials have a few loosely bound electrons in their outer orbits. Energy in the form of heat can cause these electrons in the outer orbit to break loose and drift throughout the material. Copper and silver have one electron in their outer orbits. At room temperature, a piece of silver wire has billions of free electrons.

Insulators are materials that do not conduct electrical current very well or not at all, such as glass, ceramic, and plastic.

Under normal conditions, atoms in these materials do not produce free electrons. The absence of free electrons means that electrical current cannot be conducted through the material. Only when the material is in an extremely strong electrical field will the outer electrons be dislodged. This action is called breakdown and usually causes physical damage to the insulator.

The material properties of semiconductors fall in between conductors and insulators. In their pure state, they are not good at conducting or insulating. Semiconductors can operate like a conductor or insulator depending on what external load is placed on the material. Semiconductors are used to make transistors and integrated circuits. Silicon and germanium are the most widely used semiconductor materials. For a more detailed explanation on this topic, refer to page 12-98 in this chapter.

## 2. 中文翻译

物质可以定义为具有质量和体积、并且是构成物体的那种东西。从本质上说，凡是能触摸到的东西都是物质。物质是一切物体由之构成的东西；凡是占据空间、具有质量、能以某种方式被感官察觉的，都是物质。重量是测定质量的间接方法，但重量与质量并不是一回事。重量是重力作用在物体质量上的拉力的量度。物体的质量越大，在地球重力作用下就越重。用数学方式，重量可表示如下：

Weight = Mass × Gravity 物质的类别按分子活动的程度来划分。共有四个类别或四种状态：固体、液体、气体和等离子体。就飞机技术员的工作而言，只考虑固体、液体和气体。

元素是无法用化学方法再分解成更简单形态的物质。铁、金、银、铜和氧都是元素的例子。超过这一分解限度，元素就不再是它本身了。

化合物是两种或两种以上元素的化学结合。水是最常见的化合物之一，由两个氢原子和一个氧原子构成。

能存在并保持其特性的最小物质粒子称为分子，例如水（H 2O）。只由一种原子构成的物质称为元素。但自然界中的大多数物质以化合物形式存在，也就是两种或两种以上类型的原子组成。如果水由一个氢原子和两个氧原子化合而成，就不再具有水的特性。把一滴水一再分割，直到再也分不下去，它仍然是水。

原子 原子被认为是所有物质最基本的构件。原子由三种亚原子粒子构成：质子、中子和电子。这三种粒子决定特定原子的性质。元素就是由具有特定性质的同种原子构成的物质，氧就是一个例子。界定每种元素的主要性质是中子、质子和电子的数目。氢和氦都是元素的例子。这两种元素都有中子、质子和电子，但数目不同。仅这一差别就造成了这两种不同元素在化学和物理性质上的差异。周期表中已知元素有一百多种。人们按周期表上的性质对它们分类。物质分子运动论还指出，构成物质的粒子始终在运动。热膨胀也属于分子运动论的范畴，它解释了物质冷却时收缩、受热时膨胀的原因，水/冰是例外。

### 电子、质子与中子

原子的中心是原子核，其中含有质子和中子。质子是带正电的粒子，中子是不带电的粒子。中子的质量与质子大致相同。原子的第三种粒子是电子，它是带负电的粒子，质量比质子小得多。质子的质量约为电子的 1,837 倍。由于质子和中子位于原子的中心部分（原子核），而电子位于远离中心的原子外围，因此在化学反应中发生变化的正是电子。由于质子的质量约为电子的 1,845 倍，所以原子核中质子和中子的数目决定原子的总重量。在确定原子重量时不计电子的重量。确实，电的本质无法被清楚地定义，因为无法确定电子究竟是没有质量（重量）的负电荷，还是带负电的物质粒子。

氢代表最简单的原子形式。氢原子核中有一个质子，外层有一个绕核运动的电子。氧原子较为复杂：它有八个电子分两层绕核运动，核中有八个质子和八个中子。当原子核中质子的正电荷总量等于绕核运动的电子的负电荷总量时，就说该原子呈中性。

### 电子壳层与能级

电子要停留在某个轨道上需要一定的能量。这个特定的量称为电子的能级。仅凭运动，电子就具有动能，而电子在轨道上的位置决定其势能。电子的总能是决定其轨道半径的主要因素。

原子中的电子只出现在某些确定的能级（壳层）上。能级之间的间隔使得人们在把各种元素的化学性质编成表时，可以把若干彼此靠近的允许能级归并成电子壳层，以便处理。任一壳层或亚壳层能容纳的最大电子数对所有原子都相同，定义为电子容量 = 2n 2。式中 n 表示所讨论的能级。第一层只能容纳两个电子；第二层只能容纳八个；第三层十八个，依此类推，直到最重原子的第七层——这些原子有六个能级。由于最内层是能量最低的能级，壳层从最靠近原子核的一层开始填充，并随着元素原子序数增大而向外填充。不过，一个能级不必填满，电子就可以开始填充下一个能级。要确定某元素的电子排布，应查阅元素周期表。

#### 价电子

价是原子能形成的化学键数目。价电子是能与其他原子形成化学键的电子。原子最外层中的电子数是决定其价的因素。因此，这一壳层中所含的电子称为价电子。

电离是原子失去或获得电子的过程。使电子脱离原子会使原子带正电。这种带净正电荷的原子称为正离子或阳离子。获得多余电子的原子带负电，称为负离子或阴离子。原子呈中性时，带正电的质子与带负电的电子数量相等。

价电子在两个原子核之间的中途游荡。有些电子与原子的核结合得比其他电子更紧，因而位于离核较近的壳层或球壳上；另一些电子结合得较松，在离核较远处绕核运动。这些最外层电子称为「自由」电子，因为它们很容易从原子核中质子的正吸引下脱离出来。电子一旦脱离原子，就能从一个原子行进到另一个原子，形成电子流，实际电路中通常称之为电流。

### 电子运动

#### 导体、绝缘体与半导体

原子的价决定其获得或失去电子的能力，并最终决定该原子的化学性质和电学性质。按材料产生自由电子的能力，这些性质可分为导体、半导体或绝缘体。当一种材料中存在大量自由电子时，

该材料就能传导更大的电流。

金、铜、银等元素具有许多自由电子，是良导体。这些材料的原子在外层轨道上有少量结合松散的电子。以热形式出现的能量可使外层轨道上的这些电子挣脱，并在材料中漂移。铜和银的外层轨道上各有一个电子。在室温下，一段银导线拥有数十亿个自由电子。

绝缘体是导电性能很差或完全不导电的材料，例如玻璃、陶瓷和塑料。

在正常情况下，这些材料中的原子不产生自由电子。没有自由电子就意味着电流不能通过该材料传导。只有当材料处于极强的电场中时，外层电子才会被剥离。这一作用称为击穿，通常会对绝缘体造成物理损伤。

半导体的材料性质介于导体与绝缘体之间。在纯净状态下，它们既不善导电也不善绝缘。半导体可以像导体那样工作，也可以像绝缘体那样工作，取决于加在材料上的外部负载。半导体用于制造晶体管和集成电路。硅和锗是应用最广的半导体材料。有关这一课题的更详细说明，参见本章第 12-98 页。

## 4. 语法解析

### ① 被动语态 + of which 定语从句
**原句**：Matter can be defined as anything that has mass and volume and is the substance of which physical objects are composed.
**拆解**：主句 Matter can be defined as anything 为情态被动；that 引导定语从句修饰 anything，从句内 has 与 is 并列；of which physical objects are composed 为介词 of 提到关系词前的定语从句，正常语序是 physical objects are composed of which。
**要点**：be defined as 是「被定义为」的固定被动；of which 结构书面色彩强，多见于定义、规范文字，翻译时宜还原成「由……构成」而不是直译「的……」。注意 that 从句里第二个谓语 is 前省略了 that，不要漏看并列关系。

### ② the + 比较级，the + 比较级
**原句**：The more mass an object has, the more it weighs under the earth’s force of gravity.
**拆解**：前一分句 The more mass an object has 表条件（宾语 the more mass 因强调前置），后一分句 the more it weighs 表结果，under… 为状语。
**要点**：这是「越……越……」的固定句式，两个 the 都不能省，且必须放在比较级之前；后一分句常把宾语提前，语序与陈述句不同。技术文本里用它表达正比关系，比 if…then 更简练。

### ③ 介词短语前置引起的倒装
**原句**：At the center of the atom is the nucleus, which contains protons and neutrons.
**拆解**：地点状语 At the center of the atom 提到句首，谓语 is 之后才是真正的主语 the nucleus，构成完全倒装；which 引导非限制性定语从句补充说明 nucleus。
**要点**：英语里地点状语前置、谓语为 be 或 come、lie 等时常用完全倒装，目的是让新信息（the nucleus）落在句末；识别办法是看谓语后面的名词才是主语，不要误把 the atom 当主语。

### ④ 强调句型 It is … that …
**原句**：Due to the proton and the neutron location in the central portion of the atom (nucleus) and the electron’s position at the distant periphery of the atom, it is the electron that undergoes the change during chemical reactions.
**拆解**：句首 Due to… 为原因状语；主句是强调句型 it is the electron that undergoes…，被强调的成分是主语 the electron，that 引导其余部分，谓语 undergoes 用第三人称单数与原主语一致。
**要点**：强调句 it is/was + 被强调成分 + that/who…，去掉 it is 和 that 后句子仍完整，这是与形式主语 it 的判别方法；本句强调「发生变化的正是电子」，汉语用「正是……」来对应。

### ⑤ 形式主语 it + whether…or 主语从句
**原句**：Indeed, the nature of electricity cannot be defined clearly because it is not certain whether the electron is a negative charge with no mass (weight) or a particle of matter with a negative charge.
**拆解**：主句为情态被动 cannot be defined；because 引导原因状语从句，从句中 it 为形式主语，真正主语是 whether…or… 引导的主语从句（whether the electron is A or B）。
**要点**：whether…or… 作主语时不能换成 if；it is not certain 是「尚不能确定」的固定说法，比 we are not certain 更客观，适合技术论述。翻译时把真正主语提前：「电子究竟是……还是……，尚不能确定」。

### ⑥ once + 过去分词作状语 + 现在分词作结果状语
**原句**：Once freed from the atom, the electron can then travel from atom to atom, becoming the flow of electrons commonly called current in a practical electrical circuit.
**拆解**：Once freed from the atom 为 once + 过去分词短语作时间状语（省略 it is）；主句 the electron can then travel…；逗号后 becoming… 为现在分词短语作结果状语，其中 commonly called current… 为过去分词短语作后置定语修饰 electrons。
**要点**：once 后接过去分词表示「一旦被……」，其逻辑主语必须与主句主语一致（the electron）；becoming 的逻辑主语也是 the electron，表示自然结果。commonly called 是「被称为」的压缩表达，相当于 which is commonly called。
